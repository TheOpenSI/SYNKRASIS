Case 072
Case Classification
Primary phenotype: Central vision loss phenotype
Secondary phenotype: None
Primary diagnosis: Advanced dry AMD with geographic atrophy OU
Modifiers: pseudophakia; single fall
Demographics
Age: 82
Gender: Female
Care setting: community optometry practice following GP referral
Falls pathway / referral context: referred after increasing trouble reading medication labels and one recent stumble in the kitchen; GP requested retinal assessment.
History
Chief complaint: “The middle is patchy in my better eye now.”
Ocular history: Gradual worsening of central vision over 18 months, now affecting reading, face recognition, and meal preparation. She recently stumbled in the kitchen after not clearly noticing a dark saucepan on a patterned floor mat directly ahead. She has not had recurrent falls. Uses bifocals and a hand magnifier for fine print. No sudden distortion or flashes. Previously told she had dry macular degeneration and has not attended retinal review in over a year. Lives independently with occasional family support.
General medical history (including relevant medications): Hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, and osteoporosis; on antihypertensive therapy and statin.
Driving status: No longer drives.

Clinical Impression
Advanced dry AMD with bilateral geographic atrophy causing a central vision loss phenotype. Reduced distance and near acuity and contrast sensitivity explain difficulties with medication labels, food preparation, and direct-ahead object identification.
Management Plan
Routine retinal ophthalmology review for ongoing AMD monitoring.
Low vision referral for magnification, task lighting, and contrast enhancement strategies.
Advised brighter kitchen lighting and improved contrast for commonly used household objects.
Recommended extra care with patterned surfaces and low-contrast items directly ahead.
Review in 4 months, or urgently if new distortion or sudden change develops.
