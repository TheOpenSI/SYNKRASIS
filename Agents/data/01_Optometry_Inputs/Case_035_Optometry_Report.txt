Case 035
Case Classification
Primary phenotype: Peripheral field / spatial awareness loss phenotype
Secondary phenotype: None
Primary diagnosis: Primary open-angle glaucoma OU
Modifiers: glaucoma suspect history progressed to established disease
Demographics
Age: 67
Gender: Male
Care setting: shared-care glaucoma review in community optometry
Falls pathway / referral context: routine review where worsening obstacle awareness was identified; no confirmed falls, but patient reports several near-misses in dim hallways.
History
Chief complaint: “I’m not seeing things at floor level until late.”
Ocular history: Treated glaucoma for 4 years with latanoprost (1 drop OU nocte), generally compliant, under care of ophthalmology. Over the past 9 months he has become less confident stepping around low furniture and says darker floor-level hazards are harder to notice. Uses single-vision distance spectacles for walking and readers for near. No central blur, diplopia, or sudden change. He remains active and attends the gym regularly. Ophthalmology follow-up is due in the next few months.
General medical history (including relevant medications): Hypertension and obstructive sleep apnoea.
Driving status: Drives daytime and occasional dusk trips.

Clinical Impression
Moderate glaucoma OU causes a peripheral field / spatial awareness loss phenotype.
Glaucoma progression from last eye exam.
Automated visual field showed superior nasal arcuate loss OS and superior defect OD, with corresponding structure-function correlation (neuroretinal rim, nerve fibre layer, OCT RNFL/GCA).  
Functional side-awareness complaints are consistent with glaucoma progression despite good central acuity.
Management Plan
Discussion on trialing a combination IOP lowering agent, or glaucoma surgery (MIGS with early cataract surgery).
Refer to ophthalmology to update glaucoma management (goal: slow down progression). If new topical IOP lowering agent is prescribed, review in 6 weeks to check compliance, target pressure, side effects/adverse events. Otherwise, review structural and functional testing (VF, OCT, optic nerve assessment) in 6 months to check progression (collaborative care arrangement with ophthalmology)
Advised improved low-level lighting and removal of floor clutter at home. Discussed extra caution in dim corridors and when stepping around low obstacles.
Referral to Orientation & Mobility/Occupational therapy.
