Case Classification

Secondary phenotype: None
Primary diagnosis: Right homonymous hemianopia and bilateral cataract
Modifiers: stroke history; hearing impairment; recurrent falls history; multifocal / progressive use outdoors
Demographics
Age: 84
Gender: Male
Care setting: community optometry practice following rehabilitation team recommendation
Falls pathway / referral context: referred after recurrent falls following stroke rehabilitation; family reports missing walls and objects on the right side and worsening glare/blur.
History
Chief complaint: “I don’t see well on my right, and the whole scene looks hazy.”
Ocular history: Right homonymous hemianopia following a previous stroke approximately 2 years ago, with added gradual bilateral blur and glare over the last year. He has had two confirmed falls in the past 4 months, including one while turning toward a doorway and another when stepping from bright sunlight into a shaded entry. Uses bifocals full-time. No diplopia or acute visual change. He lives with his wife and receives rehabilitation follow-up for mobility and home safety.
General medical history (including relevant medications): Stroke history, hypertension, arthritis, and hearing impairment; taking antihypertensive and antiplatelet medication.
Driving status: Not driving.

Clinical Impression
Mixed falls-relevant visual impairment phenotype due to stroke-related right homonymous hemianopia and visually significant bilateral cataract. Both the right-sided field loss and reduced clarity/contrast are materially contributing to recurrent falls and poor mobility confidence.
Management Plan
Routine cataract referral, with note that surgery may improve clarity but not the hemianopic field loss.
Continue rehabilitation-based scanning strategies and communicate findings to OT, orientation and mobility, GP, and rehabilitation physician. Trial Pelli Prisms to expand visual field on the right side.
Advised brighter entryway lighting, high-contrast step edges, and avoidance of bifocals on stairs if possible.
Reinforced need for a support guide or added caution in visually complex environments.
Referral to Vision Australia/Guide Dogs Australia for low vision aids including mobility cane. 
Review in 3–4 months, or sooner if rapid visual decline occurs.
