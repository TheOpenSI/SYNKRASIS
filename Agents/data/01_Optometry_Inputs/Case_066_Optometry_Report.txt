Case 066
Case Classification
Primary phenotype: Fluctuating / variable visual function phenotype
Secondary phenotype: None
Primary diagnosis: Medication-related ocular surface disease
Modifiers: glaucoma; polypharmacy
Demographics
Age: 71
Gender: Female
Care setting: community optometry practice
Falls pathway / referral context: routine examination where variable visual clarity and evening mobility hesitation were identified during history.
History
Chief complaint: “By evening, the signs and steps lose clarity unless I blink a lot.”
Ocular history: Long-standing glaucoma treated with multiple drops, now with six months of fluctuating blur, stinging, and watering that worsen through the day. She reports uncertainty on kerbs and outdoor steps late in the afternoon but no confirmed fall. Uses single-vision distance spectacles for walking and readers for near. No diplopia or field-type symptoms beyond known stable glaucoma. She remains active and walks daily.
General medical history (including relevant medications): Hypertension and glaucoma on multi-drop therapy.
Driving status: Drives daytime only; avoids late-day driving if the eyes feel irritated.

Clinical Impression
ocular surface disease causing a fluctuating / variable visual function phenotype. Aetiology is likely medication related (preservative hypersensitivity) and some meibomian gland dysfunction. Vision is relatively preserved, but tear film instability from chronic topical therapy is the main driver of late-day blur and reduces kerb confidence.
Management Plan
Review glaucoma drops timing and tolerance. Discuss preservative-free formulations, including potential cost implications. 
Write a letter to the ophthalmologist regarding the above (ophthalmologist to advise whether to initiate medication change or whether appointment and ophthalmology review is required first).
Discussed using lubricants before outdoor walking or late-day visually demanding tasks.
Advised extra caution on steps and kerbs when symptoms are active.
Review in 6 weeks to reassess ocular surface status, compliance, IOP, and functional impact.
