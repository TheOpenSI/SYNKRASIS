Case 067
Case Classification
Primary phenotype: Blur / reduced visual clarity (optical or media-related) phenotype
Secondary phenotype: None
Primary diagnosis: Dense bilateral cataract
Modifiers: single fall; multifocal / progressive use outdoors
Demographics
Age: 79
Gender: Male
Care setting: community optometry practice following GP referral
Falls pathway / referral context: referred after a recent fall on a poorly lit outdoor step; patient reports severe blur and glare in both eyes.
History
Chief complaint: “I can’t tell where the step edge is unless the light is strong.”
Ocular history: Progressive bilateral blur and severe glare over the last year, with rapid worsening in the past 3 months. He fell once stepping off a poorly lit patio edge and has since become reluctant to go outside at dusk. Uses old bifocal spectacles full-time. No diplopia or flashes. No previous ocular surgery. He lives with his wife and now relies on her for transport and reading small print.
General medical history (including relevant medications): Hypertension and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease; on inhalers (steroidal) and antihypertensive therapy.
Driving status: Stopped driving 1 month ago because of poor vision and glare.

Distance VA (presenting, habitual): OD 6/120 | OS 6/96 | OU 6/96 @6 m with bifocals
Distance VA (best corrected): OD 6/60 | OS 6/60 | OU 6/60 @6 m
Near VA (habitual): N48 OU @40 cm
Near VA (with add, best corrected): N32 OU @40 cm
Contrast sensitivity (Pelli-Robson, habitual): Not testable due to reduced acuity
Visual field (confrontation): full to confrontation OU
Binocular alignment (cover test, habitual): orthophoria distance | 4 XP near
Stereo (best corrected): Not testable due to reduced acuity
Motility: Full OU, no diplopia
Pupils: Equal and reactive, no RAPD
Colour vision (best corrected): Ishihara screening: Not testable due to reduced acuity
IOP: OD 16 mmHg | OS 15 mmHg @ 11 am (Goldmann tonometry)
Ocular Health Findings
Anterior segment:
Mild dermatochalasis OU
Cornea clear, conjunctiva quiet OU
Lens:
Dense nuclear sclerosis with posterior subcapsular cataract OU
Posterior segment:
Optic nerve: Dilated fundus examination; view limited by media opacity, discs seen sufficiently to note C/D about 0.35 OU without obvious neuroretinal rim abnormality
Macula: Reduced view due to cataract; no obvious macular haemorrhage or gross lesion seen OU
Vessels: Reduced view due to cataract OU 
Periphery: Limited peripheral view due to cataract; no obvious retinal break seen on available view OU
Clinical Impression
Dense bilateral cataract causing a blur / reduced visual clarity phenotype with severe acuity loss, glare, and markedly reduced contrast sensitivity. Poor step-edge visibility in low illumination is the most likely visual contributor to the recent fall.
Management Plan
Routine cataract referral with priority noted because of severe functional limitation and recent fall.
Explained to patient that updated spectacles will not meaningfully restore vision.
Advised on home modifications such as path and entry lighting and high-contrast step edging; and avoidance of bifocals on stairs where possible.
Continue not driving until vision is improved (post cataract removal/IOL surgery) provided that vision will meet legal criteria for fitness to drive 
Review time frame is based on outcome from ophthalmology appointment; or sooner if patient observes a change to their vision
