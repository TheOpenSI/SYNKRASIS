Case 033
Case Classification
Primary phenotype: Blur / reduced visual clarity (optical or media-related) phenotype
Secondary phenotype: None
Primary diagnosis: Refractive error / under-correction
Modifiers: no correction when mobile
Demographics
Age: 63
Gender: Female
Care setting: community optometry practice
Falls pathway / referral context: routine eye examination where mobility-related blur was identified; no confirmed fall, but patient reports hesitancy on uneven paving when walking without glasses.
History
Chief complaint: “I take my glasses off to read and then forget to put them back on.”
Ocular history: 
Last eye exam: 3 years ago. History of low myopia. Gradual distance blurs over 18 months with habitual removal of spectacles for near tasks, then continuing to walk around unaided. She reports misjudging garden path edges and needing to slow down outdoors, especially in dull weather. Uses single-vision distance spectacles only and no dedicated reading correction. No previous ocular surgery, no diplopia, and no field-type symptoms. She works part-time and walks daily for exercise.
General medical history (including relevant medications): Hypertension that is under control via medication. No diabetes reported.
Driving status: Drives regularly; avoids driving without glasses. License states that glasses must be worn.

Clinical Impression
Undercorrected myopic refractive error with no correction when mobile, causing a blur / reduced visual clarity phenotype. Visual function normalises with appropriate correction, and the main falls-relevant issue is behavioural spectacle use rather than ocular pathology.
Management Plan
Updated distance and near prescriptions provided, with suggestions for first time multifocals with advice on adaptation process and fall risk. Advised wearing distance correction consistently when walking indoors and outdoors.
Advised distance vision correction is required for driving licence. 
Review in 6 weeks to check adaptation and mobility confidence.
Return sooner if blur persists or a fall occurs.
