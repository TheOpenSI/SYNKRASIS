Case 046
Case Classification
Primary phenotype: Central vision loss phenotype
Secondary phenotype: None
Primary diagnosis: Branch retinal vein occlusion with macular oedema OS
Modifiers: cardiovascular disease; single fall
Demographics
Age: 74
Gender: Male
Care setting: community optometry practice following GP referral
Falls pathway / referral context: referred after new reading difficulty and a recent stumble on a low kerb; GP requested assessment for possible retinal cause.
History
Chief complaint: “The left eye is blurred and patchy in the middle.”
Ocular history: Gradual reduction in central clarity OS over 2 months, with trouble reading small print and identifying price labels. He stumbled once off a low kerb while distracted by blurred vision but denies recurrent falls. Uses separate distance and near spectacles. No flashes, floaters, or diplopia. He has a history of hypertension and says vision in the right eye remains relatively clear. No previous retinal treatment.
General medical history (including relevant medications): Hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, and atrial fibrillation; on antihypertensive therapy and anticoagulation medications.
Driving status: Drives locally but has reduced driving since the left eye worsened.

Distance VA (presenting, habitual): OD 6/12 | OS 6/30 | OU 6/15 @6 m with distance spectacles
Distance VA (best corrected): OD 6/9 | OS 6/24 | OU 6/12 @6 m
Near VA (habitual): N14 OU @40 cm
Near VA (with add, best corrected): N10 OU @40 cm
Contrast sensitivity (Pelli-Robson, habitual): 1.20 log units @1 m — Reduced
Visual field (confrontation): full peripheral fields OU; central fixation less clear OS
Binocular alignment (cover test, habitual): orthophoria distance | 4 XP near
Stereo (best corrected): 80”, limited by central vision loss
Motility: Full OU, no diplopia

Colour vision (best corrected): Ishihara screening: Borderline / inconclusive — OS 8/14; interpreted with caution in macular disease

Anterior segment:
Mild blepharitis OU
Corneas clear, conjunctiva quiet
Lens:
Mild nuclear sclerosis OU
Posterior segment:
Optic nerve: Dilated fundus examination; C/D 0.3 OU, rims healthy
Macula: OS branch retinal vein occlusion involving the superior temporal arcade with macular oedema, intraretinal haemorrhages, and hard exudates. OD macula unremarkable. OCT macula OS shows intraretinal fluid with central thickening
Vessels: OS venous tortuosity in the affected quadrant; mild AV nicking OU
Periphery: Dilated examination otherwise unremarkable OU
Clinical Impression
Superior branch retinal vein occlusion with macular oedema OS causing a central vision loss phenotype. Reduced central acuity, near function, and contrast sensitivity explain the recent kerb misjudgement and reading difficulty.
Management Plan
Ophthalmology retinal referral within 4 weeks for centre-involving macular oedema assessment and treatment.
Communication to GP regarding retinal vascular event and cardiovascular risk review. Work up including blood pressure, hbA1c, fasting blood glucose, serum total cholesterol, LDL-C, HDL-C, and triglycerides.
Advised caution on steps and kerbs until vision stabilises.
Discussed avoiding driving if the patient feels vision is not adequate for sign recognition or safe hazard detection.
Review in optometry based on collaborative care arrangement with ophthalmologist, or sooner if new visual symptoms occur.
