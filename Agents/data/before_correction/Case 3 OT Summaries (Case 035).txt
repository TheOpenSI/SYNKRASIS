==============================
Case 035
==============================
1. Patient Context
    • Age: 67
    • Setting: Shared-care glaucoma review
    • Referral context: Near-misses in dim hallways
    • Key diagnosis: Moderate glaucoma causing reduced peripheral/spatial awareness
2. Key Vision Issues
    • Peripheral field loss may reduce detection of floor-level hazards.
    • Reduced contrast may make darker objects harder to notice in dim areas.
    • Good central clarity may mask mobility risk during functional assessment.
3. Functional Impact
    • Mobility/Falls: May miss low furniture, hallway objects, and hazards outside central gaze.
    • ADLs/IADLs: Household movement while carrying items may be riskier if obstacles are not detected early.
    • Participation: Dim or crowded environments may reduce confidence.
    • Environment: Clutter and poor floor contrast are likely high-risk.
4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations
Environmental Modifications
    • Remove low stools, shoes, and cords from hallway walking routes so reduced peripheral awareness is less likely to result in late obstacle detection.
Lighting and Contrast
    • Add plug-in night lights along bedroom-to-bathroom routes and hallway corners to improve detection of low-level hazards.
Mobility Strategies
    • Train a “scan floor zone → check side space → proceed” routine before entering dim corridors or stepping around furniture.
5. Falls Risk Level
    • Moderate vision-related falls risk — peripheral field loss and reduced contrast are associated with near-misses in dim indoor mobility.
6. Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings
    • Central acuity alone is unlikely to reflect functional mobility safety.
    • Assess obstacle detection in dim, cluttered, and visually complex spaces.
