==============================
Case 032 
==============================
1. Patient Context
    • Age: 80
    • Setting: Community optometry following GP referral
    • Referral context: Recurrent outdoor falls with intermittent double vision and kerb-height difficulty
    • Key diagnosis: Recent left sixth nerve palsy causing distance double vision and poor depth judgement
2. Key Vision Issues
    • Distance double vision may make kerbs, car parks, and uneven footpaths difficult to judge.
    • Loss of functional depth perception is likely to affect step-down accuracy.
    • Bifocal use outdoors may further increase uncertainty when looking down at ground level.
3. Functional Impact
    • Mobility/Falls: May be unsafe crossing car parks, stepping off kerbs, or walking alone outdoors.
    • ADLs/IADLs: Outdoor errands may require planning or support while diplopia persists.
    • Participation/Confidence: Reduced independent walking outside may reflect safety concerns.
    • Environment: Uneven paving and outdoor level changes are priority risks.
4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations
Mobility Strategies
    • Consider training: stop at kerb → close/align vision as advised by optometry strategy → identify kerb edge → step down with support if needed, supporting safe kerb negotiation.
Education and Support
    • Wife/support person may assist with outdoor route choice and car-park crossing until diplopia stabilises.
Clinical Follow-up / Communication
    • OT may assess how current eyewear affects outdoor mobility and liaise with optometry if falls risk remains high.
5. Falls Risk Level
    • High vision-related falls risk — recurrent falls are associated with diplopia, poor depth judgement, and outdoor level-change difficulty.
6. Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings
    • Prioritise outdoor mobility safety and supervision needs.
    • Integrate vision findings with balance, footwear, cognition, and environmental assessment.
