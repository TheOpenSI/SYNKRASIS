==============================
Case 057
==============================
1. Patient Context
    • Age: 84
    • Setting: Community optometry after rehabilitation recommendation
    • Referral context: Recurrent post-stroke falls
    • Key diagnosis: Right-sided visual field loss with bilateral cataract-related blur/glare
2. Key Vision Issues
    • Right homonymous field loss may cause missed obstacles and doorway edges on the right.
    • Reduced clarity and contrast may worsen step detection and glare transitions.
    • Bifocal use outdoors may further complicate stair and level-change judgement.
3. Functional Impact
    • Mobility/Falls: High risk during turning, doorways, bright-to-shade transitions, and complex environments.
    • ADLs/IADLs: Moving between tasks and rooms may require extra cueing or support.
    • Participation: Community access may need guided support to preserve safe participation.
    • Environment: Entryways, stairs, and high-glare areas are priority risks.
4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations
Mobility Strategies
    • Practise: stop before doorway → turn head/eyes to right boundary → identify frame and floor edge → step through using a wide turn rather than pivot.
Lighting and Contrast
    • Fit high-contrast strips to shaded entry step edges and add bright sensor lighting at the front entry to support transition safety.
Education and Support
    • Consider wife/support guide training for right-side cueing in unfamiliar or busy environments.
5. Falls Risk Level
    • High vision-related falls risk — recurrent falls reflect interacting field loss, reduced contrast, glare, and mobility complexity.
6. Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings
    • Independent mobility may not be safe in complex or changing light conditions.
    • Integrate visual findings with rehabilitation mobility, hearing, cognition, and home-safety assessment.
