==============================
Case 066
==============================
1. Patient Context
    • Age: 71
    • Setting: Community optometry
    • Referral context: Variable clarity and late-day mobility hesitation
    • Key diagnosis: Medication-related ocular surface disease causing fluctuating vision
2. Key Vision Issues
    • Vision may worsen later in the day due to ocular surface instability.
    • Kerbs and outdoor steps may be harder to judge when blur and watering are active.
    • Symptoms appear variable, so performance may differ across the day.
3. Functional Impact
    • Mobility/Falls: Late-afternoon outdoor walking may carry higher risk.
    • ADLs/IADLs: Reading signs, labels, or instructions may fluctuate with symptoms.
    • Participation: May choose daytime activities when vision feels clearer.
    • Environment: Outdoor steps and kerbs are priority risk points during symptomatic periods.
4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations
Task Adaptations
    • Schedule shopping, outdoor walking, and unfamiliar community access earlier in the day where possible, supporting participation when vision is most stable.
Mobility Strategies
    • Before kerbs: pause → blink/clear vision → locate kerb edge → step down only once the edge is clear.
Education and Support
    • Consider a simple symptom-and-activity diary to help OT identify when mobility or reading tasks are safest.
5. Falls Risk Level
    • Moderate vision-related falls risk — fluctuating late-day blur affects kerb confidence without confirmed falls.
6. Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings
    • Assess function at different times of day if symptoms vary.
    • Focus on timing, routines, and safe participation rather than restricting activity.
