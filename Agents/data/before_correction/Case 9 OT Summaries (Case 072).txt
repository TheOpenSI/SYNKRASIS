==============================
Case 072
==============================
1. Patient Context
    • Age: 82
    • Setting: Community optometry following GP referral
    • Referral context: Medication-label difficulty and one kitchen stumble
    • Key diagnosis: Advanced dry macular degeneration with central vision loss in both eyes
2. Key Vision Issues
    • Patchy central vision may affect reading medication labels, food preparation, and face recognition.
    • Reduced central detail may make directly ahead objects harder to identify.
    • Patterned flooring may reduce object visibility during kitchen tasks.
3. Functional Impact
    • Mobility/Falls: May miss low-contrast objects directly ahead, particularly in the kitchen.
    • ADLs/IADLs: Medication management and meal preparation may be slower or less reliable.
    • Participation/Confidence: Independent living may require task-specific supports.
    • Environment: Patterned mats and dark kitchen items are priority visual risks.
4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations
Task Adaptations
    • For medication management, consider large-print labels, fixed storage locations, and a check-back routine to support accurate identification.
Lighting and Contrast
    • Place a plain light-coloured mat or remove the patterned kitchen mat where saucepans are placed so dark items stand out.
Education and Support
    • Low vision input is already planned; OT may focus on interim kitchen and medication routines.
5. Falls Risk Level
    • Moderate vision-related falls risk — central vision loss has affected kitchen object detection with one reported stumble.
6. Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings
    • Prioritise medication safety and meal preparation.
    • Assess how central vision loss affects direct-ahead search during routine home tasks.
