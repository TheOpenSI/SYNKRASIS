==============================
Case 046
==============================
1. Patient Context
    • Age: 74
    • Setting: Community optometry following GP referral
    • Referral context: New reading difficulty and one kerb stumble
    • Key diagnosis: Left-eye central vision loss from retinal vascular disease with macular swelling
2. Key Vision Issues
    • Patchy central blur may affect reading, labels, and fine detail.
    • Reduced contrast may make low kerbs and edges harder to identify.
    • Right-eye clarity may partly compensate, but distracted mobility may remain vulnerable.
3. Functional Impact
    • Mobility/Falls: May misjudge low kerbs or step edges when attention is divided.
    • ADLs/IADLs: Reading price labels, medication instructions, and small print may be slower or unreliable.
    • Participation: Community shopping and local driving confidence may be affected.
    • Environment: Low-contrast edges may be difficult to interpret.
4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations
Task Adaptations
    • For medication and shopping tasks, consider large-print labels, consistent storage locations, and checking routines to support accurate identification.
Lighting and Contrast
    • Apply high-contrast tape to front step and outdoor kerb-like edges at home to support safer edge detection with reduced central detail.
Clinical Follow-up / Communication
    • Retinal care is already planned; OT may focus on interim routines for reading-dependent tasks and safe community mobility.
5. Falls Risk Level
    • Moderate vision-related falls risk — central blur and reduced contrast have already been associated with one kerb stumble.
6. Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings
    • Prioritise reading-dependent IADLs and kerb negotiation.
Consider how distraction affects safe mobility when central detail is unreliable.
