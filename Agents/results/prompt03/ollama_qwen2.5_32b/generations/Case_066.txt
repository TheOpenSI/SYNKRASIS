==============================
Case 066
==============================

1. Patient Context
- Age: 71, Female
- Care setting: Community optometry practice
- Falls pathway / referral context: Routine examination identified variable visual clarity and evening mobility hesitation.
- Vision diagnosis: Medication-related ocular surface disease affecting visual function, particularly in the late afternoon.
- Contextual impairment: Long-standing glaucoma treated with multiple drops, hypertension, polypharmacy.

2. Key Vision Issues
- Fluctuating visual acuity and blur throughout the day, worsening in the late afternoon.
- Reduced contrast sensitivity, impacting visual clarity and confidence.
- Tear film instability and ocular surface disease, leading to irritation and uncertainty on steps and kerbs.

3. Functional Impact

**Mobility and Falls**
- Difficulty in detecting kerbs and outdoor steps late in the afternoon.
- Reduced kerb confidence and hesitation when walking outdoors during the evening, leading to uncertainty and potential falls risk.
- Hesitation in late-day outdoor activities and reduced confidence in navigating familiar environments.

**ADLs/IADLs**
- Potential challenges in locating and identifying household objects in low light or complex environments, especially in the late afternoon.
- Reduced contrast sensitivity may affect tasks requiring fine visual acuity, such as reading medication labels or cooking.

**Participation / Confidence / Roles**
- Reduced confidence in outdoor mobility, potentially leading to avoidance of community activities or social outings in the late afternoon.
- Possible dependence on others for late-day errands or activities due to visual uncertainty.

**Environmental Interaction**
- Lighting and contrast levels may need adjustment to support visual clarity and confidence, particularly in the evening.
- Reduced contrast sensitivity may affect the perception of steps and surface changes in the home.

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations

**Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**
- Assess lighting levels in the home, particularly in areas used in the evening, to ensure adequate illumination and reduce glare.
- Consider contrast-enhancing strategies, such as high-contrast visual aids or non-visual cues, to support task performance.

**Lighting and Contrast**
- Evaluate and enhance lighting and contrast in the home to improve visual clarity and confidence in performing daily tasks.
- Consider task-specific lighting, such as a well-lit reading area or adequate lighting near steps and kerbs.

**Mobility Strategies**
- Assess and develop strategies for safe navigation of steps and kerbs, particularly in low-light conditions.
- Consider the use of mobility aids or assistive devices to enhance confidence and safety during evening activities.

**Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies**
- Explore the use of non-visual compensatory strategies, such as tactile or auditory cues, to support tasks where vision is fluctuating.
- Assess the use of assistive devices, such as magnifiers or talking devices, for tasks requiring fine visual acuity.

**Education and Support**
- Educate the patient on the importance of consistent use of prescribed lubricants and the timing of glaucoma medications to manage symptoms.
- Provide guidance on modifying routines or task timing based on fluctuating visual function.

**Clinical Follow-up / Communication**
- Liaise with the ophthalmologist and optometrist to monitor ocular surface health and visual function changes.
- Coordinate with the patient's primary care provider to discuss medication management and polypharmacy.

**Falls Risk Level: Moderate**
- The fluctuating visual function, particularly in the late afternoon, increases the risk of hesitation and uncertainty when navigating steps and kerbs, leading to a moderate falls risk.

5. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings
- Assess and modify lighting and contrast in the home to support visual clarity and confidence.
- Develop safe mobility strategies for navigating steps and kerbs, especially in low-light conditions.
- Explore the use of non-visual compensatory strategies and assistive devices for tasks requiring fine visual acuity.
- Educate the patient on managing fluctuating visual function and coordinating medication use with visual needs.
