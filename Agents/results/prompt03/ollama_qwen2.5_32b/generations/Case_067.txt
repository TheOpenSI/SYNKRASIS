==============================
Case 067
==============================

1. Patient Context
- Age: 79, male
- Care setting: community optometry practice
- Falls pathway: referred after a recent fall on a poorly lit outdoor step
- Key vision diagnosis: Dense bilateral cataract with severe blur, glare, and reduced contrast sensitivity
- Other contextual impairment: hypertension, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (on inhalers and antihypertensive therapy)
- Driving status: Stopped driving 1 month ago due to poor vision and glare
- Living situation: Lives with his wife and now relies on her for transport and reading small print

2. Key Vision Issues
- Severe blur and reduced visual clarity, particularly in low-light conditions, affecting step-edge visibility
- Glare sensitivity, limiting outdoor mobility at dusk and night
- Reduced contrast sensitivity, making it difficult to distinguish objects in low illumination and poor lighting environments
- Current bifocal spectacles do not significantly improve visual acuity or reduce glare, potentially increasing falls risk when navigating stairs and steps

3. Functional Impact

**Mobility and Falls**
- Difficulty detecting step edges in low illumination, increasing risk of falls during outdoor activities
- Reluctance to go outside at dusk due to fear of falling, potentially limiting community access and participation
- Risk of falls while navigating stairs and steps, especially when bifocals are used

**ADLs/IADLs**
- Reliance on spouse for transport and reading small print, affecting independence in mobility and daily tasks
- Difficulty identifying objects and reading labels due to reduced visual acuity and contrast sensitivity, impacting tasks such as meal preparation, medication management, and reading correspondence

**Participation / Confidence / Roles**
- Decreased confidence in outdoor mobility, leading to social isolation and reduced community access
- Reduced independence in daily activities, potentially leading to increased dependence on others for support

**Environmental Interaction**
- Poor lighting conditions in outdoor environments and at home, contributing to step-edge detection issues and task difficulty
- Visual clutter and glare may interfere with task performance in daily activities

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations

**Self-care**
- Assess the impact of visual impairment on self-care tasks such as grooming, dressing, and bathing.
- Consider assistive devices or non-visual compensation techniques to support tasks requiring visual acuity.

**Household Management**
- Evaluate the use of high-contrast labels and markers for medication management and household items.
- Assess the person's ability to manage meal preparation and identify kitchen hazards related to low vision.

**Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**
- Assess and modify the home environment to reduce glare and improve lighting, especially in areas where step edges are present.
- Install high-contrast step edging and consistent task lighting in areas where visual acuity is critical.
- Consider furniture placement and clutter reduction to enhance safety and task performance.

**Lighting and Contrast**
- Assess household lighting for brightness, glare, and shadowing, and modify as needed to support visual acuity and contrast sensitivity.
- Ensure appropriate contrast between objects and backgrounds, particularly in task-specific areas like the kitchen and bathroom.

**Mobility Strategies**
- Consider Orientation & Mobility (O&M) assessment for safe community mobility and route learning.
- Educate the person on scanning strategies and non-visual mobility techniques for low-light and unfamiliar environments.
- Review the use of mobility aids such as canes or walking sticks, particularly in low-visibility conditions.

**Task Adaptations**
- Assess how the person identifies and manages medications, considering the use of larger print labels or assistive technology.
- Evaluate the person's ability to navigate stairs and steps, particularly with bifocals, and consider alternative mobility aids or visual aids.

**Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies**
- Explore the use of assistive technology for reading, such as text-to-speech devices or magnifiers.
- Consider non-visual compensation strategies, such as tactile identification and auditory cues, to support daily tasks.

**Education and Support**
- Educate the spouse on maintaining predictable environments, keeping walkways clear, and reinforcing agreed strategies.
- Provide support-person education on human-guide techniques and mobility practice following specialist instruction.
- Discuss the impact of driving cessation and explore alternative transport options to maintain community access and participation.

**Clinical Follow-up / Communication**
- Liaise with the eye-care provider for updates on the status of the cataract surgery and postoperative visual function.
- Plan follow-up assessments to monitor the person's functional status and support needs post-surgery.

5. Falls Risk Level
- **High**: Severe visual impairment and reduced contrast sensitivity significantly affect step-edge visibility and outdoor mobility, leading to a demonstrated fall history and reluctance to go outside.

6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings
- Environmental assessment and modification to reduce glare and improve lighting conditions.
- Evaluation of household management tasks and use of assistive devices for improved visual acuity and contrast.
- O&M assessment for safe community mobility and non-visual mobility techniques.
- Driving cessation and alternative transport options to maintain community access.
- Support-person education and adaptation of home environment to enhance safety and task performance.
