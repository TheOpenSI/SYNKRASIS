==============================
Case 064
==============================

1. Patient Context
- Age: 82, male
- Care setting: Rehabilitation outpatient clinic
- Falls/referral context: Single fall occurred shortly after discharge due to missing a doorway on the left; currently undergoing rehabilitation following stroke.
- Key vision diagnosis: Stroke-related left homonymous hemianopia
- Other contextual impairments: History of stroke, atrial fibrillation, hypertension on anticoagulation and antihypertensive therapy.

2. Key Vision Issues
- Left homonymous hemianopia affecting obstacle detection and navigation in left visual field.
- Reduced contrast sensitivity, potentially impacting navigation in low-light or complex environments.
- Preserved central acuity but impaired mobility due to hemianopic field loss.

3. Functional Impact

Mobility and Falls
- Obstacle detection: Collisions with left-sided objects such as doorframes and furniture.
- Doorway navigation: Difficulty detecting the start of doorways on the left side.
- Crowded spaces: Slower navigation and higher risk of collisions in busy indoor environments.
- Low-light mobility: Potentially more challenging due to reduced contrast sensitivity.

ADLs/IADLs
- Reading: Difficulty starting lines on the left side, potentially impacting tasks like reading labels or instructions.
- Medication management: May have difficulty locating and identifying medications, particularly if they are placed on the left side.
- Meal preparation: Challenges in locating and using kitchen appliances and items on the left side.

Participation / Confidence / Roles
- Community access: Reduced confidence in navigating public spaces and using public transport.
- Social participation: Potential reduction in social activities due to mobility concerns.

Environmental Interaction
- Clutter: Increased risk of collisions with clutter placed on the left side.
- Lighting: May require improved lighting to enhance contrast and reduce navigation difficulties.

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations

Self-care
- Assess whether the patient has difficulty identifying and locating items during grooming and personal care tasks, particularly those positioned on the left side.
- Consider non-visual strategies or assistive technology (e.g., tactile labels, consistent placement of items) to support these tasks.

Household Management
- Assess the layout of the home for potential obstacles on the left side, such as doorframes and furniture.
- Consider task adaptations to reduce the risk of collisions, such as consistent placement of essential items and reducing clutter in high-traffic areas.

Environmental Modifications / Home Environment
- Assess the home environment for lighting and contrast issues that could affect mobility and navigation.
- Recommend lighting modifications that enhance visibility and reduce glare, particularly in areas where the patient navigates frequently.

Mobility Strategies
- Consider a mobility cane to provide additional safety and orientation.
- Assess the need for a low vision identification badge for increased awareness from others.
- Recommend ongoing OT-led compensatory scanning techniques and environmental cueing to improve navigation.

Task Adaptations
- Assess whether the patient has difficulty starting lines while reading and consider adaptations such as reading guides or text-to-speech technology.
- Evaluate medication management and explore the use of visual or tactile aids to support the identification and organization of medications.

Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies
- Consider the use of auditory or tactile cues to assist with navigation and task performance.
- Explore the use of magnification aids in coordination with a low-vision specialist to support tasks such as reading and meal preparation.

Education and Support
- Educate the patient and support persons on the use of compensatory scanning techniques and environmental cues.
- Consider the need for supervision or assistance, particularly in new or complex environments.
- Discuss the use of a support guide for turning and navigating crowded spaces.

Clinical Follow-up / Communication
- Liaise with the rehabilitation team and general practitioner to communicate vision findings and management strategies.
- Plan for follow-up in 6 months or sooner if there are changes in functional status or new visual symptoms.

5. Falls Risk Level
- Moderate: The left homonymous hemianopia and reduced contrast sensitivity contribute to a moderate falls risk, particularly in environments with clutter or in low-light conditions.

6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings
- Assess and adapt the home environment to reduce collision risks and enhance visibility.
- Implement compensatory scanning techniques and environmental cueing to support safe mobility.
- Consider the use of assistive technology and non-visual strategies to support daily activities.
- Evaluate medication management and meal preparation tasks for specific adaptations or supports.
