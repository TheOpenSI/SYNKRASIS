==============================
Case 057
==============================

1. Patient Context

- Age: 84, male
- Care setting: community optometry practice
- Falls pathway: referred after recurrent falls following stroke rehabilitation
- Key vision diagnosis: right homonymous hemianopia and bilateral cataract
- Contextual impairments: stroke history, hearing impairment, arthritis
- Recurrent falls history: two confirmed falls in the past 4 months
- Driving status: not driving

2. Key Vision Issues

- Right homonymous hemianopia affecting both eyes, leading to missing walls and objects on the right side.
- Bilateral cataracts causing gradual bilateral blur and glare, contributing to falls risk.
- Significantly reduced contrast sensitivity, impacting mobility and task performance.
- Mild to moderate nuclear sclerosis with cortical spokes in both eyes, indicating visually significant cataracts.

3. Functional Impact

**Mobility and Falls:**

- Recurrent falls related to turning toward doorways and stepping from bright sunlight into shaded areas.
- Reduced obstacle detection and poor depth perception may increase risk of falls in low-light environments and stairs.
- Right-sided hemianopia may cause difficulty locating objects and people on the right side, particularly in complex environments.

**ADLs/IADLs:**

- Difficulty in reading and identifying objects due to reduced contrast sensitivity and cataracts.
- Potential challenges in meal preparation, reading medication labels, and managing household tasks.

**Participation / Confidence / Roles:**

- Reduced confidence in outdoor mobility and community access due to visual field loss and glare.
- Possible reliance on others for support and assistance in daily activities.

**Environmental Interaction:**

- Poor lighting and glare contribute to falls risk.
- High-contrast edges may improve stair safety, but current visual acuity may limit effectiveness.

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations

**Self-care:**
- Assess difficulty in identifying and managing medications due to reduced acuity and contrast sensitivity.
- Consider non-visual methods for organizing and identifying daily necessities.

**Household Management:**
- Assess meal preparation skills for heat and blade safety given visual limitations.
- Evaluate the need for task lighting and high-contrast labels on kitchen items.

**Environmental Modifications / Home Environment:**
- Assess the overall lighting environment, including brightness, glare, and shadowing, and consider modifications appropriate to the person's visual needs.
- Recommend the addition of high-contrast step edges and consistent furniture placement to reduce falls risk.

**Lighting and Contrast:**
- Recommend assessment of lighting levels in key task areas, such as the kitchen and bathroom, to reduce glare and enhance contrast.

**Mobility Strategies:**
- Recommend Orientation & Mobility (O&M) assessment for individualized mobility strategies, particularly for outdoor mobility and community navigation.
- Consider the use of a white cane for additional support in low-light and complex environments.

**Task Adaptations:**
- Assess the use of technology or tactile labels for reading and identifying objects.
- Evaluate the need for task-specific adaptations in meal preparation and medication management.

**Education and Support:**
- Educate the support person on maintaining a consistent and predictable environment to enhance safety.
- Provide guidance on the use of Pelli Prisms and other low-vision aids to expand the visual field.

**Clinical Follow-up / Communication:**
- Coordinate with O&M services for community mobility training.
- Liaise with the vision care team regarding the use of low-vision aids and cataract surgery outcomes.

5. Falls Risk Level

**High**

- Recurrent falls history with two confirmed falls in the past 4 months.
- Combined visual field loss and cataracts significantly impair mobility and obstacle detection.
- Visual impairment is partially correctable, but residual field loss and reduced contrast sensitivity remain.

6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings

- Assess medication management and kitchen safety given reduced acuity and contrast sensitivity.
- Recommend environmental assessment for lighting and high-contrast modifications.
- Refer for O&M assessment for individualized mobility strategies.
- Educate the support person on environmental consistency and visual aids.

This summary translates the optometry findings into functional implications and provides OT-relevant considerations for assessment, intervention, and multidisciplinary communication.
