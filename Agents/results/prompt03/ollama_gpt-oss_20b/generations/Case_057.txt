==============================
Case 057
==============================


1. **Patient Context**  
   - 84‑year‑old male, community resident, lives with wife.  
   - History of ischemic stroke (≈2 y ago) with right homonymous hemianopia; bilateral age‑related cataract.  
   - Recurrent falls (2 in 4 months) during turning and step‑transition; reports missing walls/objects on right side, glare, and haze.  
   - Uses bifocals full‑time; no diplopia.  
   - Medical comorbidities: hypertension, arthritis, hearing impairment; on antihypertensive and antiplatelet therapy.  
   - Not driving; receiving rehabilitation follow‑up for mobility and home safety.

2. **Key Vision Issues**  
   - **Right homonymous hemianopia** affecting both eyes → loss of peripheral and central right‑side visual information.  
   - **Reduced distance acuity and contrast sensitivity** (6/36 OD/OU, Pelli‑Robson 1.05) → impaired detection of edges, shadows and low‑contrast objects.  
   - **Bilateral cataract‑induced glare/blur** → worsened task performance in bright light and during transitions between lighting conditions.  
   - **Use of bifocals full‑time** → potential depth‑perception compromise on stairs and steps.

3. **Functional Impact**  

   **Mobility and Falls**  
   - Difficulty detecting walls, doorways, step edges, and changes in floor level on the right side.  
   - Reduced ability to anticipate glare‑induced visual distortion when moving from sunlight to shade.  
   - Potential risk of misjudging depth when using bifocals on stairs or uneven surfaces.  

   **ADLs/IADLs**  
   - Likely challenges in activities requiring right‑field awareness: e.g., navigating kitchen aisles, locating utensils, reading labels on right‑handed shelves.  
   - Potential difficulty with medication handling due to reduced near acuity and glare.  

   **Participation / Confidence / Roles**  
   - Reduced confidence in navigating community routes, entering and exiting buildings.  
   - Possible reliance on wife for spatial orientation and safety checks.  

   **Environmental Interaction**  
   - Inconsistent lighting (bright sunlight to shade) exacerbates glare and loss of contrast.  
   - Home layout may contain uneven surfaces or clutter that are harder to detect on the right side.

4. **Vision‑Informed OT Considerations**  

   **Mobility Strategies**  
   - *Assess* the effectiveness of current scanning techniques taught in rehabilitation; consider formal O&M referral for advanced scanning, route learning and environmental awareness training.  
   - *Explore* the feasibility of a cane or guide‑dog for independent navigation; coordinate with low‑vision services for training and support.  

   **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**  
   - *Assess* home lighting: brightness, glare control, and consistent illumination across rooms; recommend installing brighter, adjustable entryway lights and eliminating or reducing high‑contrast glare sources (e.g., reflective surfaces).  
   - *Assess* step and surface edge visibility: high‑contrast tape or paint on stair treads and thresholds; consider non‑slip, low‑contrast edge markers.  
   - *Assess* placement of key items: keep frequently used objects on the left side or in well‑lit, high‑contrast areas to compensate for right‑field loss.  

   **Lighting and Contrast**  
   - *Assess* effectiveness of current ambient lighting; recommend using diffuse, wide‑beam fixtures in living areas and kitchens to reduce glare.  
   - *Assess* potential benefits of task lighting (e.g., adjustable LED desk lamps) during meal prep or medication handling to improve contrast.  

   **Task Adaptations**  
   - *Assess* right‑field–dependent tasks: e.g., placing utensils in right hand; consider reorganising kitchen layout to reduce right‑side reliance.  
   - *Assess* near‑reading tasks (labels, medication instructions); explore use of larger print or audio aids.  

   **Assistive Technology / Non‑visual Compensation**  
   - *Explore* use of tactile cues (e.g., distinct floor textures, raised edge markers) for step negotiation.  
   - *Explore* auditory cues for spatial orientation: e.g., using a timer or audio prompts during transitions.  

   **Education and Support**  
   - Provide education to wife on maintaining consistent home layout, clearing walkways, and reinforcing scanning strategies.  
   - Discuss safe use of bifocals: advise against full‑time use on stairs; consider daytime use only in low‑risk settings.  

   **Clinical Follow‑up / Communication**  
   - Coordinate with ophthalmology for cataract surgery outcomes and potential changes in acuity/contrast.  
   - Re‑evaluate OT goals after cataract intervention to reassess fall risk and mobility performance.  

   **Orientation & Mobility Referral**  
   - Given right‑hemispheric field loss and recurrent falls, *recommend* formal O&M assessment to optimise route planning, cane technique, and environmental awareness.  

5. **Falls Risk Level**  
   **High** – severe right‑field loss combined with reduced acuity/contrast, glare, and recent falls on turning and step transitions, despite ongoing rehabilitation.

6. **Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings**  
   - Prioritise assessment of safe stair/step negotiation and doorway navigation using cane or guide‑dog.  
   - Conduct comprehensive environmental lighting and contrast evaluation; implement high‑contrast edge markers and glare‑reducing measures.  
   - Re‑evaluate scanning strategies and consider formal O&M referral to reduce right‑side blind‑spot risks.  
   - Provide targeted education to the spouse on consistent home organization and support strategies for ambulation and task safety.
