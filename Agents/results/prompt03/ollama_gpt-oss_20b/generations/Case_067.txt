==============================
Case 067
==============================


1. **Patient Context**  
79‑year‑old male, community optometry referral after a single fall stepping off a poorly lit patio edge.  
Has dense bilateral cataract causing severe blur, glare and reduced contrast sensitivity.  
Lives with his wife; currently relies on her for transport and for reading small print.  
Stopped driving one month ago due to vision loss.  
Relevant medical history: hypertension and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).  
Uses old bifocals full‑time; no diplopia or ocular surgery.

2. **Key Vision Issues**  
- Severe bilateral loss of distance and near acuity (6/120 OD, 6/96 OS; N48 OU) limiting detection of low‑contrast step edges and fine print.  
- Pronounced glare sensitivity; step‑edge visibility markedly reduced in low illumination or at dusk.  
- Contrast sensitivity not measurable due to reduced acuity, implying poor discrimination of subtle surface transitions.  
- Near‑addition of +4.00 D (4 XP) with orthophoria distance; depth perception likely reduced, affecting safe stair negotiation.

3. **Functional Impact**

| Domain | Potential Implications | Notes |
|--------|------------------------|-------|
| **Mobility & Falls** | Difficulty locating step edges, especially outdoors in low light; reluctance to venture outside at dusk. | Recent fall on poorly lit step; glare and low contrast directly contribute. |
| **ADLs/IADLs** | Challenges reading medication labels, fine print on labels or instructions; potential for medication errors. | Wife provides reading assistance. |
| **Participation / Confidence** | Reduced outdoor activity, dependence on spouse for transport, possible social isolation. | Falls history and glare may discourage independent mobility. |
| **Environmental Interaction** | Lighting and glare levels in home; step edging and surface changes may be insufficiently contrasted; clutter or uneven flooring may be hard to detect. | Home modifications recommended by optometrist. |

4. **Vision‑Informed OT Considerations**

**Self‑Care & Medication Management**  
- *Assess* whether the patient can reliably identify medication blister packs or labels in current lighting; consider high‑contrast or tactile markings if deficits noted.  
- *Intervention* could include large‑print medication lists, voice‑activated pill dispensers, or audible reminders.

**Household Management**  
- *Assess* the safety of navigating uneven outdoor surfaces (e.g., garden paths, porch steps) under varying light levels.  
- *Consider* high‑contrast step edging, clear pathways, and consistent storage of frequently used items to aid non‑visual navigation.

**Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**  
- *Assess* lighting levels in key areas (entryway, kitchen, bathroom) and the presence of glare from windows or fixtures.  
- *Interventions*: install task lighting on stair risers, use bright, glare‑reducing fixtures, and add high‑contrast markings or tactile cues on steps and thresholds.  
- *Consider* removing or rearranging clutter that could interfere with safe movement.

**Mobility Strategies**  
- *Assess* the patient’s current step‑edge detection ability and confidence when using bifocals on stairs.  
- *Intervention*: encourage use of bifocals only on flat surfaces; provide a step‑edge guide or textured mat if necessary.  
- *Referral*: recommend Orientation & Mobility (O&M) assessment for outdoor navigation, focusing on low‑light conditions and safe route planning.

**Assistive Technology / Non‑Visual Compensation**  
- *Assess* the feasibility of large‑print materials, screen magnifiers, or voice‑output devices for reading.  
- *Consider* tactile identification tools (e.g., Braille labels) if visual function remains insufficient.

**Education and Support**  
- Provide training for the patient and wife on safe step navigation, lighting management, and consistent storage habits.  
- Discuss strategies to mitigate glare (e.g., sunglasses, window treatments) and to maximize ambient light at dusk.  
- Encourage the wife to maintain clear walkways and to assist during outdoor activities until vision improves.

**Driving / Transport**  
- Reinforce the decision to refrain from driving until post‑surgery vision meets legal criteria.  
- Explore alternative transport options (public transport, community shuttles) and ensure safe transfer protocols if using a car.

**Clinical Follow‑Up / Communication**  
- Coordinate with ophthalmology for cataract surgery timeline; review visual function improvements prior to re‑assessing mobility or driving readiness.  
- Re‑evaluate fall risk and environmental safety after any vision change.

5. **Falls Risk Level**  
**High** – Severe visual acuity loss, glare sensitivity, and reduced contrast severely compromise step‑edge detection in low light, leading to a recent fall and ongoing fear of outdoor mobility.

6. **Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings**  
- Prioritize environmental lighting and high‑contrast step edging to reduce low‑light fall risk.  
- Assess and support medication management through large‑print or auditory aids.  
- Facilitate safe outdoor navigation via an O&M referral and spouse‑led transfer training.  
- Monitor vision changes post‑cataract surgery to inform future mobility and driving decisions.
