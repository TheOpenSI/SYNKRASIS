==============================
Case 035
==============================


1. **Patient Context**  
   - 67‑year‑old male with moderate primary open‑angle glaucoma OU.  
   - Reports worsening difficulty detecting floor‑level hazards, especially in dim lighting; several near‑misses in hallway areas but no confirmed falls.  
   - Uses single‑vision distance spectacles for walking and readers for near work; no central blur or diplopia.  
   - Active lifestyle: attends gym regularly.  
   - Drives daytime and occasional dusk trips; no driving cessation reported.  
   - Medical comorbidities: hypertension, obstructive sleep apnea.  


2. **Key Vision Issues**  
   - Peripheral field loss (inferior arcuate defects) → impaired spatial awareness and obstacle detection.  
   - Reduced contrast sensitivity (1.35 log units) → challenges distinguishing low‑contrast floor patterns and shadows.  
   - Decreased low‑level illumination detection (reported near‑misses in dim hallways).  


3. **Functional Impact**  
   **Mobility and Falls**  
   - Risk of stepping on low furniture or uneven floor surfaces; difficulty judging step/kerb edges in low light.  
   - Near‑misses suggest impaired hazard detection, increasing fall risk in dim environments.  

   **ADLs/IADLs**  
   - Potential difficulty navigating narrow passages, doorways, and cluttered living areas.  
   - Possible challenge in stair negotiation when lighting is insufficient.  

   **Participation / Confidence / Roles**  
   - Confidence in community mobility (gym, errands) may be compromised by obstacle‑detection anxiety.  
   - Driving, especially dusk trips, may be affected by reduced peripheral awareness and low‑light vision.  

   **Environmental Interaction**  
   - Low illumination, shadows, and contrast differences on floor and walls may mask hazards.  
   - Potential clutter or variable lighting levels in home corridors could increase risk.  


4. **Vision‑Informed OT Considerations**  

   **Mobility Strategies**  
   - *Assess* current hallway and stairway lighting levels and the visibility of floor edges.  
   - *Consider* the use of high‑contrast floor markings or tactile cues (e.g., raised strips) to delineate low obstacles.  
   - *Recommend* a routine visual scanning strategy (e.g., slow, deliberate steps, head turning to scan ahead) after an Orientation & Mobility (O&M) evaluation.  

   **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**  
   - *Assess* overall lighting: task lighting at entrances, hallways, and stair treads; identify glare sources.  
   - *Consider* installing motion‑activated or brighter LED fixtures in low‑light areas.  
   - *Recommend* removal or repositioning of low‑lying furniture and rugs that may serve as obstacles.  
   - *Encourage* consistent placement of commonly used items to minimise surprise hazards.  

   **Lighting and Contrast**  
   - *Assess* contrast between floor surfaces and surrounding walls; verify adequate illumination (lux levels) for safe navigation.  
   - *Consider* the addition of high‑contrast paint or tape on stair risers and step edges.  

   **Task Adaptations**  
   - *Assess* whether low‑light situations (e.g., evening shopping, driving dusk) increase task difficulty.  
   - *Consider* scheduling high‑risk activities during daylight when vision is better.  

   **Assistive Technology / Non‑visual Compensation**  
   - *Explore* the use of reflective or light‑reflecting markers on furniture edges.  
   - *Consider* simple tactile or auditory cues (e.g., using a cane with vibration feedback) if O&M indicates benefit.  

   **Education and Support**  
   - *Educate* the patient on safe navigation techniques: step ahead, turn head, use handrail.  
   - *Inform* informal caregivers or household members about maintaining clear pathways and providing assistance during low‑light situations.  
   - *Discuss* the importance of consistent spectacle wear during walking and for near tasks to ensure optimal vision.  

   **Driving / Transport**  
   - *Review* with the patient the risk of dusk trips; *consider* a formal driving assessment if visual field loss is progressive.  
   - *Educate* on using vehicle lighting (e.g., interior lights, high‑beam) and avoiding driving in low‑visibility conditions.  

   **Referral**  
   - *Referral to O&M* to develop personalised scanning strategies and route planning.  
   - *Coordinate* with ophthalmology for ongoing glaucoma management; note that any change in eyewear prescription should be communicated to OT for potential task‑specific implications.  

   **Support / Formal Services**  
   - *Assess* need for additional support (e.g., in‑home occupational therapy visits, home modification assistance) if hazards persist despite modifications.  


5. **Falls Risk Level**  
   - **Moderate** – peripheral field loss and reduced contrast sensitivity, coupled with near‑miss incidents in dim lighting, increase fall risk, though no actual falls have occurred yet.  

6. **Summary of Key OT Considerations**  
   - Prioritise safe navigation of low‑level obstacles through environmental modification and mobility strategy training.  
   - Enhance lighting and contrast in home corridors and stairways to mitigate low‑light hazards.  
   - Coordinate with O&M for individualized scanning and route strategies, especially for driving and community mobility.  
   - Provide education to the patient and informal caregivers on maintaining clear pathways and employing consistent visual scanning habits.  

This summary offers occupational‑therapy‑relevant interpretations of the vision findings to guide assessment and intervention planning while respecting multidisciplinary boundaries.
