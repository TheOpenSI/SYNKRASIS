==============================
Case 072
==============================


1. **Patient Context**  
   - 82‑year‑old woman, lives independently with occasional family support.  
   - Recent retinal review skipped; now experiencing worsening central vision due to bilateral geographic atrophy (advanced dry AMD).  
   - Reports difficulty reading medication labels and a recent stumble in the kitchen; no other falls recorded.  
   - No longer drives.  
   - Medical history: hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, osteoporosis (antihypertensive and statin therapy).  

2. **Key Vision Issues**  
   - **Central visual acuity loss** (6/60 OD, 6/30 OS; near VA N12 OU).  
   - **Reduced contrast sensitivity** (not testable but consistent with central vision loss).  
   - **Preserved peripheral fields** – no field loss.  
   - **Orthophoria** with minimal near convergence weakness (4 Δ near).  
   - **Pseudophakia OU**; no refractive error beyond bifocals.  

3. **Functional Impact**  

   **Mobility and Falls**  
   - Central vision loss can impair object detection directly ahead, increasing tripping risk especially on patterned floors.  
   - One documented stumble in kitchen; no recurrent falls but potential for future incidents.

   **ADLs/IADLs**  
   - **Medication management:** difficulty reading labels may lead to errors.  
   - **Meal preparation:** challenges in reading ingredient labels, measuring spoons, identifying food items, and gauging cooking times.  
   - **Household organisation:** may be affected by reduced ability to locate items quickly.

   **Participation / Confidence / Roles**  
   - Possible reduced confidence in cooking independently, which may impact social participation or meal sharing.  

   **Environmental Interaction**  
   - Patterned floor mats and low‑contrast surfaces present visual hazards; lighting inadequacies may reduce task visibility.

4. **Vision‑Informed OT Considerations**  

   **Self‑care & Household Management**  
   - *Assess:* how she currently reads medication labels; consider if she uses a magnifier or assistive device.  
   - *Consider:* integrating large‑print labels, high‑contrast labels, or a medication reminder app.  
   - *Task analysis:* evaluate step‑by‑step meal‑preparation routine to identify high‑risk points (e.g., reaching for items, turning in kitchen).  

   **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**  
   - *Assess:* overall kitchen layout, lighting levels, floor‑surface contrast, placement consistency of frequently used items.  
   - *Recommend:* brighter, uniform task lighting in the kitchen; use of high‑contrast paint or tape to delineate countertops, stove edges, and cabinet fronts.  
   - *Recommend:* replace patterned floor mats with solid‑color, high‑contrast mats or remove them to reduce visual clutter; ensure clear visual pathways to sink, stove, and fridge.  
   - *Recommend:* maintain a clutter‑free walk‑way and keep essential items in predictable locations.  

   **Lighting and Contrast**  
   - *Assess:* adequacy of ambient and task lighting; presence of glare or shadows that may obscure objects.  
   - *Recommend:* use of adjustable desk or pendant lights over key work surfaces; addition of LED strip lighting along cabinets to enhance edge visibility.  

   **Mobility Strategies**  
   - *Consider:* training in a systematic scanning technique to locate objects directly ahead, especially when walking in narrow spaces.  
   - *Consider:* brief orientation and mobility refresher if she experiences difficulty navigating between kitchen zones.  

   **Assistive Technology / Non‑visual Compensation**  
   - *Assess:* suitability and training for a handheld magnifier for label reading; evaluate smartphone or tablet with screen‑reader or text‑to‑speech for medication lists and recipes.  
   - *Consider:* use of audible kitchen timers or voice‑activated assistants to support cooking and medication reminders.  

   **Education and Support**  
   - *Educate:* family/support person on maintaining consistent kitchen layout and lighting; importance of removing potential hazards.  
   - *Educate:* she on safe strategies for reading labels (e.g., use of magnifier, moving to well‑lit areas).  

   **Clinical Follow‑up / Communication**  
   - *Coordinate:* with low‑vision service for magnification and contrast‑enhancement devices.  
   - *Plan:* routine retinal review every 4 months or sooner if new visual changes occur; discuss any emerging functional challenges.  

5. **Falls Risk Level**  
   - **Moderate** – central vision loss and recent kitchen stumble indicate a tangible but not yet recurrent fall risk, especially on patterned surfaces; ongoing monitoring and environmental adaptation are warranted.

6. **Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings**  
   - **Fall Prevention in the Kitchen:** assess and modify lighting, floor‑surface contrast, and clutter; train in scanning techniques.  
   - **Medication Management Safety:** evaluate label‑reading strategies; implement large‑print or electronic reminders.  
   - **Meal Preparation Adaptation:** analyze cooking routine for visual hazards; provide magnification, high‑contrast tools, and auditory cues.  
   - **Environmental Consistency & Education:** ensure predictable item placement, educate carer/family on maintaining a safe home environment, and integrate low‑vision support referrals.
