==============================
Case 033
==============================


1. **Patient Context**  
- 63‑year‑old woman, community optometry practice.  
- No documented falls; reports hesitancy walking on uneven paving when glasses are removed.  
- Lives independently, works part‑time, walks daily for exercise, drives regularly (must wear glasses).  
- Under‑corrected myopia with habitual removal of distance spectacles for near tasks, leading to blurred distance vision while walking.  
- Mild meibomian gland dysfunction, early nuclear sclerosis; otherwise normal ocular fields and colour vision.

2. **Key Vision Issues**  
- **Distance under‑correction** → blurred distance vision when glasses are not worn.  
- **Behavioural non‑use** of distance spectacles for walking, with forgetting to re‑apply after near tasks.  
- **Reduced visual clarity on uneven or low‑contrast surfaces** (e.g., garden path edges, dull weather).  
- **Potential for mis‑judgement of steps/edges** due to lack of clear distance focus.  
- **Driving dependency on correct spectacle use** (license requirement).

3. **Functional Impact**  

| Domain | Likely implications (documented or inferred) |
|--------|---------------------------------------------|
| **Mobility & Falls** | • Difficulty detecting edges and uneven paving when spectacles are off.<br>• Slower walking speed outdoors, especially in dull lighting.<br>• Potential for trips or falls if path changes. |
| **ADLs/IADLs** | • Reading tasks trigger removal of spectacles, which may continue while walking.<br>• Uncertainty about object distance may affect meal preparation (e.g., pouring) or grocery shopping if vision is impaired at distance. |
| **Participation / Confidence** | • Hesitancy may reduce willingness to walk outdoors or participate in exercise.<br>• Confidence in driving and daily errands may be impacted by fear of visual loss. |
| **Environmental Interaction** | • Low‑contrast pathways and dim lighting increase risk of mis‑judgement.<br>• Visual clutter or inconsistent placement of household items may be misinterpreted if spectacles are not worn. |

4. **Vision‑Informed OT Considerations**

- **Self‑Care / Household Management**  
  - Assess whether the patient can reliably remember to put on distance glasses before walking and after near tasks.  
  - Consider environmental cues (e.g., a coloured peg or light near the glasses case) to prompt re‑application.

- **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**  
  - Evaluate walkways for uneven paving or changes in surface texture; where feasible, smooth or seal high‑contrast edges.  
  - Ensure outdoor pathways are well lit, with adequate illumination and minimal glare.  
  - Assess household layout for consistent placement of items that might be mistaken for obstacles when spectacles are off.

- **Lighting and Contrast**  
  - Recommend consistent, glare‑controlled lighting in indoor spaces, especially near outdoor entrances.  
  - Use high‑contrast floor strips or tactile edging to delineate steps or garden paths.

- **Mobility Strategies**  
  - If the patient continues to experience hesitancy or near‑miss incidents, recommend a formal Orientation & Mobility (O&M) assessment to develop individualized scanning and route‑learning strategies.  
  - Encourage routine pre‑walking checks (e.g., “glasses on?”) to reduce the chance of accidental removal.

- **Assistive Technology / Non‑Visual Compensation**  
  - Explore the feasibility of magnification aids (e.g., handheld magnifier) for near tasks that might encourage prolonged spectacle wear.  
  - Consider voice‑activated reminders or a smartwatch notification to prompt glasses use before walking.

- **Education and Support**  
  - Provide clear instructions on the importance of wearing distance spectacles for safe walking and driving.  
  - If a partner or caregiver is present, educate them on how to support consistent spectacle use and safe mobility practices.

- **Driving / Transport**  
  - Reinforce that glasses must be worn for driving; discuss strategies to ensure glasses are on before leaving home (e.g., placing them in a visible location).  
  - Discuss alternative transport options should the patient be unable to maintain consistent spectacle use.

- **Clinical Follow‑Up / Communication**  
  - Plan a reassessment in 6 weeks (or sooner if a fall occurs) to evaluate adaptation to multifocals and mobility confidence.  
  - Coordinate with the optometrist regarding prescription changes and any emerging visual concerns.

5. **Falls Risk Level**  
- **High** – Under‑corrected distance vision combined with habitual spectacle removal for near tasks creates a significant risk of mis‑judging steps and uneven surfaces. The patient’s hesitancy and recent reports of slowing down outdoors further indicate a high likelihood of future falls if the issue remains unaddressed.

6. **Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings**  
- **Behavioural compliance**: assess and support consistent use of distance spectacles during walking.  
- **Mobility safety**: evaluate and modify uneven pathways; consider O&M referral if hesitancy persists.  
- **Environmental optimisation**: improve lighting, contrast, and pathway delineation to mitigate edge detection failures.  
- **Driving and transport**: reinforce spectacle use for driving and explore alternative transport if consistent compliance cannot be achieved.
