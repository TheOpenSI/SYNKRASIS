==============================
Case 083
==============================


**1. Patient Context**  
- 78‑year‑old female, community optometry shared‑care review.  
- Primary diagnosis: normal‑tension glaucoma OU with progression to moderate‑right, advanced‑left field loss.  
- Reports increased side‑collision incidents (doorframes, laundry baskets) and pauses when turning in hallways; remains independent at home but more cautious in cluttered areas.  
- Driving limited to short daytime distances; advised to suspend driving pending Esterman assessment.  
- Relevant comorbidity: hypertension (on treatment), migraine, Raynaud’s phenomenon.  
- No recent falls, but two documented side‑collision events.

**2. Key Vision Issues**  
- **Peripheral field loss** – inferior arcuate and paracentral defects producing reduced side‑vision awareness, particularly on the right.  
- **Reduced contrast sensitivity** (1.35 log units) – may affect detection of low‑contrast hazards in dim or cluttered environments.  
- **Minor binocular misalignment** (4 XP near) – could influence near‑task hand‑eye coordination, though not currently limiting.

**3. Functional Impact**  

*Mobility & Falls*  
- Increased risk of side‑collisions when negotiating doorways, narrow hallways, and cluttered pathways.  
- Greater caution and pausing when turning, indicating awareness of impaired lateral visual information.  
- Potentially higher risk of tripping or bumping into stationary objects in poorly lit or cluttered spaces.

*ADLs/IADLs*  
- Likely safe to perform near‑tasks (reading, using readers) but may struggle to identify and retrieve items in peripheral areas (e.g., locating a dropped object on a counter or a book on a shelf edge).  
- No documented issues with medication management or meal preparation; however, peripheral field loss could impact spotting kitchen hazards or locating items during cooking.

*Participation / Confidence*  
- Maintains independence at home but reports increased caution in cluttered environments; may affect confidence in navigating living areas or social activities requiring navigation (e.g., community outings).  

*Environmental Interaction*  
- Hallway lighting, doorway visibility, cluttered pathways, and contrast of furniture edges are potential safety modifiers.

**4. Vision‑Informed OT Considerations**  

- **Mobility Strategies**  
  - *Assess* whether current hallway navigation and turning practices are adequate.  
  - *Recommend* deliberate scanning of peripheral zones before initiating a turn; consider a routine “scan‑look‑move” cue system.  
  - *Consider* referral to an Orientation & Mobility specialist if the patient’s side‑collision incidents increase or if she expresses anxiety about community mobility.

- **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**  
  - *Assess* overall clutter levels in hallways, living rooms, and entryways; identify high‑risk objects (laundry baskets, doorframes).  
  - *Recommend* removal or relocation of low‑lying or protruding items that could be collided with.  
  - *Advise* consistent furniture placement to aid spatial orientation.  
  - *Consider* the use of visual markers (e.g., contrasting strips) at doorframe edges to improve perceptual cueing.

- **Lighting and Contrast**  
  - *Assess* hallway and entryway illumination; determine if current lighting is adequate for peripheral detection.  
  - *Recommend* task‑lighting for entryways and increased ambient lighting in narrow passages to enhance edge visibility and reduce reliance on peripheral vision alone.  
  - *Explore* use of high‑contrast, non‑reflective floor strips to delineate steps and curbs.

- **Task Adaptations**  
  - *Assess* whether the patient can reliably locate kitchen utensils or food items; if not, suggest placing frequently used items in a consistent, easily accessible location.  
  - *Consider* a tactile or auditory cue system (e.g., placing a small bell on a drawer handle) to aid in locating and retrieving items.

- **Support & Education**  
  - *Educate* the patient on consistent spectacle use (distance lenses during walking) and on the importance of maintaining a clutter‑free environment.  
  - *Inform* any informal caregivers about the side‑collision risk and the importance of keeping pathways clear.  
  - *Provide* brief instruction on how to use a “stop‑scan‑move” technique for hallway navigation.

- **Driving / Transport**  
  - *Document* the current driving restriction and plan for re‑assessment after Esterman test.  
  - *Explore* alternative transport options (public transport, community ride‑share) to maintain independence while visual field limitations persist.

- **Participation / Community Access**  
  - *Assess* whether reduced side‑vision impacts the patient’s confidence in attending community activities; consider gradual re‑introduction to group settings with supportive environmental modifications.

**Falls Risk Level**  
- **Moderate** – advanced left‑field loss and recent side‑collision incidents raise concern for future falls, particularly in cluttered or poorly lit environments, but no falls have occurred yet.

**5. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings**  
- **Side‑collision risk**: evaluate hallway navigation, implement scanning strategies, and remove or relocate obstacles.  
- **Reduced contrast sensitivity**: improve lighting, add high‑contrast visual cues to pathways.  
- **Peripheral field loss**: consider O&M referral if community mobility becomes problematic; educate on consistent spectacle wear and clutter management.  
- **Driving restriction**: coordinate with patient to ensure safe transportation alternatives and plan for re‑assessment.

These points should guide your initial assessment and identify priorities for task‑specific evaluations and environmental modifications.
