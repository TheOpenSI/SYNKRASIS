==============================
Case 032
==============================


1. **Patient Context**  
   • 80‑year‑old male living with wife in a community dwelling.  
   • Recurrent outdoor falls (two documented on uneven footpaths, multiple falls in past 4 months).  
   • Recent onset left sixth‑nerve palsy producing binocular horizontal diplopia worse at distance and in left gaze.  
   • Uses bifocals full‑time; has stopped driving after diplopia became frequent.  
   • Medical history: type 2 diabetes, hypertension, hypercholesterolaemia.  
   • Vision: mild loss of stereopsis, mild contrast‑sensitivity reduction, no field loss.  

2. **Key Vision Issues**  
   • **Binocular depth‑perception dysfunction** due to loss of fusion at distance (18 ST deviation, diplopia).  
   • **Horizontal diplopia** that worsens on left gaze and with distance, affecting object localisation.  
   • **Use of bifocals** for all activities, which may compound double‑vision on uneven surfaces and at kerb height.  
   • **Microvascular cranial‑nerve palsy** – an acute, potentially reversible source of visual disturbance.  

3. **Functional Impact**  

   **Mobility and Falls**  
   - Difficulty judging kerb height and step depth → increased fall risk on uneven footpaths and stair negotiation.  
   - Double vision may limit awareness of obstacles, curbs, and surface changes.  
   - Reduced confidence walking alone outdoors; reliance on wife for support.  

   **ADLs/IADLs**  
   - Potential difficulty safely stepping onto/off stairs or curb edges.  
   - Possible challenges in identifying household items or labels when double‑vision interferes with distance focus.  
   - Uncertainty in reading driving‑related or medication labels at distance.  

   **Participation / Confidence / Roles**  
   - Cessation of driving reduces community access, likely impacting shopping, appointments, and social outings.  
   - Possible social isolation if walking outdoors remains restricted.  

   **Environmental Interaction**  
   - Home and outdoor environment may contain uneven surfaces, low or glare‑prone lighting, cluttered pathways, and inconsistent curb heights.  

4. **Vision‑Informed OT Considerations**

   **Mobility Strategies & O&M**  
   - *Assess* current walking patterns: step‑length, gait speed, use of cane or other aids, and confidence levels when navigating curbs, uneven terrain, and stairs.  
   - *Consider* referral for Orientation & Mobility (O&M) assessment if depth‑perception loss continues or if the patient demonstrates persistent gait instability despite adaptation.  
   - *Explore* prism or occlusion training: temporary Fresnel prism or patch may reduce diplopia during ambulation; O&M or low‑vision professionals can coordinate training and safety checks.  

   **Environmental Modifications**  
   - *Assess* home and outdoor pathways for uneven surfaces, kerb height, step depth, and handrail presence.  
   - *Recommend* that the patient (and wife) maintain a predictable layout: keep pathways clear of clutter, ensure adequate task lighting, and use high‑contrast markers on steps or curb edges.  
   - *Assess* bathroom and stairwell for safe handrail placement, non‑slip flooring, and adequate lighting.  

   **Eye‑Wear & Prescription Adjustments**  
   - *Assess* functional impact of bifocals during walking: if double‑vision persists, switch to single‑vision distance correction for ambulation while maintaining bifocals for near tasks.  
   - *Discuss* with optometrist about interim prism or occlusion solutions that balance safety and independence.  

   **Task Adaptations**  
   - *Assess* whether the patient can reliably identify household objects at distance; if not, consider tactile or auditory cues for key items (e.g., medication boxes, keys).  
   - *Educate* on using a “walking line” (e.g., tape or carpet edge) to aid step depth perception when negotiating stairs or curbs.  

   **Support & Education**  
   - *Educate* wife on maintaining clear pathways, using consistent lighting, and providing verbal cues when the patient navigates uneven surfaces.  
   - *Consider* formal support services (e.g., home‑visit occupational therapy, community mobility training) if independent outdoor walking remains unsafe after interventions.  

   **Participation & Community Access**  
   - *Assist* in identifying alternative transport options (public transport, rideshare, community shuttle) to mitigate loss of driving independence.  
   - *Encourage* gradual re‑introduction to outdoor walking with supervision, beginning in low‑risk environments (e.g., flat indoor corridor) before progressing to uneven footpaths.  

5. **Falls Risk Level**  
   **High** – Recurrent outdoor falls, acute depth‑perception loss from diplopia, and age‑related physiological changes create a compounded risk that warrants focused OT assessment and targeted interventions.

6. **Summary of Key OT Considerations**  
   • Assess and support safe ambulation on uneven surfaces and stairs, with a focus on depth‑perception strategies and potential prism/occlusion use.  
   • Conduct an environmental assessment of home and outdoor pathways to remove obstacles, improve lighting, and enhance step/kerb visibility.  
   • Coordinate with optometrist on switching to single‑vision distance correction for walking and on temporary prism/occlusion solutions.  
   • Provide wife‑focused education on maintaining a predictable, clear environment and on supportive cues during outdoor mobility.
