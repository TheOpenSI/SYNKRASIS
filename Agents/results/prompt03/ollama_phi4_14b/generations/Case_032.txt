==============================
Case 032
==============================

1. **Patient Context**

An 80-year-old male with recent onset left sixth nerve palsy presents at a community optometry practice following GP referral due to recurrent falls outdoors. The patient has a history of type 2 diabetes, hypertension, hypercholesterolaemia, and uses bifocals full-time. He lives with his wife and has stopped driving due to frequent diplopia.

2. **Key Vision Issues**

- **Binocular horizontal diplopia**: More pronounced at a distance and in left gaze, leading to functional difficulties in depth perception and spatial awareness.
- **Loss of functional stereopsis**: Impairs depth judgement, affecting tasks like kerb crossing and mobility on uneven surfaces.
- **Left sixth nerve palsy**: Results in horizontal diplopia and left eye abduction deficit, complicating safe navigation and mobility outdoors.
- **Reduced contrast sensitivity**: Could affect the perception of depth and detail in low-light or complex visual environments.

3. **Functional Impact**

**Mobility and Falls**
- Difficulty judging kerb height and uneven surfaces may increase fall risk.
- Horizontal diplopia complicates safe navigation and obstacle avoidance.
- Impaired binocular vision affects stability when stepping off kerbs or crossing uneven ground.

**ADLs/IADLs**
- Outdoor mobility tasks such as walking on footpaths or crossing car parks may be challenging.
- Navigating unfamiliar or complex environments could be problematic, potentially leading to increased reliance on others for assistance.

**Participation / Confidence / Roles**
- The patient may experience reduced confidence in outdoor activities, potentially leading to decreased independence and social participation.
- Reliance on others for mobility assistance may impact daily routines and independence.

**Environmental Interaction**
- The patient's ability to navigate different lighting conditions or visually cluttered environments may be compromised, necessitating environmental modifications.

4. **Vision-Informed OT Considerations**

**Self-care**
- Assess the patient's ability to perform daily tasks independently, considering the potential impact of vision loss on confidence and safety.

**Mobility Strategies**
- Explore visual enhancement strategies, such as single-vision distance correction, to support safer mobility.
- Assess the need for non-visual mobility strategies or assistive devices to compensate for reduced depth perception.

**Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**
- Assess the home and community environment for potential hazards, recommending modifications like improved lighting or clear walkways to enhance safety.

**Education and Support**
- Educate the patient and his wife on safe mobility practices and the use of temporary occlusion or prisms if prescribed.
- Discuss strategies to maintain independence and confidence in outdoor activities.

**Clinical Follow-up / Communication**
- Coordinate with the GP and other healthcare providers to ensure comprehensive management of the patient's vision and overall health.

**Falls Risk Level**
- Moderate: The patient's vision impairment and history of falls suggest a moderate risk, particularly in outdoor environments.

5. **Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings**

- Assess and support safe outdoor mobility, considering the impact of binocular horizontal diplopia on depth perception and fall risk.
- Evaluate the home and community environment for potential hazards, recommending modifications to enhance safety.
- Educate the patient and his wife on safe mobility practices and the use of visual aids or temporary occlusion to manage diplopia.
- Monitor the patient's confidence and independence in daily activities, providing support as needed to maintain participation in meaningful roles.
