==============================
Case 066
==============================

1. **Patient Context**

The patient is a 71-year-old female presenting at a community optometry practice. She is diagnosed with medication-related ocular surface disease, which is modified by glaucoma and polypharmacy. Her referral context involved concerns about variable visual clarity and evening mobility hesitation. She is active, walking daily, and restricts her driving to daytime due to late-day visual disturbances.

2. **Key Vision Issues**

- **Fluctuating Visual Clarity:** The patient's vision fluctuates, particularly worsening in the evening due to ocular surface disease, likely related to preservative hypersensitivity and meibomian gland dysfunction.
- **Reduced Kerb and Step Confidence:** Difficulty with visual clarity affects her confidence in navigating steps and kerbs, particularly in the evening.
- **Contrast Sensitivity:** Reduced contrast sensitivity could affect her ability to discern objects from their backgrounds.

3. **Functional Impact**

- **Mobility and Falls:** Variable visual clarity can lead to hesitation and potential trips or falls, especially in low-light conditions or when navigating steps and kerbs. The patient reports uncertainty about kerbs and steps in the evening.
- **Community Mobility:** Evening restrictions on driving due to visual disturbances may affect her independence and community access.
- **Environmental Interaction:** Reduced contrast sensitivity and visual clarity may impact her ability to navigate and interact with her environment effectively, especially under challenging lighting conditions.

4. **Vision-Informed OT Considerations**

- **Self-care and Mobility Strategies:**
  - Assess whether fluctuating vision affects her ability to safely navigate stairs and kerbs, particularly in the evening.
  - Consider strategies to improve confidence and safety in low-light conditions, such as using visual aids or tactile cues.

- **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment:**
  - Evaluate the need for improved lighting in the home, particularly in areas frequently used in the evening, to enhance visibility and contrast.
  - Recommend consistent storage of walking aids or visual aids to ensure accessibility during periods of visual fluctuation.

- **Lighting and Contrast:**
  - Assess the need for modifications in home and community environments to improve contrast and reduce glare, particularly during evening activities.

- **Education and Support:**
  - Educate the patient on strategies to manage visual fluctuations, such as using lubricants before visually demanding tasks and planning activities during times of optimal vision.
  - Explore the potential need for support or supervision during evening activities to ensure safety.

- **Clinical Follow-up / Communication:**
  - Maintain communication with the ophthalmologist to monitor the effectiveness of any changes in medication or treatment plans on her visual function and overall safety.

**Falls Risk Level**

- **Moderate:** The vision-related fluctuation and reduced contrast sensitivity increase the risk of falls, particularly in low-light conditions or when navigating steps and kerbs. The patient's active lifestyle and current precautions suggest a moderate risk level.

5. **Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings**

- Assess the impact of fluctuating vision on mobility, particularly in navigating steps and kerbs during the evening.
- Evaluate and enhance environmental lighting and contrast to support safe navigation in low-light conditions.
- Educate the patient on managing visual fluctuations and planning activities during optimal vision periods to maintain independence and safety.
- Maintain communication with the ophthalmologist regarding treatment efficacy and necessary adjustments.
