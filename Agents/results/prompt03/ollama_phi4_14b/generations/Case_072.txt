==============================
Case 072
==============================

1. **Patient Context**

An 82-year-old female living in the community was referred from her GP to a community optometry practice due to increasing difficulty reading medication labels and a recent stumble in the kitchen. She has a primary diagnosis of advanced dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) with geographic atrophy in both eyes (OU) and a history of pseudophakia. She does not drive and has a history of hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, and osteoporosis.

2. **Key Vision Issues**

- **Central vision loss:** Advanced dry AMD with geographic atrophy OU is causing reduced central acuity, impacting tasks requiring detailed vision.
- **Impaired distance and near acuity:** Visual acuity of 6/60 OD and 6/30 OS/OU affects her ability to see clearly both at a distance and up close.
- **Reduced contrast sensitivity:** Challenges with tasks that require distinguishing between subtle differences in shading and light.
- **Compromised central fixation tasks:** Difficulty with tasks that require central vision, such as reading and recognizing faces.

3. **Functional Impact**

**Mobility and Falls**

- **Obstacle detection:** Central vision loss may affect her ability to detect obstacles directly ahead, as evidenced by her stumble in the kitchen.
- **Unfamiliar environments:** Increased difficulty navigating areas with complex or patterned surfaces.

**ADLs/IADLs**

- **Reading labels and medication management:** Difficulty reading fine print on medication labels due to reduced central acuity.
- **Meal preparation:** Central vision loss may affect her ability to safely prepare meals, particularly when working with sharp or hot objects.
- **Grooming and personal care:** Challenges with tasks that require detailed vision, such as applying makeup or shaving.

**Participation / Confidence / Roles**

- **Community access and social participation:** Potential reduction in confidence when engaging in social activities or shopping, due to difficulty reading signs or recognizing faces.
- **Independence:** May face challenges maintaining independence in daily activities that require central vision.

**Environmental Interaction**

- **Lighting and contrast:** Importance of adequate lighting and high-contrast environments to mitigate vision difficulties.
- **Clutter and patterned surfaces:** Risk of falls or trips in environments with patterned or cluttered surfaces, which may blend into the background.

4. **Vision-Informed OT Considerations**

**Self-care**

- Assess ability to perform grooming tasks independently and consider adaptive strategies or tools to assist with fine visual tasks.

**Household Management**

- Evaluate meal preparation routines for safety risks and consider task adaptations or non-visual strategies.

**Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**

- Assess kitchen lighting and contrast; recommend improvements for better visibility of household objects and surfaces.
- Consider simplifying or removing patterned surfaces that could blend with low-contrast items, reducing fall risk.

**Lighting and Contrast**

- Assess household lighting, focusing on brightness, glare control, and contrast to support her functional vision needs.

**Mobility Strategies**

- Consider strategies to improve detection of obstacles directly ahead, possibly involving Orientation & Mobility assessment.

**Task Adaptations**

- Assess how the patient identifies medication and food items before recommending specific adaptations or systems.

**Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies**

- Evaluate the need for visual enhancement tools such as task lighting and magnifiers, or non-visual strategies for tasks where central vision is crucial.

**Education and Support**

- Educate on the importance of consistent use of prescribed aids like bifocals and hand magnifiers.
- Review and reinforce strategies for safer navigation and task performance.

**Clinical Follow-up / Communication**

- Maintain communication with optometry and low-vision services to monitor vision changes and adapt strategies as needed.

5. **Falls Risk Level**

- **Moderate:** Central vision loss significantly impacts her ability to detect obstacles and navigate safely, with a history of a recent stumble, though no recurrent falls have been reported.

6. **Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings**

- Assess safety in meal preparation and identify task adaptations to reduce risk.
- Evaluate and enhance kitchen lighting and contrast to support better visibility.
- Consider strategies to improve obstacle detection directly ahead, potentially involving an Orientation & Mobility assessment.
- Educate on the use of visual aids and adaptive techniques for safe navigation and task performance.
