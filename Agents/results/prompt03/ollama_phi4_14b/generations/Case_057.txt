==============================
Case 057
==============================

1. **Patient Context**

The patient is an 84-year-old male living in the community, seen at an optometry practice following a recommendation from his rehabilitation team. He has a history of stroke, resulting in right homonymous hemianopia and bilateral cataracts. His medical history includes hypertension, arthritis, and hearing impairment. He uses bifocals full-time and has a history of recurrent falls post-stroke rehabilitation. He does not drive and is accompanied by his wife.

2. **Key Vision Issues**

- **Right Homonymous Hemianopia**: Likely affects the ability to detect obstacles and objects on the right side, impacting mobility and safety.
- **Bilateral Cataracts**: Contributes to reduced clarity and contrast sensitivity, affecting the ability to perform detailed visual tasks and potentially increasing fall risk.
- **Reduced Contrast Sensitivity**: May affect the ability to distinguish between objects and backgrounds, impacting activities such as reading labels or navigating uneven surfaces.
- **Use of Bifocals**: May impact depth perception and transitions between near and distance tasks, such as navigating steps or changes in lighting.

3. **Functional Impact**

**Mobility and Falls**:
- Obstacle detection and navigation may be compromised, particularly on the right side, increasing fall risk.
- Difficulty transitioning between different lighting environments may affect safe navigation in and out of shaded areas or buildings.
- Reduced visual field may affect the ability to safely cross roads or navigate busy environments.

**ADLs/IADLs**:
- Reading labels and instructions may be challenging due to reduced clarity and contrast.
- Meal preparation and cooking may be affected by impaired depth perception and visual field loss.
- Identification of household objects could be difficult, impacting independence.

**Participation / Confidence / Roles**:
- Confidence in mobility and independence may be reduced, potentially leading to social isolation or decreased community participation.
- Reliance on a support person for navigation in complex environments may be necessary.

**Environmental Interaction**:
- Increased risk of accidents due to glare and reduced contrast in various settings.
- Environmental clutter may pose a significant challenge for navigation and safety.

4. **Vision-Informed OT Considerations**

**A. Self-care**:
- Assess ability to manage personal care tasks independently, considering visual field loss and reduced contrast sensitivity.

**B. Household Management**:
- Evaluate the organization and consistency of household items to facilitate easier identification and access.

**C. Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**:
- Recommend high-contrast step edges and brighter entryway lighting to improve visibility.
- Suggest removing visual clutter and ensuring consistent furniture placement to reduce navigation challenges.

**D. Lighting and Contrast**:
- Assess household lighting for brightness, glare, and shadowing, and consider modifications to enhance visibility.

**E. Mobility Strategies**:
- Assess the need for scanning strategies to compensate for right-sided field loss.
- Consider referral to Orientation & Mobility (O&M) specialists for training in safe community mobility and use of mobility aids.

**F. Task Adaptations**:
- Assess meal preparation tasks and identify specific risk points that may require adaptations or assistance.

**G. Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies**:
- Consider the use of non-visual strategies or assistive technology to support reading and identification tasks.

**H. Education and Support**:
- Educate the patient and his wife on the importance of consistent use of visual aids and strategies to compensate for visual field loss.
- Discuss the potential benefits of low vision aids and mobility training.

**I. Support, Carers and Formal Services**:
- Assess the capacity of the patient’s wife to provide support and explore the need for additional formal services or supervision.

**J. Participation and Community Access**:
- Encourage activities that maintain community access and social participation, ensuring safety through appropriate strategies and supports.

**Falls Risk Level**: Moderate

**Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings**:
- Assess the impact of right homonymous hemianopia and bilateral cataracts on mobility and daily activities.
- Consider environmental modifications and task adaptations to enhance safety and independence.
- Evaluate the need for mobility training and the use of non-visual strategies to support navigation and task performance.
- Engage with the patient and his support system to ensure understanding and implementation of recommended strategies.
