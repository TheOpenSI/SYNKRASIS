==============================
Case 046
==============================

1. **Patient Context**

The patient is a 74-year-old male presenting at a community optometry practice following a GP referral. He has a history of hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, and atrial fibrillation and is on antihypertensive and anticoagulation medications. He reported a recent stumble on a low kerb and a decline in central vision in the left eye, leading to referral for a possible retinal cause. He uses separate distance and near spectacles and drives locally, although with reduced frequency due to vision concerns.

2. **Key Vision Issues**

- **Central Vision Loss OS**: The patient experiences blurred and patchy central vision in the left eye, affecting reading and detail recognition.
- **Reduced Contrast Sensitivity OS**: Likely affecting the ability to perceive differences in lighting and surface textures.
- **Reduced Near Visual Acuity OS**: Hinders tasks such as reading, identifying labels, and fine handiwork.
- **Branch Retinal Vein Occlusion with Macular Oedema OS**: Compromises central vision and depth perception, crucial for tasks like stair navigation and kerb detection.

3. **Functional Impact**

**Mobility and Falls**
- The central vision loss and reduced contrast sensitivity may affect obstacle detection, turning, and kerb negotiation, increasing fall risk, as evidenced by a recent stumble.
- Peripheral vision remains full, which may assist in mobility but does not fully compensate for central vision deficits.

**ADLs/IADLs**
- Reading and identifying objects, such as price labels, may be challenging, impacting shopping and medication management.
- Near vision tasks, like cooking or personal care, might require additional lighting or magnification aids.

**Participation / Confidence / Roles**
- Vision issues may affect confidence in driving, particularly in recognizing road signs and hazards, potentially leading to restricted driving.
- Social participation and independence may be impacted by reduced confidence in mobility and task performance.

**Environmental Interaction**
- Adjustments in lighting and contrast may be necessary to support visual function during daily tasks, like reading or cooking.

4. **Vision-Informed OT Considerations**

**Self-care and Household Management**
- Assess ability to perform tasks requiring central vision, such as grooming or reading labels, and consider adaptive strategies or aids.
- Evaluate medication management skills and explore tools like large-print labels or pill organizers.

**Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**
- Assess household lighting, including brightness, glare, and shadowing, to support visual tasks.
- Consider consistent storage and clear, clutter-free pathways to enhance navigation and safety.

**Mobility Strategies**
- Explore scanning strategies to compensate for central vision loss and assess the need for Orientation & Mobility training, particularly for safe community mobility and kerb negotiation.

**Task Adaptations**
- Assess meal-preparation tasks for safety risks, and consider adaptations such as using contrasting cutting boards or tactile markers on appliances.

**Education and Support**
- Educate the patient on strategies to optimize remaining vision, such as consistent lighting and magnification devices.
- Provide guidance on safe driving practices and explore alternative transportation options if driving restrictions become necessary.

**Support, Carers and Formal Services**
- Review current informal supports and assess carer capacity, ensuring they are informed about maintaining a predictable environment and supporting mobility practices.

**Participation and Community Access**
- Consider how vision loss may affect community access, social participation, and leisure activities, aiming to preserve independence and engagement safely.

**Falls Risk Level**
- Moderate: Due to the severity of central vision loss, demonstrated impact on mobility, and recent stumble, with vision expected to stabilize with treatment.

5. **Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings**

- Prioritize fall prevention strategies by enhancing home safety and mobility training.
- Address reading and near vision challenges with adaptive tools and environmental modifications.
- Support driving confidence by evaluating visual function and exploring transportation alternatives if necessary.
- Educate the patient and supports on optimizing visual function and safe task performance.
