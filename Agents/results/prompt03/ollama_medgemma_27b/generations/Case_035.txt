==============================
Case 035
==============================


1.  Patient Context

67-year-old male with primary open-angle glaucoma OU, progressed from glaucoma suspect. Routine review identified worsening obstacle awareness. Reports near-misses in dim hallways. No confirmed falls. Actively attends the gym. Hypertension and obstructive sleep apnoea. Drives daytime and occasionally at dusk.


2.  Key Vision Issues

*   **Peripheral Visual Field Loss:** Glaucomatous visual field defects OU (inferior OD, inferior nasal arcuate OS), confirmed by automated perimetry (MD –7.0 dB OD, MD –8.0 dB OS) and structural changes (OCT RNFL/GCA, optic nerve head rim thinning). This corresponds to a peripheral field/spatial awareness loss phenotype.
*   **Reduced Contrast Sensitivity:** Pelli-Robson contrast sensitivity is reduced (1.35 log units).
*   **Progressive Glaucoma:** Disease progression confirmed by worsening visual field defects, structural changes on OCT (RNFL/GCA), and optic nerve head changes (C/D ratio, rim thinning) since the last eye examination.
*   **Functional Impact:** Difficulty noticing floor-level hazards, especially in dim lighting. Reports less confidence stepping around low furniture. Near-misses reported in dim hallways.


3.  Functional Impact

*   **Mobility and Falls:** Peripheral field loss and reduced contrast sensitivity, particularly in dim light, impair obstacle detection and spatial awareness. This increases the risk of misjudging step heights (kerbs, stairs), bumping into objects, and tripping, especially in low-light conditions (e.g., dim hallways, dusk driving). The reported near-misses highlight this functional risk.
*   **Environmental Interaction:** Difficulty navigating environments with poor lighting or high visual clutter, particularly floor-level obstacles.
*   **Participation / Confidence / Roles:** Reduced confidence in mobility, especially in challenging visual conditions (dim light, dusk). May lead to activity avoidance or increased reliance on others. Active gym attendance may be impacted by visual field deficits or fear of falls.
*   **Driving:** Glaucoma progression and peripheral field loss may impact driving safety, especially in low light or complex environments. The patient drives at dusk, which may increase risk.


4.  Vision-Informed OT Considerations

*   **Mobility Strategies:**
    *   Assess current mobility strategies in different environments (home, gym, community) and lighting conditions.
    *   Consider O&M referral for specialist assessment of safe mobility techniques, including head turning, scanning strategies, and use of visual cues.
    *   Discuss safe navigation techniques for dim environments and potential hazards.
    *   Assess the need for a mobility aid (e.g., white cane) for enhanced obstacle detection, particularly in challenging conditions.
*   **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment:**
    *   Assess the home environment for potential hazards related to peripheral field loss and reduced contrast sensitivity (e.g., floor clutter, poor lighting, lack of contrast between steps/edges and background).
    *   Recommend specific modifications: improved low-level lighting (e.g., nightlights in hallways, motion-sensor lights), removal of clutter, contrasting tape on step edges, consistent furniture placement.
*   **Lighting and Contrast:**
    *   Assess lighting levels and glare in the home and gym.
    *   Recommend adjustments to lighting to enhance visibility and reduce glare/shadowing.
    *   Discuss the use of portable task lighting.
    *   Consider high-contrast objects or markings for key items.
*   **Task Adaptations:**
    *   Assess tasks performed in the gym and identify potential visual challenges (e.g., spatial awareness, depth perception, obstacle avoidance).
    *   Discuss modifications to gym activities or environment as needed.
*   **Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies:**
    *   Consider non-visual compensatory strategies (e.g., auditory cues, tactile exploration).
    *   Assess need for low vision aids (magnification) for specific tasks if central vision becomes affected (not currently indicated but may progress).
*   **Education and Support:**
    *   Educate the patient and family/support network about the impact of peripheral vision loss and reduced contrast sensitivity on functional tasks and safety.
    *   Discuss strategies to maintain independence and safety.
    *   Discuss the importance of consistent spectacle wear.
    *   Discuss the implications for driving and potential need for assessment or cessation.
*   **Clinical Follow-up / Communication:**
    *   Maintain communication with the ophthalmology team regarding the patient's functional adaptation and any new challenges.
    *   Review the effectiveness of environmental modifications and mobility strategies.
    *   Monitor for changes in functional abilities or confidence.

*   **Falls Risk:**
    *   Assess the risk of falls related to vision impairment, particularly in the home and during mobility activities.
    *   Consider referral to a falls prevention program or specialist assessment if the risk is high.

*   **Participation and Community Access:**
    *   Discuss how vision loss might affect participation in preferred activities (gym, driving, community activities).
    *   Explore strategies to maintain engagement and independence.

5.  Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings

*   The patient has progressive peripheral vision loss and reduced contrast sensitivity, increasing the risk of falls and mobility challenges, especially in dim lighting.
*   Functional difficulties are already evident (near-misses, reduced confidence with low obstacles).
*   OT assessment should focus on environmental safety, mobility strategies, and compensatory techniques to maintain safe participation in daily activities and valued roles (e.g., gym attendance, driving).
*   Education and support are needed for the patient and potentially their support network regarding visual impairment and safety strategies.
