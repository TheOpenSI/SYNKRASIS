==============================
Case 057
==============================


1. Patient Context

84-year-old male, living in the community, referred after recurrent falls following stroke rehabilitation. He has a history of stroke, hearing impairment, and recurrent falls. Key visual impairments are right homonymous hemianopia (from stroke ~2 years ago) and visually significant bilateral cataracts (worsening over ~1 year). Family reports functional difficulties related to both field loss (missing objects on the right) and reduced clarity/glare.

2. Key Vision Issues

*   **Right homonymous hemianopia:** Significant visual field loss on the right side in both eyes, likely impacting obstacle detection, navigation, and spatial awareness, particularly when turning or in visually complex environments.
*   **Bilateral cataracts:** Moderate nuclear sclerosis and cortical spokes OU, leading to reduced visual acuity (6/24 best corrected), significantly reduced contrast sensitivity (1.05 log units), glare, and overall visual blur. This impacts detail vision, especially in varying lighting conditions (e.g., stepping from bright sunlight).
*   **Reduced contrast sensitivity:** Significantly impaired contrast sensitivity OU (1.05 log units) affects the ability to distinguish objects from backgrounds, especially in low light or glare, increasing fall risk and difficulty with tasks requiring visual discrimination.
*   **Reduced visual acuity:** Reduced best-corrected distance VA (OD 6/36, OS 6/24) and near VA (N16 OU @40cm) impacts tasks requiring visual detail, such as reading, medication management, and identifying objects.
*   **Hearing Impairment:** Hearing impairment is present, which may affect communication, environmental awareness, and compensatory strategies for vision loss.

3. Functional Impact

*   **Mobility and Falls:** The combination of right hemianopia and reduced acuity/contrast sensitivity likely contributes significantly to falls risk. He has already experienced falls related to turning (doorways) and lighting transitions (sunlight to shade), suggesting difficulty detecting obstacles and changes in environment, especially on the right side and in conditions of low contrast or glare. The hearing impairment may compound difficulty in identifying hazards.
*   **ADLs/IADLs:** Reduced visual acuity and contrast sensitivity likely impact tasks requiring visual detail and discrimination, such as medication management, meal preparation, reading labels, and managing household tasks. The hemianopia may affect tasks requiring peripheral vision, like locating items or navigating the environment.
*   **Environmental Interaction:** Glare (from cataracts) and reduced contrast sensitivity make it difficult to navigate environments with varying lighting conditions, shadows, or poor contrast. The hemianopia makes it difficult to detect hazards on the right side.
*   **Participation:** The visual impairments and falls history may affect confidence and participation in community activities, potentially leading to activity restriction or reliance on others.

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations

*   **Falls Risk Assessment:** A comprehensive falls risk assessment is warranted, considering both visual and non-visual factors (stroke history, arthritis, hearing impairment, antihypertensive medication).
*   **Environmental Modifications:**
    *   Assess the home environment for lighting (brightness, glare, shadows), contrast (step edges, floor surfaces, object visibility), and potential obstacles, particularly on the right side.
    *   Consider modifications to improve lighting (e.g., brighter bulbs, task lighting), enhance contrast (e.g., high-contrast tape on steps), and ensure clear pathways. The recommendation for brighter entryway lighting and high-contrast step edges should be investigated and implemented as appropriate.
    *   Assess the feasibility of removing bifocals for stair use, considering the patient's preference and ability to manage without them.
*   **Task Adaptations:**
    *   Assess specific ADL/IADL tasks (e.g., medication management, meal preparation, grooming) to identify difficulties related to acuity, contrast sensitivity, and field loss.
    *   Consider strategies to compensate for hemianopia, such as scanning techniques (already recommended by rehab team) and positioning objects to be within the remaining visual field.
    *   Consider strategies to manage reduced contrast sensitivity and glare, such as using brighter lighting, adjusting task position relative to light sources, and using high-contrast materials.
*   **Assistive Technology/Non-visual Strategies:**
    *   Explore the potential benefits of low vision aids, as recommended by Vision Australia/Guide Dogs Australia. This may include magnification devices, lighting aids, or electronic devices.
    *   Assess the need for a mobility cane, considering the patient's mobility confidence, falls history, and environmental navigation challenges.
    *   Consider strategies that do not rely solely on vision, such as tactile cues or auditory aids.
*   **Education and Support:**
    *   Educate the patient and his wife about the visual impairments, their functional implications, and safety strategies.
    *   Reinforce the importance of consistent spectacle use (if appropriate and tolerated).
    *   Discuss strategies for managing visually complex environments and seeking assistance when needed.
    *   Provide information about community resources and support services.
*   **Mobility Strategies:**
    *   Assess current mobility strategies and practice scanning techniques for the visual field defect.
    *   Consider referral to Orientation & Mobility (O&M) services for specialised assessment and training in safe community mobility, including strategies for navigating with hemianopia and low vision. This is particularly relevant given the stroke history, falls, and reported difficulties.
*   **Clinical Follow-up/Communication:** Maintain communication with the optometry, rehabilitation physician, and other members of the multidisciplinary team to coordinate care and monitor progress. Discuss the potential impact of cataract surgery (when/if it occurs) on function.

5. Falls Risk Level

High: The patient has multiple significant falls-relevant visual impairments (hemianopia, low contrast sensitivity, reduced acuity), a history of recurrent falls, and environmental risk factors (lighting transitions). These factors significantly increase his risk of future falls.

6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings

*   Significant risk of falls due to combined impact of right hemianopia, reduced acuity, and poor contrast sensitivity.
*   Need for comprehensive environmental assessment and modification to enhance safety and navigation, particularly regarding lighting, contrast, and obstacles on the right side.
*   Requirement for strategies to compensate for visual field loss and reduced visual function during ADLs/IADLs and community mobility.
*   Potential benefit from low vision aids, mobility cane, and/or O&M training to improve independence and safety.
