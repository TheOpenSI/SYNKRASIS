==============================
Case 083
==============================


1. Patient Context

78-year-old female with known normal-tension glaucoma, followed in shared care. She has cardiovascular disease and a family history of glaucoma. She is experiencing increasing difficulty with peripheral vision, specifically noticing objects approaching from the side and reporting side-collision risks (clipping doorframes, laundry baskets). She remains independent at home but is more cautious in cluttered environments. She drives short distances.

2. Key Vision Issues

*   **Peripheral visual field loss:** Bilateral inferior and nasal (OD) / inferior (OS) field loss, confirmed by visual field testing (VFI), consistent with glaucoma progression.
*   **Reduced contrast sensitivity:** Pelli-Robson reduced to 1.35 log units.
*   **Advanced glaucomatous structural damage:** RNFL thinning (OCT, GCA) and optic nerve cupping (C/D 0.85 OD, 0.75 OS) with progression since the last exam.
*   **Impact on spatial awareness:** Patient reports difficulty detecting objects approaching from the side and increased caution in cluttered environments and when turning.
*   **Potential driving impairment:** Advised to refrain from driving pending Esterman test due to visual field loss.

3. Functional Impact

*   **Mobility and Falls:** Peripheral field loss and reduced contrast sensitivity increase risk during ambulation, particularly turning, navigating hallways, detecting obstacles from the side, and potential for side collisions (doorframes, laundry baskets). This may increase difficulty negotiating stairs, kerbs, and uneven surfaces, especially in low light or cluttered environments. The patient's reported clipping of objects suggests an immediate functional impact on safety.
*   **ADLs/IADLs:** Reduced peripheral vision may affect tasks requiring awareness of objects approaching from the side, such as reaching for items, transferring objects, or navigating kitchen spaces. Reduced contrast sensitivity may affect identifying objects, reading labels, or detecting changes in surface texture.
*   **Participation/Confidence:** Increased caution in cluttered environments and side-collision history may reduce confidence in mobility, potentially leading to activity restriction or avoidance of certain environments. The advice to refrain from driving pending testing suggests potential impact on community access and independence.
*   **Environmental Interaction:** The patient is already aware of the need for better lighting (hallways) and decluttering, indicating an understanding of how the environment interacts with her vision.

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations

*   **Assessment:** Assess the impact of peripheral vision loss and reduced contrast sensitivity on specific mobility tasks, such as turning, navigating doorways and hallways, detecting obstacles, and managing stairs/kerbs. Assess the patient's current strategies for compensating for vision loss (e.g., head turning, scanning). Assess the home environment for clutter, lighting, contrast, and potential hazards. Assess the impact of reduced contrast sensitivity on specific tasks like identifying objects, reading, or medication management. Assess the patient's current level of activity participation and confidence in community mobility.
*   **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment:** The optometry plan already includes advice on decluttering and improved hallway lighting. OT can assess the effectiveness of these modifications or suggest further adjustments, such as brighter task lighting, improved contrast on edges (e.g., step markings), or consistent placement of frequently used items. Assess the safety of the patient's home environment, particularly pathways and potential obstacles.
*   **Lighting and Contrast:** Evaluate ambient and task lighting levels throughout the home. Assess for glare or shadows that might further reduce visibility. Consider high-contrast aids or modifications (e.g., contrasting tape on steps).
*   **Mobility Strategies:** Assess current mobility strategies. Consider teaching compensatory strategies like consistent head turning or scanning techniques, especially before turning corners or entering new environments. Explore the use of a mobility aid (e.g., cane) if appropriate, considering the patient's preferences and functional needs.
*   **Task Adaptations:** Evaluate how the patient performs tasks requiring peripheral awareness (e.g., meal preparation, transferring items). Suggest adaptations like placing items in consistent locations or using tactile cues.
*   **Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies:** Explore the use of assistive technology like talking devices (e.g., clocks, medication reminders) or apps that can aid navigation or object identification. Consider non-visual compensatory strategies like using auditory cues or asking for assistance.
*   **Education and Support:** Educate the patient and any support person on the nature of the vision loss, its functional implications, and strategies for safe ambulation and task performance. Discuss the impact of driving cessation and explore alternative transportation options.
*   **Clinical Follow-up / Communication:** Liaise with the ophthalmologist regarding the management plan, including potential surgical interventions and the results of the Esterman test. Follow up to assess the effectiveness of implemented strategies and environmental modifications.
*   **Community Mobility:** Given the driving restriction and potential impact on community access, consider assessment of community mobility skills and strategies, potentially involving an Orientation & Mobility (O&M) specialist if needed.

5. Falls Risk Level

High: The combination of progressive peripheral field loss, reduced contrast sensitivity, reported side collisions, and advanced structural damage significantly increases the risk of falls and mobility-related incidents.

6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings

*   Significant peripheral vision loss impacts mobility safety and spatial awareness, requiring environmental assessment, compensatory strategies, and potentially O&M input.
*   Reduced contrast sensitivity further compounds mobility and task difficulties, necessitating lighting and contrast adjustments.
*   The need to cease driving pending testing highlights the functional impact on independence and requires exploration of alternative transport and community access strategies.
*   Assessment of current functional strategies and environmental safety is crucial to maximise independence and minimise falls risk.
