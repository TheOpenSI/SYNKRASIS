==============================
Case 032
==============================


1.  Patient Context

80-year-old male with type 2 diabetes, hypertension, and hypercholesterolaemia, referred by GP after recurrent falls outdoors. He reports intermittent double vision (diplopia) and difficulty judging kerb height, and has stopped driving. The primary vision issue is a recent onset left sixth nerve palsy causing binocular horizontal diplopia, worse at distance and in left gaze, leading to functional loss of stereopsis. He uses bifocals full-time.

2.  Key Vision Issues

*   **Recent onset left sixth nerve palsy:** Causes horizontal diplopia, particularly at distance and in left gaze, limiting binocular function.
*   **Loss of functional stereopsis:** Due to manifest deviation, impairing depth perception, especially outdoors.
*   **Distance visual acuity:** Reduced to 6/12 OU at 6m (best corrected 6/12), possibly compounded by diplopia and stereopsis loss.
*   **Bifocal use:** May contribute to difficulties with depth perception and negotiation of changes in height (e.g., kerbs) due to induced vertical separation of images.
*   **Mildly reduced contrast sensitivity:** May affect obstacle detection, especially outdoors or in variable lighting.

3.  Functional Impact

*   **Mobility and Falls:**
    *   Recurrent falls outdoors, particularly on uneven footpaths and stepping off kerbs, likely due to diplopia and loss of depth perception.
    *   Difficulty judging kerb height and navigating uneven surfaces.
    *   Closing one eye when crossing car parks suggests visual reliance and difficulty with binocular tasks in complex environments.
    *   Feeling less steady when stepping off kerbs indicates impaired depth perception and postural instability related to visual input.
    *   Increased risk of falls during ambulation, especially outdoors and in complex environments.
*   **ADLs/IADLs:**
    *   Difficulty with tasks requiring depth perception or binocular vision (e.g., pouring, reaching for objects, navigating doorways).
    *   Potential difficulty identifying objects at distance due to diplopia.
    *   Reduced ability to safely negotiate stairs or steps.
*   **Participation / Confidence / Roles:**
    *   Stopped driving due to frequent diplopia, impacting community access and independence.
    *   Reduced walking outside alone, potentially leading to social isolation or deconditioning.
    *   Likely reduced confidence in navigating outdoor environments.
*   **Environmental Interaction:**
    *   Difficulty with outdoor environments (uneven paths, kerbs) due to loss of depth perception and diplopia.
    *   Potential difficulty adapting to variable lighting conditions outdoors, possibly exacerbated by reduced contrast sensitivity.

4.  Vision-Informed OT Considerations

*   **Mobility Strategies:**
    *   Assess current mobility strategies (e.g., closing one eye, reduced outdoor walking) and their effectiveness/limitations.
    *   Consider strategies to enhance obstacle detection and kerb negotiation (e.g., using a cane, consistent route planning, identifying landmarks).
    *   Referral to Orientation & Mobility (O&M) specialist may be appropriate for assessment of community mobility skills and training in safe travel techniques with impaired binocular vision/depth perception.
*   **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment:**
    *   Assess home environment for fall risks, particularly on stairs, changes in floor level, and clutter.
    *   Ensure adequate lighting, especially in areas where depth perception is critical (e.g., stairs, doorways).
    *   Ensure clear pathways and removal of obstacles.
*   **Task Adaptations:**
    *   Assess specific tasks affected by diplopia and loss of depth perception (e.g., meal preparation, medication management, transfers).
    *   Consider task adaptations or assistive devices to compensate for visual deficits (e.g., using tactile cues, consistent item placement).
*   **Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies:**
    *   Assess the need for a mobility aid (e.g., cane) for enhanced obstacle detection and balance.
    *   Explore the use of non-visual cues (e.g., auditory signals, tactile markers) for navigation.
*   **Education and Support:**
    *   Educate the patient and wife about the visual condition, its impact on function, and strategies for safe mobility and daily tasks.
    *   Advise on safe ambulation techniques (e.g., scanning, maintaining a clear path, using handrails).
    *   Discuss the importance of consistent spectacle use (if prescribed).
    *   Provide information on low-vision services and support groups.
    *   Discuss implications of driving cessation and explore alternative transport options.
*   **Clinical Follow-up / Communication:**
    *   Communicate with the GP and optometry regarding OT assessment findings and recommendations.
    *   Monitor the effectiveness of implemented strategies and make adjustments as needed.
    *   Review environmental safety and mobility skills periodically.

5.  Falls Risk Level

High: Due to recent onset diplopia, loss of functional stereopsis, recurrent outdoor falls, difficulties with kerb negotiation, and reduced mobility confidence. The falls history and specific functional deficits related to binocular vision significantly increase falls risk.

6.  Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings

*   Significant impairment to mobility and community participation due to diplopia and loss of depth perception, leading to recurrent falls.
*   Need for assessment of mobility strategies, environmental safety, and potential need for O&M training.
*   Need for education and support regarding safe mobility techniques and adaptation to visual limitations.
*   Need to address the impact of driving cessation on community access and independence.
