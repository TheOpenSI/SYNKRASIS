==============================
Case 064
==============================


1. Patient Context

82-year-old male in rehabilitation outpatient care following a stroke and a fall. He has a history of stroke, atrial fibrillation, and hypertension, is on anticoagulation and antihypertensives, and uses bifocal spectacles. He is currently undergoing OT and physiotherapy for mobility retraining. He has left homonymous hemianopia following a right occipital stroke three months prior, which has led to a fall and collisions.

2. Key Vision Issues

*   **Left Homonymous Hemianopia:** Stroke-related visual field loss affecting the left side of both eyes.
*   **Reduced Contrast Sensitivity:** Impaired ability to distinguish objects from their background, especially in low light or complex environments.
*   **Difficulty with Bifocals:** Habitual vision is 6/9 at 6m with bifocals, may impact distance viewing in rehabilitation.
*   **Difficulty with Near Vision:** N8 OU at 40cm, improves to N6 OU with add, suggesting potential difficulty with tasks requiring fine detail at near.
*   **Exophoria at Near:** 4 prism dioptres exophoria at near, may contribute to eye strain or difficulty with near tasks.

3. Functional Impact

*   **Mobility and Falls:**
    *   Difficulty navigating doorways and turning, leading to collisions and a fall.
    *   Impaired obstacle detection on the left side.
    *   Difficulty navigating crowded spaces.
    *   Increased risk of falls, particularly when turning or entering/exiting spaces.
*   **ADLs/IADLs:**
    *   Difficulty locating the start of lines when reading.
    *   Fatigue in busy environments may affect participation in community activities or ability to perform tasks requiring sustained visual attention.
    *   Difficulty identifying objects or features on the left side may impact tasks like medication identification, meal preparation, or locating items.
*   **Participation / Confidence / Roles:**
    *   May impact confidence in community mobility and participation in social activities.
    *   May increase reliance on others for mobility and task completion.
    *   May lead to activity avoidance due to fear of falls or collisions.
*   **Environmental Interaction:**
    *   Difficulty with spatial awareness, particularly regarding the left side of the environment.
    *   Busy environments are tiring, suggesting potential issues with visual clutter, lighting, or contrast.

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations

*   **Mobility Strategies:**
    *   Assess current compensatory scanning strategies (e.g., head turning) and their effectiveness.
    *   Teach and practice systematic scanning techniques for navigating doorways, turning, and crowded environments.
    *   Assess the need for and train in the use of a mobility cane for obstacle detection and enhanced awareness.
    *   Consider Orientation & Mobility (O&M) assessment and training for safe community travel strategies, including route planning and hazard identification.
*   **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment:**
    *   Assess the home environment for potential hazards, particularly on the left side (e.g., furniture placement, steps, clutter).
    *   Recommend environmental modifications to improve safety, such as rearranging furniture to create clear pathways, using contrasting colours for step edges or doorways, and ensuring adequate lighting, especially in areas where he has difficulty.
*   **Task Adaptations:**
    *   Assess reading strategies and consider adaptations like using a line tracker, larger print, or specific lighting.
    *   Assess medication management tasks and consider strategies to ensure correct identification and dosage.
    *   Review meal preparation tasks and identify potential safety risks (e.g., reaching for items, using sharp objects) related to the visual field loss.
*   **Education and Support:**
    *   Educate the patient and family/support people about the visual field loss, its implications for safety and function, and strategies for managing it.
    *   Provide information about low vision identification badges and their use in the community.
    *   Discuss the importance of consistent spectacle wear and proper bifocal use.
    *   Communicate findings to the rehabilitation team (OT, PT, physician) to ensure a coordinated approach.
    *   Assess support needs and capacity of informal supports (family, friends) to implement strategies.
*   **Lighting and Contrast:**
    *   Assess household lighting, including brightness, glare, and shadows, particularly in areas where he navigates (hallways, doorways).
    *   Consider strategies to improve contrast (e.g., contrasting tape on step edges, brightly coloured items).

5. Falls Risk Level

Moderate: The patient has a documented fall related to the visual field defect and ongoing difficulties with mobility tasks (doorways, turning) despite rehabilitation. The impairment is permanent and significantly impacts mobility.

6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings

*   Significant functional impact of left homonymous hemianopia on mobility and safety, evidenced by falls and collisions.
*   Need for targeted mobility training and compensatory strategies (scanning, cane use, O&M referral).
*   Requirement for environmental assessment and modification to reduce hazards and enhance safety.
*   Potential impact on ADLs (reading, medication) and participation, requiring task adaptation and support strategies.
