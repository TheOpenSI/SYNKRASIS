==============================
Case 064
==============================

1. Patient Context

- 82-year-old male, rehabilitation outpatient following a right occipital stroke.
- Primary visual impairment: left homonymous hemianopia (left-field loss in both eyes); central acuity relatively preserved.
- History of mobility challenges and a fall shortly after discharge, occurring when turning into a doorway; reported left-sided doorframe collisions and difficulty locating the start of lines when reading.
- Ocular status: pseudophakia OU; full motility; no diplopia; contrast sensitivity reduced; bifocal spectacles in use; mild meibomian gland dysfunction; macula with age-related changes; no RAPD.
- Medical: atrial fibrillation, hypertension; on anticoagulation and antihypertensives.
- Functional engagement: currently undergoing OT and physiotherapy for mobility retraining; not driving.
- Vision-related complaints: “I forget to look far enough to the left”; busy environments tiring.
- Care setting and referral context: rehabilitation outpatient clinic; assessment during rehabilitation after stroke-related admission; single fall occurred after discharge.

2. Key Vision Issues

- Left homonymous hemianopia from stroke: left-field loss with preserved central acuity, contributing to missed left-sided cues, left doorway collisions, and slower detection of hazards when turning or navigating in crowded or unfamiliar spaces.
- Reduced contrast sensitivity (Pelli-Robson 1.35 log units at 1 m): decreased ability to distinguish edges/obstacles in cluttered or low-contrast environments, increasing risk during mobility and doorway/edge negotiations.
- Reading/line-location challenges: difficulty locating the start of lines, which may reflect field loss interacting with scanning demands during near tasks.
- Mobility-related functional risk in real-world settings: prior fall when turning into a doorway; left-side collisions with doorframes indicate practical safety risks during ambulation and orientation.
- Eye-wear status: bifocal spectacles used; potential implications for scanning, depth cues, and transition between distances during mobility tasks; no diplopia.
- Ocular health is otherwise stable for functional purposes; no necrosis or retinal findings noted; macular changes noted but not functionally limiting beyond contrast and field loss in this context.

3. Functional Impact

Falls Risk and Mobility
- Left-field deficit likely reduces detection of obstacles and edge cues on the left during ambulation, turning, and doorway navigation.
- History of a fall related to turning into a doorway and documented left-doorframe collisions in busy environments; vulnerability in home and rehabilitation settings, especially in cluttered or unfamiliar spaces.
- Magnitude of impairment may be amplified by reduced contrast sensitivity and fatigue in busy environments.

ADLs/IADLs
- Reading tasks (e.g., locating start of lines, forms) may be affected by field loss and scanning demands; near-vision tasks could be impacted, with possible slower information extraction in certain layouts.

Participation/Confidence
- Busy environments are tiring and may reduce participation in community/school/clinic activities; potential impact on independence and participation in broader activities if adaptations are not in place.

Environmental Interaction
- Cluttered spaces and doorway navigation require heightened left-field awareness; lighting and contrast around key environmental features (doorways, edges) influence safety.

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations

A. ASSESS BEFORE PRESCRIBING
- Assess compensatory scanning strategies and left-field awareness during functional mobility tasks (e.g., walking in a straight corridor, turning at doorways, navigating crowded spaces).
- Evaluate reading tasks that involve lines and text near the left side to quantify practical impact on near tasks.
- Determine current use and adequacy of bifocals for mobility and whether wear consistency aligns with safety goals.

B. ENVIRONMENTAL ASSESSMENT AND MODIFICATION
- Review home and rehab environments for left-field hazards: doorways, thresholds, clutter, and edge visibility.
- Recommend environmental cues and clear pathways (e.g., keep left-side route free of obstacles, doorways clearly delineated, consistent layouts).
- Consider high-contrast, non-glare lighting around transition areas (doorways, thresholds, reading spaces) and reduce shadows at edges that may be misinterpreted as obstacles.
- Where feasible, implement simple adjustments rather than major renovations (e.g., floor-edge markings, doorway edge contrast, uncluttered routes).

C. LIGHTING AND CONTRAST
- Ensure even illumination with glare control; maximize contrast between objects and backgrounds important for left-field detection (e.g., door frames, step edges). Adjust lighting to minimize shadow misperceptions during scanning to the left.

D. MOBILITY AND ORIENTATION & MOBILITY (O&M) RELEVANCE
- Consider referral to Orientation & Mobility (O&M) for tailored safe community mobility and left-field scanning strategies; avoid prescriptive, one-size-fits-all routines and instead support goal-directed, individualized training.
- Emphasize goals such as safe navigation through doorways, improved detection of left-side hazards, and maintenance of independence within current mobility limits.

E. VISUAL + NON-VISUAL COMPENSATION / ASSISTIVE TECHNOLOGY
- Mobility cane: discuss potential for cane use to augment left-side detection during ambulation; provide training and safety coaching if pursued.
- Identification and labeling aids (low-vision identification badge) to support navigation in crowded spaces or unfamiliar environments.
- Non-visual strategies (verbal prompts, environmental cues) and consistent routines to support safe orientation.

F. TASK ADAPTATION
- For reading tasks: use left-to-right text organization, larger print, or digital aids when feasible; ensure seating/contrast supports are in place to ease line tracking.
- In daily mobility tasks, emphasize consistent path planning with explicit left-field scanning before turning or crossing thresholds.

G. SUPPORT, CARERS AND FORMAL SERVICES
- Educate rehabilitation team and caregivers about left-field loss, strategies to maintain safe environments, and consistent cueing for left-space awareness.
- Consider supervision needs during transitional activities (e.g., home safety checks, doorway navigation) and plan for safe practice in real-world settings.

H. PARTICIPATION AND COMMUNITY ACCESS
- Prioritize safe community participation by balancing independence with environmental adaptations and support in unfamiliar settings.
- Explore alternatives to risky routes/activities (e.g., avoiding high-traffic or poorly lit areas during times of poorer vision) while maintaining participation where feasible.

I. DRIVING / LICENSING / TRANSPORT
- Driving remains inappropriate without formal medical and visual field clearance; support planning for alternative transportation and community access as needed.

J. EYEWEAR FUNCTION
- Acknowledge bifocals in mobility and scanning; discuss with optometry whether distance vs. near use impacts left-field scanning and edge detection, while avoiding changes to ophthalmic management within OT scope.
- Encourage consistent spectacle use during mobility and when practicing compensatory strategies to ensure visual inputs are stable.

K. LOW VISION SERVICE
- If existing low-vision input is planned or available, coordinate with the eye-care team; otherwise, consider referral to low-vision rehabilitation for longer-term functional rehabilitation while OT focuses on compensatory strategies and safety within current acuity.

L. BEHAVIORAL ADAPTATION
- Monitor for inconsistent scanning or habitual environment-related risks; use environmental cues and routine-building to support consistent left-field scanning and safer navigation.

N. FEASIBILITY
- Recommendations are framed with rehabilitation setting constraints in mind; implement practical, low-risk environmental and routine modifications first; escalate to O&M or low-vision services as appropriate and feasible.

O. PRIORITISATION
- Safety-critical risk related to left-field scanning and doorway navigation is the priority, followed by reading-related near tasks and participation in busy environments. All recommendations tie back to documented findings or clearly identified functional needs.

Falls Risk Level: Moderate
- Based on left-field hemianopia with documented left-side collisions at doorframes and a fall related to turning into a doorway, compounded by age and vascular risk, the patient has a meaningful but not solely vision-driven risk profile requiring targeted compensatory and environmental strategies.

Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings

- Primary priorities are to mitigate left-field mobility risks through compensatory scanning, environmental cueing, and possibly cane use, while preserving independence in home and rehab settings.
- Immediate assessment should focus on left-sided obstacle detection, doorways, and turning maneuvers, plus reading-related line-tracking tasks to inform task adaptations.
- Environmental modifications should target left-field safety: clear pathways, high-contrast doorway cues, consistent layouts, and glare/shadow reduction to aid edge detection.
- Consider referral to O&M for individualized mobility strategies tailored to left-hemianopic field loss; ensure the approach is person-centered and avoids generic routines.
- Non-visual strategies and staff education are essential to support safe participation in crowded or unfamiliar environments; ensure carers and rehab staff are informed about left-field loss and cueing practices.
- Eyewear considerations should be acknowledged but not altered without formal optometric input; emphasize consistent use during mobility to maintain stable visual input.
- If not already in place, coordination with low-vision services could provide longer-term optimization, while OT continues to focus on compensatory strategies and safety within the patient’s current functional level.

Notes for clinical handover
- Document and communicate the left HH field loss and reduced contrast sensitivity as key contributors to mobility risk.
- Highlight the need for left-side environmental cueing, doorway safety strategies, and possible O&M consultation.
- Convey the plan to continue OT-led compensatory scanning and consider cane use or ID badge for safety and independence in indoor environments.
