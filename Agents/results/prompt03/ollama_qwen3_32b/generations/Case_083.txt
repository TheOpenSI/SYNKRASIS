==============================  
Case 083  
==============================  

### 1. Patient Context  
78-year-old female with progressing **normal-tension glaucoma OU**, presenting with **inferior peripheral field loss** and **reduced spatial awareness**. Reports side-collisions with doorframes and laundry baskets in cluttered environments. Uses separate distance spectacles and readers. **No recent falls**, but increased caution in mobility. **Driving restricted to short daytime distances** per optometry advice.  

---

### 2. Key Vision Issues  
- **Progressive peripheral field loss (inferior/inferonasal)**: Likely contributes to side-collision incidents and reduced spatial awareness, particularly during turning or in cluttered spaces.  
- **Reduced contrast sensitivity (1.35 log units)**: May affect object detection in low-contrast environments (e.g., locating laundry baskets, identifying step edges).  
- **Binocular alignment (4 XP near)**: May impact near-task depth judgment (e.g., pouring, reaching).  

---

### 3. Functional Impact  

**Mobility and Falls**  
- **Peripheral field loss** increases risk of side-collisions with stationary or moving objects (e.g., doorframes, furniture).  
- **Cluttered environments** (e.g., hallways, laundry areas) heighten collision risk due to reduced spatial awareness.  
- **Contrast sensitivity reduction** may impair detection of low-contrast obstacles (e.g., edges of rugs, laundry baskets).  

**ADLs/IADLs**  
- **Meal preparation/pouring**: Reduced depth judgment (binocular alignment) may affect safety with hot liquids or sharp tools.  
- **Medication management**: Potential difficulty identifying pill bottles or labels in low-contrast settings.  
- **Reading/phone use**: Reduced contrast sensitivity may strain visual tasks in dim lighting.  

**Participation / Confidence**  
- **Driving restriction** (pending Esterman test) may reduce independence and community access.  
- **Cautious mobility** may lower confidence, potentially leading to deconditioning or social isolation.  

**Environmental Interaction**  
- **Clutter and furniture placement** in travel paths may exacerbate spatial awareness deficits.  
- **Hallway lighting** and **step-edge contrast** could benefit from assessment to reduce collision risk.  

---

### 4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations  

**A. Self-care**  
- **Medication management**: Assess whether reduced contrast sensitivity or field loss affects label identification or pill bottle selection. Consider high-contrast labels or tactile markers.  
- **Bathing/showering**: Evaluate for risk of missing step edges (inferior field loss) or low-contrast thresholds. Recommend increased contrast or handrails.  

**B. Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**  
- **Decluttering**: Prioritize open travel paths in hallways and laundry areas to reduce collision risk.  
- **Lighting**: Optimize hallway and entryway lighting to enhance peripheral object detection. Avoid glare that obscures low-contrast objects.  
- **Contrast enhancement**: Ensure high-contrast between steps, laundry baskets, and walls. Consider tactile indicators for step edges.  

**C. Mobility Strategies**  
- **Scanning techniques**: Teach deliberate scanning before turning in hallways or approaching cluttered areas. Use a hand or cane to "sweep" for obstacles.  
- **Orientation & Mobility (O&M) referral**: Consider if side-collision incidents persist despite environmental modifications. Focus on safe turning strategies and cluttered-space navigation.  

**D. Task Adaptations**  
- **Meal preparation**: Assess pouring safety (binocular alignment impact). Recommend stable surfaces and non-slip mats.  
- **Reading/phone use**: Trial larger print, text-to-speech, or high-contrast screens to address reduced contrast sensitivity.  

**E. Education and Support**  
- **Patient education**: Reinforce awareness of peripheral field loss and its impact on spatial navigation. Teach compensatory scanning.  
- **Caregiver education**: Educate family on decluttering strategies, lighting adjustments, and supporting safe mobility practices.  

**F. Driving / Community Access**  
- **Driving cessation planning**: Coordinate with optometry for Esterman test. Assess need for alternative transport (public transit, community services) to maintain independence.  
- **Community mobility**: Evaluate confidence in unfamiliar or busy environments. Consider O&M training for route learning and road-crossing strategies.  

---

### 5. Falls Risk Level  
**Moderate**: Peripheral field loss and reduced contrast sensitivity increase collision risk in cluttered environments. No recent falls, but functional decline (side-collisions) and progressive glaucoma suggest elevated risk.  

---

### 6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings  
- **Environmental modifications**: Declutter travel paths, enhance lighting/contrast in high-risk areas (hallways, laundry).  
- **Mobility strategies**: Teach deliberate scanning before turning; consider O&M assessment for cluttered-space navigation.  
- **Task adaptations**: Address medication/label identification and meal-prep safety via contrast enhancement and assistive tech.  
- **Driving/transport**: Coordinate with optometry for Esterman test; plan for alternative community access.
