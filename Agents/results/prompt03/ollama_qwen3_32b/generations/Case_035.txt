==============================  
Case 035  
==============================  

### 1. Patient Context  
- **Age**: 67-year-old male  
- **Care Setting**: Shared-care glaucoma review in community optometry  
- **Falls Context**: Routine review with worsening obstacle awareness noted; reports multiple near-misses in dim hallways (no confirmed falls).  
- **Key Vision Diagnosis**: Moderate primary open-angle glaucoma **OU** causing peripheral field loss and reduced contrast sensitivity.  
- **Other Contextual Factors**: Hypertension and obstructive sleep apnea (no direct OT relevance unless exacerbating fatigue/mobility).  

---

### 2. Key Vision Issues  
- **Bilateral glaucomatous visual field defects**:  
  - **OD**: Inferior visual field defect (MD –7.0 dB) with corresponding inferior RNFL thinning.  
  - **OS**: Inferior nasal arcuate defect (MD –8.0 dB) with inferior temporal RNFL thinning.  
  - **Functional Relevance**: Impaired **floor-level hazard detection** and **spatial awareness**, particularly in dim lighting, aligning with patient-reported difficulty seeing low furniture and floor obstacles until late.  

- **Reduced contrast sensitivity (1.35 log units)**:  
  - Likely contributes to difficulty identifying low-contrast obstacles (e.g., cables, rugs) and increases risk of misjudging depth/shadowed surfaces.  

- **Binocular alignment (4 XP near)**:  
  - May affect **depth perception** during near tasks (e.g., reaching, pouring), though central acuity and stereoacuity remain intact.  

---

### 3. Functional Impact  

#### **Mobility and Falls**  
- **Obstacle negotiation**: Peripheral field loss impairs awareness of low-level hazards (e.g., furniture, stairs, cables), particularly in dim lighting.  
- **Low-light mobility**: Reduced contrast sensitivity and field loss increase risk of tripping in poorly lit areas (e.g., hallways, outdoor steps).  
- **Unfamiliar environments**: May struggle with navigating cluttered or uneven surfaces (e.g., public restrooms, gyms).  

#### **ADLs/IADLs**  
- **Grooming and hygiene**: Difficulty locating small objects (e.g., toothbrush, razor) in low-light or cluttered spaces.  
- **Meal preparation**: Potential risk of misjudging object placement (e.g., reaching for items on a cluttered countertop).  
- **Reading/written materials**: Good near acuity with readers supports medication management and label reading.  

#### **Environmental Interaction**  
- **Lighting needs**: Dim areas (e.g., hallways, bathroom) may require **task-specific lighting** to enhance visibility of floor hazards.  
- **Contrast enhancement**: High-contrast markings on steps, furniture edges, and handrails may improve hazard detection.  
- **Clutter management**: Removal of low-lying obstructions (e.g., rugs, cables) reduces trip risk.  

---

### 4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations  

#### **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**  
- **Assess lighting**: Evaluate brightness, glare, and shadowing in dim areas (e.g., hallways, bathrooms). Recommend **task lighting** (e.g., motion-sensor lights) to highlight floor hazards.  
- **Contrast enhancement**: Suggest high-contrast tape on stair edges, furniture legs, and door thresholds.  
- **Clutter reduction**: Address low-lying obstructions (e.g., cables, rugs) that may not be detected due to peripheral field loss.  

#### **Mobility Strategies**  
- **Gait training**: Educate on scanning techniques (e.g., head/eye movements) to compensate for field loss when navigating dim or cluttered spaces.  
- **Gym safety**: Assess gym environment for hazards (e.g., uneven flooring, equipment placement) and recommend a dedicated walking path or cane use if needed.  

#### **Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies**  
- **Cane use**: Consider a **long cane** for community mobility and a **short cane** for indoor use in dim areas, pending O&M assessment.  
- **Contrast-enhanced tools**: Recommend high-contrast handles or markers for household objects (e.g., sink faucets, door handles).  

#### **Education and Support**  
- **Patient education**: Reinforce caution in dim corridors and use of consistent storage locations for frequently used items.  
- **Driving implications**: Discuss risks of reduced contrast sensitivity during dusk driving. Consider referral for **vision-based driving assessment** if continued nighttime driving is necessary.  

#### **Clinical Follow-up / Communication**  
- **Liaison with O&M**: Confirm referral for O&M training to address spatial awareness deficits and community mobility safety.  
- **Glaucoma management**: Monitor functional changes as disease progresses, particularly during ophthalmology follow-ups.  

---

### 5. Falls Risk Level  
**Moderate**  
- Peripheral field loss and reduced contrast sensitivity increase trip risk in dim or cluttered environments. Near-misses documented, but no confirmed falls.  

---

### 6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings  
- **Assess home lighting and contrast** to enhance floor hazard visibility.  
- **Evaluate mobility strategies** (e.g., cane use, scanning techniques) for dim and cluttered environments.  
- **Collaborate with O&M** to address spatial awareness deficits and community mobility safety.  
- **Monitor driving risks** related to reduced contrast sensitivity and dusk driving.
