==============================  
Case 064  
==============================  

1. **Patient Context**  
82-year-old male in rehabilitation outpatient clinic post-right occipital stroke causing left homonymous hemianopia. Reports left-sided collisions with doorframes, difficulty locating reading lines, and a single fall at home when missing a doorway on the left. Uses bifocals; finds busy environments tiring. Engaged with OT and physio for mobility retraining. No diplopia.  

2. **Key Vision Issues**  
- **Left homonymous hemianopia** likely contributes to left-sided spatial neglect, increasing collision risk with doorways, obstacles, and reduced awareness of left-sided environmental cues.  
- **Reduced contrast sensitivity (1.35 log units)** may complicate object detection, especially in low-contrast or cluttered settings.  
- **Bifocal use** may require attention to transitions between distance and near tasks, potentially compounding left-side awareness during activities like reading-to-walking.  
- **4 XP at near** suggests accommodative or convergence demand may affect reading endurance or accuracy.  

3. **Functional Impact**  
**Mobility and Falls**  
- High risk of left-sided obstacle collisions (doorframes, furniture) due to hemianopia, particularly during turning or doorway navigation.  
- Increased fatigue in busy environments may reduce scanning efficiency, heightening fall risk in crowded or unfamiliar settings.  
- History of one fall suggests potential for recurrent incidents during left-side spatial navigation.  

**ADLs/IADLs**  
- **Reading difficulties** due to missing line starts; may require compensatory strategies (e.g., line guides, text-to-speech).  
- Meal preparation and medication management may be compromised if left-side items (e.g., spices, pill bottles) are overlooked.  
- Visual scanning demands may slow task completion and increase fatigue during household tasks.  

**Environmental Interaction**  
- **Low-contrast environments** (e.g., doorways, cluttered surfaces) may exacerbate hemianopic field loss, reducing object visibility.  
- **Bifocal use** may necessitate careful lighting and task setup to avoid visual disruption during transitions between near and distance tasks.  

4. **Vision-Informed OT Considerations**  
**A. Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**  
- **Left-side environmental cues**: Assess feasibility of high-contrast tape or markers on left-side doorframes, furniture edges, and step beginnings to enhance visibility.  
- **Consistent item placement**: Ensure frequently used objects (e.g., medication, kitchen utensils) are placed within the right visual field to minimize left-side searching.  
- **Lighting optimization**: Evaluate lighting for even illumination and reduced glare, particularly in reading and meal-preparation areas.  

**B. Mobility Strategies**  
- **Scanning training**: Collaborate with OT to reinforce systematic left-side scanning during mobility, especially when turning into doorways or navigating crowded spaces.  
- **Mobility aid assessment**: Consider a long cane held on the left side for environmental scanning; assess suitability of a support guide for complex environments.  
- **O&M referral**: Recommend evaluation by an Orientation & Mobility specialist to refine scanning techniques and address community mobility safety.  

**C. Task Adaptations / Assistive Technology**  
- **Reading accommodations**: Trial line guides, text-to-speech software, or large-print materials to address line-start localization deficits.  
- **Medication management**: Review medication organization systems (e.g., labeled pillboxes, digital reminders) to mitigate left-side item oversight.  
- **Contrast enhancement**: Suggest high-contrast labeling for household objects (e.g., red/yellow labels on left-side items).  

**D. Education and Support**  
- **Patient education**: Reinforce awareness of left-side field loss and strategies to mitigate collisions (e.g., turning slowly, using a cane).  
- **Caregiver training**: Educate family on environmental modifications (e.g., decluttering left-side pathways) and supporting scanning routines.  
- **Energy conservation**: Schedule high-demand tasks during periods of better visual endurance to reduce fatigue.  

**E. Clinical Follow-up / Communication**  
- Monitor functional status for changes in visual field or mobility needs; coordinate with GP and rehabilitation team on progress.  
- Reassess assistive strategies (e.g., cane use, environmental cues) as mobility and cognitive status evolve.  

5. **Falls Risk Level**  
**High** – Left homonymous hemianopia, documented fall, and risk of left-sided obstacle collisions in unfamiliar or cluttered environments.  

6. **Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings**  
- Prioritize environmental modifications (high-contrast cues, consistent item placement) to reduce left-side collision risk.  
- Assess and implement scanning strategies for mobility and ADLs, with potential O&M referral.  
- Address reading and medication management challenges through task adaptations (line guides, high-contrast labeling).  
- Coordinate with rehabilitation team to optimize energy conservation and falls prevention.
