==============================  
Case 066  
==============================  

1. **Patient Context**  
71-year-old community-dwelling female with **medication-related ocular surface disease** (likely preservative hypersensitivity and meibomian gland dysfunction) and **glaucoma**. Reports **fluctuating visual clarity** (worsening in evenings) and **uncertainty on kerbs/steps** during late afternoon. Uses single-vision distance glasses and readers. Drives only during the day; avoids evening driving when eyes are irritated. No confirmed falls but reports hesitation on steps/outdoor hazards.  

2. **Key Vision Issues**  
- **Fluctuating visual clarity** due to tear film instability → Impacts mobility safety and task performance during evenings.  
- **Reduced contrast sensitivity** (1.35 log units) → May affect object identification, especially in low-contrast environments (e.g., meal preparation, locating objects).  
- **4 XP near** (binocular misalignment) → May contribute to depth perception challenges during tasks like pouring, stair negotiation, or reaching.  
- **Tear film instability** → Likely exacerbates visual fatigue and reduces tolerance for visually demanding tasks.  

3. **Functional Impact**  

**Mobility and Falls**  
- **Fluctuating clarity** and **reduced contrast sensitivity** may increase risk of misjudging kerbs, steps, or surface changes in low-light conditions.  
- **4 XP near** may affect depth perception during stair negotiation or reaching for objects.  
- **Evening visual fatigue** may reduce confidence in outdoor mobility, potentially limiting community participation.  

**ADLs/IADLs**  
- **Contrast sensitivity deficits** may hinder identification of household objects, medication labels, or food items.  
- **Binocular misalignment** (4 XP near) may complicate tasks requiring depth judgment, such as pouring or cooking.  
- **Fluctuating vision** may necessitate task adaptation (e.g., timing medication management during periods of better visual clarity).  

**Environmental Interaction**  
- **Evening lighting conditions** (dimming, shadows) likely exacerbate visual instability.  
- **Contrast between steps/kerbs and surroundings** may be insufficient to compensate for reduced contrast sensitivity.  
- **Glare or uneven lighting** during visually demanding tasks (e.g., cooking) may increase error risk.  

4. **Vision-Informed OT Considerations**  

**Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**  
- **Assess lighting levels** in high-risk areas (e.g., kitchen, bathroom, stairs) for evenness, glare, and shadowing. Recommend **task lighting** with adjustable brightness to support evening activities.  
- **Enhance contrast** on steps, kerbs, and household objects (e.g., high-contrast tape, labeled containers) to compensate for reduced contrast sensitivity.  
- **Evaluate furniture placement** and storage consistency to reduce clutter and visual search demands during periods of fluctuating vision.  

**Mobility Strategies**  
- **Assess evening mobility** for kerb/stair navigation, obstacle detection, and confidence. Consider **non-visual scanning strategies** or **O&M referral** if visual instability persists despite environmental modifications.  
- **Recommend caution** on uneven surfaces or in low-light environments (e.g., avoiding walking during peak visual fatigue periods).  

**Task Adaptations**  
- **Time visually demanding tasks** (e.g., medication management, meal prep) to periods of better visual clarity (e.g., mornings).  
- **Assess pour/jug use** and cooking safety, given potential depth perception challenges from 4 XP near. Consider adaptive tools (e.g., spill-proof containers, labeled measuring cups).  
- **Review phone/digital accessibility** (e.g., text-to-speech, high-contrast settings) to support communication and information access during low-visibility periods.  

**Education and Support**  
- **Educate on preservative-free glaucoma drop use** and lubricant timing to align with visual fluctuations. Emphasize **pre-outdoor lubricant use** to mitigate evening blur.  
- **Discuss driving restrictions** and explore alternative transport options (e.g., ride services, public transport) to maintain community access.  
- **Engage family support** to reinforce consistent routines, assist with evening mobility, and monitor for visual fatigue triggers.  

**Clinical Follow-up / Communication**  
- **Coordinate with ophthalmology** to address ocular surface disease management and potential medication adjustments.  
- **Reassess vision-informed OT needs** in 6 weeks post-optometry review, particularly if functional impacts worsen.  

5. **Falls Risk Level**  
**Moderate**: Fluctuating visual clarity and reduced contrast sensitivity increase risk of misjudging steps/kerbs, particularly in evenings. No falls reported, but visual instability compounds risk during visually demanding or low-light conditions.  

6. **Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings**  
- Assess evening mobility safety for kerb/stair navigation and implement contrast-enhancing modifications.  
- Evaluate task timing and lighting needs to accommodate fluctuating vision, particularly for medication management and cooking.  
- Consider O&M referral if binocular misalignment or visual fatigue significantly impairs mobility confidence.  
- Coordinate with ophthalmology to optimize ocular surface disease management and reduce visual fluctuations.
