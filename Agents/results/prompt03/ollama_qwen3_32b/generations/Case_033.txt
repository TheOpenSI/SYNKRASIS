==============================  
Case 033  
==============================  

---

### 1. Patient Context  
63-year-old female with **undercorrected myopic refractive error** (distance VA unaided: 6/24–6/18, best corrected: 6/6). Reports **habitual removal of distance spectacles for near tasks**, followed by **continued unaided mobility**, leading to **hesitancy on uneven paving outdoors** (especially in dull weather). Part-time worker with daily walking for exercise. No confirmed falls but acknowledges misjudging garden path edges. **Driving license requires consistent spectacle use**.  

---

### 2. Key Vision Issues  
- **Undercorrected myopia** causing reduced distance visual clarity, particularly in low-light conditions (e.g., dull weather).  
- **No habitual correction during mobility** (glasses removed for near tasks and not replaced for walking), increasing risk of misjudging environmental hazards (e.g., path edges).  
- **Binocular misalignment at near** (4 XP), potentially contributing to visual discomfort or fatigue during sustained near work.  
- **Normal visual fields, contrast sensitivity, and stereoacuity** when corrected, indicating no structural deficits limiting function.  

---

### 3. Functional Impact  

#### **Mobility and Falls**  
- **Hesitancy on uneven paving** outdoors without glasses increases fall risk, particularly in low-light or dull weather.  
- **Impaired obstacle detection** (e.g., path edges, steps) when unaided may affect safe navigation during daily walks.  
- **Behavioral spectacle use** (removing for near tasks and not reapplying for mobility) creates a **dynamic risk** during transitions between tasks and movement.  

#### **ADLs/IADLs**  
- **Reading and medication management** may be affected when unaided, though best-corrected near VA is sufficient (N4).  
- **Task transitions** (e.g., reading then walking) may require additional attention to avoid unaided mobility.  

#### **Environmental Interaction**  
- **Contrast and lighting** are critical for safe navigation outdoors (e.g., garden paths, steps) when uncorrected.  
- **Consistent spectacle use** is necessary to maintain full visual function, but current habits suggest a need for behavioral reinforcement.  

---

### 4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations  

#### **Self-Care**  
- **Assess medication management** for reliance on best-corrected near vision (N4). Consider tactile or auditory aids if unaided near tasks are required.  

#### **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**  
- **Evaluate outdoor pathways** (e.g., garden, steps) for contrast and lighting. Recommend **high-contrast edging** on path edges and **adequate lighting** for low-visibility periods.  
- **Review indoor lighting** to reduce glare and shadows, particularly in areas where glasses may be removed (e.g., reading nooks).  

#### **Mobility Strategies**  
- **Assess falls risk during unaided mobility** (e.g., walking after removing glasses for near tasks). Recommend **behavioral strategies** to reinforce spectacle use during transitions.  
- **Educate on environmental scanning** techniques for unaided outdoor navigation (e.g., pausing to locate path edges before stepping).  

#### **Task Adaptations**  
- **Develop routines** to prompt spectacle reapplication after near tasks (e.g., linking to specific cues like closing a book or setting down a phone).  
- **Modify task environments** to minimize unaided transitions (e.g., keeping reading materials in a fixed location with glasses nearby).  

#### **Education and Support**  
- **Reinforce the importance of consistent spectacle use** for driving and outdoor mobility.  
- **Educate on fall prevention** during low-visibility conditions (e.g., carrying a white cane for contrast in dull weather).  

#### **Clinical Follow-up / Communication**  
- **Coordinate with optometry** to monitor multifocal adaptation and mobility confidence at 6-week review.  
- **Escalate to O&M assessment** if falls risk persists despite behavioral interventions.  

---

### 5. Falls Risk Level  
**Moderate**. Risk is primarily behavioral (habitual spectacle removal for near tasks) and environmental (misjudging path edges in low light). No structural visual deficits contribute to falls.  

---

### 6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings  
- **Assess spectacle use behavior** and develop routines to prevent unaided mobility.  
- **Modify outdoor environments** (e.g., path edges, lighting) to reduce fall risk during uncorrected periods.  
- **Educate on environmental scanning** and task adaptations to maintain safety during transitions.  
- **Monitor multifocal adaptation** and consider O&M referral if mobility challenges persist.  

--- 

This summary aligns with the optometry findings and provides actionable OT priorities without assuming assessment outcomes or prescribing interventions.
