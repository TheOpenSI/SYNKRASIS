==============================
Case 072
==============================

1. Patient Context
- Age: 82, Female
- Care setting: Community optometry practice
- Falls pathway / referral context: Referred after increasing trouble reading medication labels and one recent stumble in the kitchen.
- Vision Diagnosis: Advanced dry AMD with geographic atrophy OU, pseudophakia OU.
- General Medical History: Hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, and osteoporosis; on antihypertensive therapy and statin.
- Driving status: No longer drives.
- Falls: Single recent stumble in the kitchen.

2. Key Vision Issues
- Central vision loss affecting reading, face recognition, and meal preparation.
- Difficulty in identifying objects directly ahead, especially on patterned surfaces.
- Contrast sensitivity challenges, not testable due to reduced central acuity.

3. Functional Impact

Mobility and Falls
- Increased risk when navigating kitchen due to difficulty identifying objects directly ahead.
- Potential issues with step and surface changes, particularly if low-contrast or on patterned surfaces.

ADLs/IADLs
- Difficulties with reading medication labels, potentially affecting medication management.
- Challenges in meal preparation and food identification.
- Difficulty in locating and identifying household objects.

Participation / Confidence / Roles
- May experience reduced confidence in performing daily tasks, potentially leading to reliance on others or social withdrawal.
- May avoid participation in community activities due to safety concerns.

Environmental Interaction
- Lighting and contrast issues in kitchen may affect task performance.
- Visual clutter and inconsistent storage locations may increase task difficulty.

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations

- **Self-care and Medication Management:**
  - Assess how the patient identifies and manages medications. Consider medication organizers or labels with larger print and higher contrast.
  - Evaluate the patient’s ability to read labels and instructions for medication management and provide assistive devices such as magnifiers if necessary.

- **Household Management:**
  - Assess the kitchen environment for lighting and contrast issues that affect task performance. Consider recommendations for brighter and glare-free lighting and better contrast between objects and the background.
  - Evaluate the kitchen for visual clutter and consistent item placement to support task performance and reduce risk.

- **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment:**
  - Recommend an environmental assessment to identify potential mobility risks and task-related hazards.
  - Consider modifications such as brighter lighting in kitchen and improved contrast for commonly used household objects.
  - Assess for consistent storage locations and visual cues to reduce reliance on central vision for object identification.

- **Mobility Strategies:**
  - Consider whether Orientation & Mobility (O&M) assessment is appropriate to address mobility concerns, especially in the kitchen and around patterned surfaces.
  - Recommend the use of consistent walking paths and visual aids for navigation.

- **Task Adaptations:**
  - Assess how the patient performs meal preparation tasks and identify specific risk points. Consider task-specific adaptations, such as using larger print recipes or tactile markers for identifying food items.
  - Evaluate the use of assistive technology or non-visual techniques for meal preparation tasks.

- **Education and Support:**
  - Educate the patient and family members on the importance of consistent item placement and visual cues to support task performance.
  - Provide support-person education on maintaining a predictable environment and reinforcing agreed strategies.

- **Clinical Follow-up / Communication:**
  - Recommend regular follow-ups with the low vision specialist and ophthalmology for ongoing monitoring and management of AMD.
  - Liaise with the low vision specialist to ensure continuity of care and implement strategies for improved task performance.

5. Falls Risk Level
- Moderate
  - The patient has experienced one recent stumble in the kitchen, and the central vision loss and difficulty in object identification contribute to a moderate risk of falls.

6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings
- **Environmental Assessment:** Assess kitchen lighting, contrast, and clutter to support meal preparation and reduce risk.
- **Medication Management:** Evaluate and adapt strategies for medication identification and management.
- **O&M Assessment:** Consider O&M for safe mobility, especially in the kitchen and around patterned surfaces.
- **Support Education:** Educate the patient and family on consistent item placement and visual cues for task performance.
