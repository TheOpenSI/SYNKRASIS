==============================
Case 032
==============================

1. Patient Context
- Age: 80, male
- Care setting: community optometry practice
- Falls pathway: referred after recurrent falls outdoors; GP noted intermittent double vision and difficulty judging kerb height.
- Key vision diagnosis: Left sixth nerve palsy causing binocular vision and depth perception dysfunction.
- Contextual impairment: Diabetes, hypertension, hypercholesterolaemia.
- Driving status: Stopped driving after diplopia became frequent.

2. Key Vision Issues
- Binocular horizontal diplopia, worse at distance and in left gaze.
- Depth perception dysfunction affecting kerb height judgment.
- Mildly reduced contrast sensitivity.
- No apparent visual field loss or colour vision impairment.

3. Functional Impact

**Mobility and Falls**
- Recurrent falls on uneven footpaths due to depth perception dysfunction.
- Difficulty judging kerb height during outdoor mobility, likely contributing to falls.
- Intermittent double vision and reduced depth perception affecting crossing car parks and stepping off kerbs.

**ADLs/IADLs**
- Reading and identifying objects at near distances are likely affected by binocular dysfunction.
- Managing medication labels and identifying household items may be challenging due to diplopia.
- Reduced depth perception may affect tasks like pouring and cooking, where accurate distance judgment is required.

**Participation / Confidence / Roles**
- Reduced independence and confidence in outdoor mobility due to falls and double vision.
- Potential restriction in community participation due to safety concerns and mobility challenges.

**Environmental Interaction**
- Lighting and contrast adjustments may help mitigate depth perception issues.
- Clutter and visual clutter can exacerbate visual difficulties.

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations

**Self-care**
- Assess the patient's ability to read and identify medications, and consider assistive devices or non-visual methods for medication management.
- Review the patient’s ability to identify household objects and consider tactile or auditory aids.

**Household Management**
- Assess the patient's ability to complete meal preparation tasks, particularly pouring and cooking safety.
- Review household navigation, focusing on areas where depth perception could lead to falls or other accidents.

**Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**
- Consider environmental modifications that reduce visual clutter and improve lighting contrast.
- Evaluate the home environment for potential hazards, such as uneven surfaces or objects in travel paths.

**Lighting and Contrast**
- Recommend lighting adjustments to improve contrast and reduce glare, particularly in areas where the patient is most active.
- Evaluate the lighting levels in key areas, such as the kitchen and living room, to ensure adequate visibility.

**Mobility Strategies**
- Assess the patient’s current mobility strategies and identify potential areas for improvement.
- Consider whether the use of mobility aids or non-visual techniques may help the patient navigate safely outdoors.
- Recommend a referral to Orientation and Mobility (O&M) services for a comprehensive assessment and training, particularly for outdoor mobility and kerb-height judgment.

**Task Adaptations**
- Assess task-specific adaptations, such as the use of tactile or auditory cues for navigating stairs or kerbs.
- Consider the use of larger print or enhanced contrast for reading labels and instructions.

**Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies**
- Explore the use of assistive technology, such as talking devices or non-visual task techniques, to support activities where vision is compromised.

**Education and Support**
- Educate the patient and family members about the importance of consistent spectacle use and the use of any prescribed visual aids.
- Provide support and education to the patient's family or caregivers to ensure they can provide appropriate assistance and maintain a safe home environment.

**Clinical Follow-up / Communication**
- Recommend regular follow-up with the optometrist and GP to monitor recovery and systemic health.

5. Falls Risk Level
- **High**: The patient has a history of recurrent falls, likely exacerbated by binocular vision dysfunction and depth perception issues, particularly when navigating kerbs and uneven surfaces.

6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings
- Assess and adapt medication management and household object identification strategies.
- Evaluate and modify lighting and contrast in the home environment.
- Refer for comprehensive O&M training to address outdoor mobility and kerb-height judgment.
- Provide education and support to the patient and family regarding consistent spectacle use and non-visual strategies for navigation.
