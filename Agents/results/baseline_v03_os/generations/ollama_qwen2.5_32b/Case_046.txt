==============================
Case 046
==============================

1. Patient Context
- Age: 74, male
- Care setting: community optometry practice
- Falls/referral context: referred after new reading difficulty and a recent stumble on a low kerb; GP requested assessment for possible retinal cause
- Key vision diagnosis: Branch retinal vein occlusion with macular oedema OS
- Contextual impairment: cardiovascular disease; single fall

2. Key Vision Issues
- Central vision loss in the left eye, impacting reading and small print identification
- Reduced near visual acuity (N14 to N10 with correction), affecting tasks requiring close-up vision
- Reduced contrast sensitivity (1.20 log units), which may affect tasks in low-light conditions or those requiring detailed visual discrimination

3. Functional Impact

Mobility and Falls
- Risk of stumbling on kerbs and steps, especially when visually distracted or in low-light conditions
- Potential difficulties in accurately judging distances and depth when negotiating stairs or kerbs
- Increased risk of falls or near-misses, particularly in environments with uneven surfaces or low visibility

ADLs/IADLs
- Reading small print, such as labels, instructions, and price tags, may be challenging
- Medication management may be affected due to difficulty in identifying small print on labels
- Meal preparation, particularly tasks requiring close-up vision or accurate reading of recipes, may be difficult

Participation / Confidence / Roles
- Social participation and leisure activities that require reading or detailed visual tasks may be impacted
- Driving locally may be affected due to reduced visual acuity and contrast sensitivity
- Confidence in community mobility may be reduced, leading to potential activity restriction

Environmental Interaction
- Low lighting levels or glare may exacerbate the visual impairment, affecting tasks such as reading or identifying objects
- Cluttered environments or areas with visual distractions may increase the risk of misjudging distances or stumbling

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations

- **Self-care**
  - Assess whether the patient is able to manage personal grooming and hygiene tasks that require close-up vision.
  - Consider whether the patient needs assistance with reading medication labels or instructions, and explore non-visual methods such as tactile markings or verbal reminders.

- **Household Management**
  - Evaluate the patient’s ability to perform tasks such as meal preparation and identify any necessary visual accommodations.
  - Consider task-specific lighting adjustments to improve visibility for tasks such as reading recipes or using appliances.

- **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**
  - Assess the home environment for potential hazards, such as uneven surfaces, clutter, or low lighting, and recommend modifications to reduce fall risk.
  - Evaluate the consistency and placement of frequently used items to support non-visual orientation and task completion.

- **Lighting and Contrast**
  - Recommend a thorough assessment of lighting levels in the home, particularly in areas where tasks requiring detailed vision are performed.
  - Consider the use of high-contrast labels or markings to enhance visibility and reduce the risk of misjudging objects or steps.

- **Mobility Strategies**
  - Recommend an assessment of the patient’s current mobility strategies and consider whether Orientation & Mobility (O&M) training may be beneficial for safe community travel and kerb negotiation.
  - Evaluate the need for mobility aids and explore non-visual navigation techniques.

- **Task Adaptations**
  - Assess whether the patient can safely perform daily tasks that require close-up vision and consider task-specific adaptations, such as the use of magnifiers or adaptive devices.
  - Explore non-visual strategies for tasks that may be challenging due to reduced contrast sensitivity, such as tactile or auditory cues.

- **Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies**
  - Evaluate the use of assistive technology, such as text-to-speech devices, magnifiers, or auditory information for tasks that require reading or visual discrimination.
  - Consider the patient’s ability to use these devices and provide necessary training.

- **Education and Support**
  - Educate the patient and family members on strategies to manage vision-related challenges and reduce fall risk.
  - Discuss the importance of consistent spectacle use and the need for caution when driving or performing tasks that require precise visual acuity.

- **Clinical Follow-up / Communication**
  - Coordinate with ophthalmology for ongoing management of the macular oedema and cardiovascular health review.
  - Maintain communication with the GP and other healthcare professionals to ensure comprehensive care.

5. Falls Risk Level
- Moderate
  - The patient's central vision loss and reduced contrast sensitivity increase the risk of stumbling on kerbs and steps, particularly in low-light conditions or when visually distracted.

6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings
- Assess mobility strategies and environmental modifications to reduce fall risk, particularly for kerb and step negotiation.
- Evaluate task-specific adaptations and assistive technology needs for activities such as reading and meal preparation.
- Educate the patient on driving safety and the importance of consistent spectacle use.
- Coordinate with ophthalmology for ongoing management and ensure comprehensive communication with the GP regarding cardiovascular health.
