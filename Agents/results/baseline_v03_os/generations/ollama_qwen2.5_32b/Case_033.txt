==============================
Case 033
==============================

1. Patient Context
- Age: 63, Female
- Care setting: Community optometry practice
- Falls pathway / referral context: Routine eye examination where mobility-related blur was identified; no confirmed falls, but patient reports hesitancy on uneven paving when walking without glasses.
- Key vision diagnosis: Undercorrected myopic refractive error with no correction when mobile, causing blur / reduced visual clarity.
- General medical history: Hypertension under control via medication.

2. Key Vision Issues
- Reduced visual clarity and distance vision impairment (6/24 OD, 6/18 OS unaided) when not wearing glasses.
- Habitual removal of spectacles for near tasks, leading to continued walking unaided.
- Need for updated distance and near prescriptions with suggestions for multifocals.

3. Functional Impact

**Mobility and Falls**
- Patient reports hesitancy on uneven paving when walking without glasses.
- Misjudgment of garden path edges and needs to slow down outdoors, especially in dull weather.
- Potential for visual misjudgment of surfaces and edges, increasing falls risk.

**ADLs/IADLs**
- Difficulty in maintaining consistent spectacle use, impacting self-care and household management tasks.
- Need to adjust to new multifocal glasses, which may affect near and distance vision tasks.

**Participation / Confidence / Roles**
- Reduced confidence in outdoor mobility may limit leisure and social participation.
- Need to adapt to new glasses, which may affect driving and outdoor activities.

**Environmental Interaction**
- Potential for increased falls risk in low-light conditions or on uneven surfaces.
- Lighting and contrast issues may impact visual clarity and task performance.

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations

**Self-care**
- Assess whether the patient can consistently use glasses for near tasks and mobility.
- Consider environmental cues or routine modifications to support consistent spectacle use.

**Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**
- Assess home environment for lighting and contrast to support the patient’s visual needs.
- Recommend consistent lighting in task-specific areas such as kitchen and bathroom.
- Evaluate for obstacles and surface changes, especially in low-light conditions.

**Mobility Strategies**
- Assess mobility confidence and safety, particularly on uneven surfaces and in low-light conditions.
- Consider Orientation and Mobility (O&M) training to develop strategies for safe outdoor travel.

**Task Adaptations**
- Assess the patient’s ability to perform daily tasks with new multifocal glasses.
- Consider environmental modifications to support task performance, such as clear pathways and consistent item placement.

**Education and Support**
- Educate the patient on the importance of consistent spectacle use for both near and distance tasks.
- Provide strategies for adapting to new glasses, including timing tasks for periods of better visual function.
- Consider support-person education for reinforcing agreed strategies and assisting with mobility practice.

5. Falls Risk Level
- **Moderate**: The patient's hesitancy on uneven paving and misjudgment of garden path edges indicate a moderate falls risk, particularly in low-light conditions. The need for consistent spectacle use and adaptation to new glasses are key factors.

6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings
- Assess consistent spectacle use for both near and distance tasks.
- Evaluate home environment for lighting and contrast to support visual clarity.
- Consider O&M training for safe outdoor travel, especially on uneven surfaces.
- Educate patient and support person on adapting to new multifocal glasses and consistent spectacle use.
