==============================
Case 046
==============================


1. Patient Context

74-year-old male, living in the community. Referred by GP following new reading difficulty and a stumble on a low kerb. Diagnosed with a superior branch retinal vein occlusion (BRVO) with macular oedema in the left eye (OS), causing central vision loss. Relevant medical history includes hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, and atrial fibrillation. Recent stumble reported, but denies recurrent falls.

2. Key Vision Issues

*   **Central vision loss OS:** Branch retinal vein occlusion (BRVO) with macular oedema has caused reduced visual acuity (6/24 best corrected), reduced near vision (N10 with add), reduced contrast sensitivity, and borderline colour vision OS. This affects detailed tasks like reading and identifying small objects.
*   **Reduced distance acuity OS:** Distance VA is 6/24 best corrected OS.
*   **Reduced contrast sensitivity:** Pelli-Robson contrast sensitivity is reduced (1.20 log units) OS.
*   **Peripheral vision intact:** Confrontation visual fields are full OU.
*   **Binocular issues:** 4 XP near phoria present.

3. Functional Impact

*   **Mobility and Falls:** Reduced central vision and contrast sensitivity OS likely contributed to the stumble on a low kerb. Reduced acuity and contrast sensitivity OS may increase difficulty detecting obstacles, judging heights (e.g., kerbs, steps), and navigating in varying light conditions. This may increase falls risk, particularly in the short term until vision stabilises.
*   **ADLs/IADLs:** Difficulty reading small print and identifying price labels (reading difficulty) is directly related to the central vision loss OS. This may affect medication identification, managing finances, and accessing information. Reduced near vision may affect tasks like grooming, hobbies, and food preparation.
*   **Participation / Confidence / Roles:** Reduced driving since left eye worsened. Vision may affect the ability to safely recognise signs or hazards while driving. Reduced central vision and contrast sensitivity may affect community mobility confidence, participation in leisure activities, and social interaction.
*   **Environmental Interaction:** Reduced contrast sensitivity OS may make it difficult to perceive edges or objects against similar backgrounds, especially in environments with poor lighting or glare.

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations

*   **Mobility Strategies:**
    *   Assess current mobility strategies and identify specific difficulties related to the vision loss (e.g., obstacle detection, kerb negotiation).
    *   Consider strategies to enhance obstacle detection and height judgement (e.g., using a cane, maintaining consistent routes, using tactile cues).
    *   Assess the need for Orientation & Mobility (O&M) assessment and training, given the recent fall and specific mobility difficulties reported.
    *   Educate on caution with steps and kerbs until vision stabilises.
*   **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment:**
    *   Assess the home environment for potential hazards related to reduced vision, contrast sensitivity, and depth perception (e.g., poor lighting, glare, clutter, loose rugs, step edges).
    *   Consider modifications to improve visibility and safety (e.g., improved lighting, high-contrast tape on step edges, clear pathways, consistent storage).
*   **Task Adaptations:**
    *   Assess reading tasks and identify strategies to manage reduced central vision and contrast sensitivity (e.g., using larger print, task lighting, magnification, electronic readers).
    *   Assess medication management tasks and consider strategies to ensure accuracy (e.g., using pill organisers, medication reminders, assistance from others).
    *   Consider strategies for tasks affected by reduced near vision (e.g., grooming, hobbies).
*   **Lighting and Contrast:**
    *   Assess household lighting, including brightness, glare, and shadows, and consider modifications to improve contrast and visibility.
    *   Recommend the use of task lighting where appropriate.
*   **Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies:**
    *   Assess the potential benefit of low-vision aids (e.g., magnifiers, electronic readers) in coordination with low-vision services.
    *   Explore non-visual strategies for tasks like medication identification (e.g., using pill organisers, large print labels).
*   **Education and Support:**
    *   Educate the patient and family/carers about the nature of the vision loss, its functional implications, and safety precautions.
    *   Discuss the ophthalmology referral and management plan.
    *   Discuss the potential impact of vision loss on driving and community participation, and explore alternative transport options if driving cessation occurs.
    *   Provide information on available support services (e.g., low-vision services, community transport, falls prevention programs).
*   **Clinical Follow-up / Communication:**
    *   Communicate with the GP regarding the functional implications of the vision loss and any safety concerns.
    *   Collaborate with the ophthalmologist regarding the management plan and visual prognosis.

5. Falls Risk Level

Moderate. The recent stumble, reduced central vision, reduced contrast sensitivity, and potential difficulties with depth perception (secondary to reduced vision/contrast) increase the risk of falls, particularly in the short term. Peripheral vision is intact, which is protective.

6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings

*   Recent stumble and reduced central vision/contrast sensitivity OS increase mobility risks, requiring assessment of strategies and potential O&M referral.
*   Reduced central vision OS affects reading and detailed tasks, requiring assessment of task adaptations and potential low-vision aids.
*   Reduced vision impacts community participation and driving, requiring assessment of alternative transport and support needs.
*   Environmental assessment and modification are needed to address hazards related to reduced vision and contrast sensitivity.
