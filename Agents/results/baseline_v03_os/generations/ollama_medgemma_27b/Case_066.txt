==============================
Case 066
==============================


1.  Patient Context

71-year-old female with long-standing glaucoma treated with multiple drops, presenting for a routine examination. She reports fluctuating visual clarity worsening through the day, particularly impacting her confidence on kerbs and outdoor steps in the late afternoon. She has medication-related ocular surface disease likely related to topical glaucoma drops. She is active, walks daily, and drives, avoiding late-day driving when symptoms are worse. No falls reported.

2.  Key Vision Issues

*   **Fluctuating vision:** Visual clarity worsens throughout the day, especially in the late afternoon/evening, likely due to medication-related ocular surface disease (tear film instability).
*   **Reduced contrast sensitivity:** Pelli-Robson score indicates reduced contrast sensitivity, which may impact object identification, particularly in variable light conditions.
*   **Late-day visual demand impact:** Ocular surface symptoms (blur, stinging, watering) increase in the late day, impacting confidence and perceived safety on steps and kerbs.
*   **Glaucoma:** Existing glaucoma with known visual field loss (though visual fields were full to confrontation at this exam) and cupping, managed with multiple drops.

3.  Functional Impact

*   **Mobility and Falls:**
    *   Reduced confidence on kerbs and outdoor steps late in the afternoon due to fluctuating blur and ocular surface symptoms.
    *   Avoids late-day driving due to eye irritation, suggesting visual function is compromised and affects driving safety/confidence.
    *   Reduced contrast sensitivity may affect obstacle detection, step identification, and depth perception, increasing fall risk, particularly in lower light or when vision fluctuates.
    *   The need for caution on steps/kerbs suggests potential difficulty with safe negotiation of environmental hazards.
*   **ADLs/IADLs:**
    *   Fluctuating vision may affect tasks requiring sustained visual attention or fine detail, particularly in the evening (e.g., reading, hobbies, meal prep, managing medication).
    *   Reduced contrast sensitivity may affect reading, identifying objects, and managing tasks with low contrast.
*   **Participation / Confidence / Roles:**
    *   Late-day visual symptoms and reduced confidence impact participation in outdoor activities and potentially driving.
    *   Fluctuating vision and reduced confidence may lead to activity avoidance or reliance on others.

4.  Vision-Informed OT Considerations

*   **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment:**
    *   Assess home lighting, particularly outdoor lighting around entryways, steps, and kerbs, to improve visibility during late-day activities.
    *   Ensure clear pathways are maintained, free from clutter, and well-lit, especially around potential hazards like steps and kerbs.
    *   Consider high-visibility marking of step edges and kerbs if feasible and appropriate.
*   **Lighting and Contrast:**
    *   Assess the need for task lighting for evening activities like reading or hobbies.
    *   Consider high-contrast aids (e.g., tableware, clothing) if contrast sensitivity issues are functionally limiting.
*   **Task Adaptations:**
    *   Assess whether the patient avoids visually demanding tasks in the late day (e.g., meal preparation, managing finances).
    *   Consider strategies to simplify visually demanding tasks or postpone them to earlier in the day when vision is clearer.
    *   Assess the specific difficulties experienced when negotiating steps and kerbs and potential strategies (e.g., using a cane, handrails).
*   **Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies:**
    *   Discuss the potential benefit of a portable magnifier for reading or other near tasks if reduced vision impacts these activities.
    *   Explore non-visual cues or strategies for navigation (e.g., tactile markers on steps, consistent routes).
*   **Education and Support:**
    *   Educate the patient and support network about the fluctuating nature of the vision impairment and its impact on safety and function.
    *   Discuss strategies for managing symptoms (e.g., lubricant use as recommended by the optometrist) and planning activities around periods of better vision.
    *   Discuss safe strategies for mobility when vision is reduced (e.g., pacing, using a sighted guide if available, avoiding complex routes).
    *   Provide information on falls prevention strategies relevant to visual impairment.
    *   If driving cessation is considered, discuss alternative transport options and community support services.
*   **Clinical Follow-up / Communication:**
    *   Communicate with the ophthalmologist regarding functional concerns and potential medication adjustments.
    *   Follow up with the patient to assess the effectiveness of management strategies (e.g., lubricant use, medication changes) and the ongoing functional impact.
    *   Assess the need for Orientation & Mobility (O&M) services to address specific mobility challenges related to fluctuating vision and reduced confidence on steps/kerbs.

5.  Falls Risk Level

Moderate. The patient reports reduced confidence on steps and kerbs in the late day due to fluctuating vision and ocular surface symptoms, and has reduced contrast sensitivity. While she has not fallen, these factors increase her risk, particularly in environments with variable lighting or complex surfaces. The risk is moderate because the vision is relatively preserved, and she is active, but the functional impact on mobility is clear.

6.  Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings

*   Fluctuating vision and ocular surface symptoms impact late-day confidence and safety, particularly during mobility tasks like navigating steps and kerbs.
*   Reduced contrast sensitivity may affect object identification and depth perception, increasing mobility risks.
*   Late-day visual limitations may affect participation in activities and require task modification or environmental adaptation.
*   Assessment of environmental safety, task strategies, and potential O&M referral is warranted to optimise safe participation.
