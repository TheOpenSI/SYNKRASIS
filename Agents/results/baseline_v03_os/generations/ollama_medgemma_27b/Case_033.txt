==============================
Case 033
==============================


1. Patient Context

63-year-old female presenting for a routine eye examination in a community setting. She reports mobility-related blur, specifically hesitancy on uneven paving when walking without glasses. She has a history of low myopia, hypertension (controlled), and drives regularly (license requires glasses). The primary vision issue stems from under-corrected refractive error and inconsistent spectacle wear.

2. Key Vision Issues

*   **Uncorrected Refractive Error:** Significant reduction in distance visual acuity (OD 6/24, OS 6/18) when not wearing spectacles, improving to 6/6 OU with correction.
*   **Habitual Near Vision:** Reduced near visual acuity (N10 OU @ 40cm) due to habitual removal of distance spectacles for reading.
*   **Inconsistent Spectacle Use:** Reports taking off glasses for reading and forgetting to put them back on, leading to functional impairment during ambulation.
*   **Mild Exophoria at Near:** 4 prism dioptres exophoria at near distance (cover test).

3. Functional Impact

*   **Mobility:** Reduced distance visual acuity without correction impacts navigation, particularly outdoors (misjudging path edges, slowing down), especially in poor lighting. Hesitancy on uneven paving suggests potential fall risk due to impaired obstacle detection and depth perception.
*   **ADLs:** Habitual removal of distance spectacles impacts near tasks like reading (N10 OU), potentially requiring closer working distances or leading to eye strain.
*   **Driving:** Requires distance correction for driving license compliance and safe driving.
*   **Participation:** Walking for exercise is impacted by reduced visual clarity and hesitancy.

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations

*   **Mobility Strategies:**
    *   Assess confidence and safety during ambulation, particularly on uneven surfaces and in varying light conditions.
    *   Consider environmental assessment of walking routes (lighting, surface changes, obstacles) and home environment for safety.
    *   Assess need for education on consistent spectacle use during ambulation, potentially using cues (e.g., placing glasses by the door).
    *   Review the use of mobility aids if ambulation remains challenging despite correction and consistent spectacle use.
    *   Referral to Orientation & Mobility (O&M) specialist may be considered if mobility confidence remains low or complex environmental navigation is required, particularly regarding uneven surfaces.
*   **Task Adaptations:**
    *   Assess near tasks (reading, hobbies) and potential need for task lighting or magnification if consistent spectacle wear is not adopted.
    *   Explore strategies for remembering to wear distance spectacles, especially after transitioning from near tasks.
*   **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment:**
    *   Assess home lighting, particularly in areas where ambulation occurs (e.g., hallways, stairs), to ensure adequate illumination and minimize glare/shadowing.
    *   Ensure walkways are free of clutter and obstacles.
*   **Education and Support:**
    *   Provide education on the importance of consistent spectacle wear for safe mobility and daily activities.
    *   Educate on the potential risks associated with ambulating without correction, especially on uneven surfaces.
    *   Advise on the adaptation process for new multifocal spectacles, including potential initial visual disturbances and the importance of gradual acclimatization, particularly regarding mobility safety.
    *   Discuss the potential benefit of bifocals/multifocals (as suggested by the optometrist) and the need for adaptation, especially regarding stair negotiation and depth perception.
    *   Consider the need for support from family/carers to reinforce consistent spectacle use and monitor for falls or difficulties.
*   **Clinical Follow-up / Communication:**
    *   Follow up in 6 weeks (as per management plan) to assess adaptation to new prescription and mobility confidence.
    *   Communicate with the optometrist regarding OT findings related to mobility and spectacle adherence.

5. Falls Risk Level

Moderate. The patient has demonstrated functional impairment (hesitancy on uneven paving, misjudging edges) related to uncorrected vision, which increases the risk of falls, although no falls have been reported. The issue is primarily behavioural (inconsistent spectacle use) rather than a severe ocular pathology, and correction is effective.

6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings

*   The primary functional issue is inconsistent spectacle wear leading to reduced distance vision and potential mobility hazards.
*   Assessment of mobility confidence and safety, particularly on uneven surfaces, is needed.
*   Strategies to promote consistent spectacle use (environmental cues, education) should be considered.
*   Assessment of the home environment for safety and lighting is relevant.
*   Education on the importance of consistent spectacle wear and adaptation to new multifocals is crucial.
