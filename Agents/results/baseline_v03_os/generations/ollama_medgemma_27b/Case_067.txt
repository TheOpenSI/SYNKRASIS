==============================
Case 067
==============================


1. Patient Context

79-year-old male, living in the community. Referred after a recent fall on a poorly lit outdoor step. Key vision diagnoses are dense bilateral cataracts causing severe blur and glare, leading to functional limitations including difficulty identifying step edges, reliance on his wife for transport and reading, and recent cessation of driving.

2. Key Vision Issues

*   **Dense bilateral cataracts:** Cause significant reduction in visual acuity (best corrected distance VA 6/60 OU), severe glare, and markedly reduced contrast sensitivity.
*   **Poor step-edge visibility in low illumination:** Directly linked to the fall and reported difficulty navigating outdoor steps.
*   **Reduced vision impacts daily function:** Difficulty reading small print, needing assistance with reading and transport.
*   **Stopped driving:** Due to poor vision and glare, 1 month prior to assessment.
*   **Habitual Near Vision:** Significant near vision difficulty (N48 OU @40 cm), likely exacerbated by cataracts and possibly age-related presbyopia/lens changes, further impacting reading and potentially medication management.

3. Functional Impact

*   **Mobility and Falls:**
    *   **High falls risk:** Documented recent fall likely due to vision impairment (poor step edge visibility in low light).
    *   **Difficulty with outdoor mobility:** Especially at dusk or in low light, impacting independence and community access.
    *   **Difficulty negotiating steps and kerbs:** Poor step edge visibility is a specific safety risk.
    *   **May affect safe navigation in the home and community:** Particularly areas with poor lighting or contrast.
*   **ADLs/IADLs:**
    *   **Difficulty with reading:** Requires assistance with reading small print (medication labels, instructions, etc.).
    *   **Difficulty with medication management:** May struggle to identify correct medication, read labels, or manage dosage due to reduced acuity and potential near vision issues.
    *   **Difficulty with potentially unsafe tasks:** Cooking, using sharp tools, or operating machinery may be hazardous due to reduced acuity, glare, and poor contrast.
    *   **Reliance on wife for transport:** Impacts independence and participation.
*   **Participation / Confidence / Roles:**
    *   **Reduced independence:** Relies on wife for transport and reading.
    *   **Reduced community participation:** Reluctance to go outside at dusk due to poor vision.
    *   **May lead to social isolation or reduced leisure activities:** Due to difficulty seeing (e.g., watching TV, hobbies).
    *   **Likely impact on confidence:** Fear of falling may further restrict activities.

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations

*   **Safety:**
    *   **Home Safety Assessment:** Assess home environment for fall risks, including lighting (especially step and pathway lighting), clutter, and step/kerb visibility.
    *   **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment:** Recommend home modifications to improve lighting (pathways, steps, entryways), use high-contrast step edging, and ensure clear walkways.
    *   **Lighting and Contrast:** Assess lighting levels and contrast throughout the home, particularly in areas where the patient performs tasks or ambulates. Consider task lighting for reading and other activities.
    *   **Mobility Strategies:** Assess current mobility strategies, especially outdoors and on stairs. Consider strategies for navigating low-lit environments, such as using a walking aid for balance and probing.
*   **Task Adaptations:**
    *   **Medication Management:** Assess current medication management strategies. Consider strategies to improve identification and accuracy, such as using large-print labels, pill organisers, or electronic reminders.
    *   **Reading:** Assess reading habits and strategies. Consider large-print options, task lighting, or audiobooks.
    *   **Meal Preparation:** Assess safety during meal preparation, particularly regarding heat/blade safety and contrast.
*   **Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies:**
    *   **Magnification:** Explore the use of handheld or stand magnifiers for reading and other close tasks, in conjunction with low-vision services.
    *   **Auditory/Tactile Aids:** Consider talking devices (e.g., watches, clocks, medication identifiers).
*   **Education and Support:**
    *   **Education:** Educate patient and wife on the impact of cataracts on vision and safety, the importance of home modifications, and strategies for safe mobility.
    *   **Support:** Discuss the impact of vision loss on independence and participation. Explore strategies for maintaining social engagement and accessing community resources.
    *   **Clinical Follow-up / Communication:** Communicate findings and recommendations to the ophthalmology team regarding functional limitations and safety concerns. Follow up post-cataract surgery to assess functional recovery and address any residual issues.
*   **Mobility and Falls:**
    *   **Community Mobility:** Assess ability to safely navigate the community independently or with support. Consider Orientation & Mobility (O&M) assessment if community mobility is significantly impaired or if specific mobility training is needed (e.g., safe navigation techniques in low light).
    *   **Driving Cessation:** Discuss the impact of driving cessation on independence and participation. Explore alternative transportation options and strategies for maintaining community access.

5. Falls Risk Level

High - due to documented recent fall directly linked to vision impairment (poor step edge visibility in low light), severe vision loss, and mobility limitations.

6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings

*   High fall risk requiring immediate safety assessment (home, community).
*   Significant functional limitations impacting ADLs (reading, medication), IADLs (transport), and participation (community access, driving cessation).
*   Need for environmental modifications (lighting, contrast) and task adaptations (medication management, reading) to improve safety and function.
*   Potential need for O&M assessment to address community mobility challenges and safe navigation strategies.
