==============================
Case 035
==============================

1. **Patient Context**

- **Age:** 67
- **Gender:** Male
- **Care Setting:** Shared-care glaucoma review in community optometry
- **Diagnosis:** Primary open-angle glaucoma OU with peripheral field and spatial awareness loss. Glaucoma suspect history progressed to established disease.
- **Relevant Contextual Impairment:** Hypertension and obstructive sleep apnoea may affect energy levels and overall stamina, potentially influencing occupational performance.

2. **Key Vision Issues**

- **Peripheral Field Loss:** Glaucoma has caused peripheral field defects, impacting spatial awareness, particularly at floor level.
- **Reduced Contrast Sensitivity:** Impaired ability to discern contrasts, especially in low-light conditions, affecting obstacle detection.
- **Progressive Glaucoma:** Worsening visual field defects and reduced visual field index (VFI) signal disease progression.
  
3. **Functional Impact**

**Mobility and Falls**
- **Obstacle Detection:** Difficulty in detecting obstacles at floor level, particularly in dim lighting, may increase fall risk. 
- **Low-Light Navigation:** Reports of near-misses in dim hallways indicate challenges in low-light environments.
- **Environmental Navigation:** Possible challenges in navigating around low furniture and other obstacles.

**ADLs/IADLs**
- **Household Navigation:** Difficulty in spotting floor-level hazards could complicate tasks like walking through hallways and moving around furniture.
- **Community Mobility:** Potential issues with navigating unfamiliar or dimly lit environments may affect community access and social participation.

**Participation / Confidence / Roles**
- **Confidence in Mobility:** Reports of reduced confidence stepping around low furniture suggest an impact on independence and daily participation.
- **Community and Social Engagement:** Diminished spatial awareness may affect confidence in participating in social activities or community events.

**Environmental Interaction**
- **Lighting Needs:** Poor contrast sensitivity and spatial awareness in low light suggest a need for improved lighting conditions at home.
- **Clutter Management:** Floor clutter removal is recommended to minimize navigation obstacles and fall risks.

4. **Vision-Informed OT Considerations**

**Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**
- **Lighting and Contrast:** Assess household lighting, including brightness and contrast, to support visual needs and minimize glare or shadows.

**Mobility Strategies**
- **Orientation & Mobility Assessment:** Consider referral to an O&M specialist to develop strategies for safe navigation in low-light environments and around obstacles.

**Task Adaptations**
- **Household Navigation:** Assess and adapt task strategies for navigating the home environment, focusing on obstacle avoidance and floor-level hazard detection.

**Support and Education**
- **Support-Person Education:** Educate family or carers on maintaining a clutter-free environment and improving lighting where necessary.
- **Self-Management Education:** Educate the patient on techniques to enhance spatial awareness and safety in daily activities.

**Clinical Follow-up / Communication**
- **Interdisciplinary Communication:** Facilitate communication between optometry, ophthalmology, and OT to coordinate care and management strategies, ensuring comprehensive support for the patient's vision and mobility needs.

**Falls Risk Level**
- **Moderate:** Due to the combination of peripheral field loss, reduced contrast sensitivity, and reports of near-misses in dim lighting, the patient presents a moderate risk for falls related to vision.

5. **Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings**

- Assess and improve household lighting to enhance visibility and contrast.
- Consider referral to Orientation & Mobility for specialized navigation strategies in low-light and obstacle-rich environments.
- Educate on maintaining a clutter-free home to minimize fall risks and enhance spatial awareness.
- Facilitate interdisciplinary communication to ensure cohesive management of the patient's visual and functional needs. 

This summary aims to support OT clinical reasoning by translating the vision findings into functional implications and prioritizing assessment areas and intervention strategies.
