==============================
Case 064
==============================

1. **Patient Context**

An 82-year-old male, in a rehabilitation outpatient clinic following a stroke, presents with a primary diagnosis of left homonymous hemianopia. He is currently undergoing rehabilitation after a fall-related event. His stroke history is notable, and he experiences difficulty with left-sided vision, impacting his mobility and safety. He uses bifocal spectacles and is actively engaged in occupational therapy (OT) and physiotherapy for mobility retraining. His medical history includes atrial fibrillation and hypertension, for which he is on anticoagulation and antihypertensive therapy. He is not currently driving.

2. **Key Vision Issues**

- **Left Homonymous Hemianopia:** Results in loss of left visual field, causing difficulty detecting obstacles and doorways on the left side.
- **Reduced Contrast Sensitivity:** May affect the ability to discern objects, particularly in lower-light conditions.
- **Impact on Reading and Navigation:** Challenges with locating the start of lines when reading and slower navigation in crowded spaces.

3. **Functional Impact**

**Mobility and Falls:**  
- Obstacle detection and doorway navigation may be challenging, increasing the risk of falls, as demonstrated by a recent fall when missing a doorway.  
- Navigation in crowded or complex environments may be slower and more tiring, potentially leading to fatigue-related safety risks.

**ADLs/IADLs:**  
- Reading may be affected by difficulty locating the start of lines, possibly impacting medication management and communication.  
- Locating and organizing household items may be affected, especially those placed on the left side.

**Participation / Confidence / Roles:**  
- Community access and participation in social activities may be affected by slower navigation in busy environments, impacting confidence and independence.  
- The risk of social isolation or deconditioning should be explored due to mobility challenges.

**Environmental Interaction:**  
- Environmental cues may be necessary to assist with obstacle detection and navigation, particularly in low-light conditions or when transitioning between spaces.

4. **Vision-Informed OT Considerations**

- **Mobility Strategies:**  
  Assess and enhance compensatory scanning techniques to improve left-sided obstacle detection.  
  Consider the use of a mobility cane or low vision identification badge to enhance safety in unfamiliar environments.

- **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment:**  
  Evaluate the home environment for potential hazards and recommend modifications, such as contrasting colors or tactile cues, to assist with navigation and obstacle detection.

- **Task Adaptations:**  
  Assess reading strategies and consider assistive devices or techniques to improve reading efficiency and accuracy.

- **Education and Support:**  
  Educate the patient and caregivers on compensatory strategies for left-sided vision loss and reinforce the importance of consistent use of these strategies.

- **Clinical Follow-up / Communication:**  
  Maintain communication with the rehabilitation team and GP to ensure a coordinated approach to managing the patient's vision-related functional challenges.  
  Schedule a follow-up review in 6 months or sooner if there are changes in functional status or new visual symptoms.

5. **Falls Risk Level**

- **Moderate:** The presence of left homonymous hemianopia significantly impacts mobility and increases the risk of falls, as evidenced by a recent incident. The patient's ongoing rehabilitation and compensatory strategies may mitigate some risks, but continued assessment and support are necessary.

6. **Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings**

- Enhance compensatory scanning techniques to improve left-sided obstacle detection and reduce fall risk.
- Evaluate the home environment for safety modifications to support navigation and obstacle awareness.
- Educate the patient and caregivers on effective use of compensatory strategies and mobility aids.
- Maintain communication with the rehabilitation team to ensure a coordinated approach to the patient's functional challenges.
