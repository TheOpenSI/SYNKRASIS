==============================
Case 033
==============================


1. **Patient Context**  
   - 63‑year‑old woman, community‑based optometry referral.  
   - Reports routine removal of single‑vision distance glasses for near tasks and forgetting to re‑wear them while walking; has misjudged garden path edges and slowed down outdoors, particularly in dull lighting.  
   - Works part‑time, walks daily for exercise.  
   - Drives regularly, wears glasses, licence requires them.  
   - No prior ocular surgery, no diplopia, no field loss; hypertension controlled.  

2. **Key Vision Issues**  
   • Under‑corrected myopia: unaided distance VA 6/24 OD / 6/18 OS, improving to 6/6 with full correction.  
   • Behavioural spectacle removal: patient self‑reports forgetting to re‑wear glasses after near tasks, resulting in intermittent distance blur during walking.  
   • No field or alignment deficits; contrast sensitivity normal; early nuclear sclerosis only.  
   • Distance blur more noticeable in low‑light or dull weather, suggesting sensitivity to ambient lighting and edge contrast.  

3. **Functional Impact**  

   **Mobility & Falls**  
   - Misjudging curb or path edges → increased risk of tripping or falling on uneven paving.  
   - Hesitancy and slower walking outdoors may compromise confidence and safety, especially in low‑light conditions.  

   **ADLs/IADLs**  
   - Near‑vision tasks (reading labels, medication instructions, phone screens) may be unreliable without dedicated reading correction; patient reports forgetting glasses.  
   - Potential for medication errors or misreading of written information.  

   **Participation / Confidence / Roles**  
   - Daily exercise walking and part‑time work may be limited by reduced confidence in outdoor mobility.  
   - Possible avoidance of uneven surfaces or poorly lit areas, potentially affecting community participation.  

   **Environmental Interaction**  
   - Requires consistent placement of spectacles and use of cues to support re‑wear.  
   - Low ambient lighting outdoors could amplify distance blur; indoor lighting for near tasks may need optimisation.  

4. **Vision‑Informed OT Considerations**  

   **Mobility Strategies**  
   - *Assess* the safety of the patient’s outdoor route: uneven paving, surface changes, visibility at dusk/dawn.  
   - Consider referral for Orientation & Mobility (O&M) assessment to develop safe walking patterns and road‑crossing techniques tailored to intermittent distance blur.  
   - Educate on consistent use of distance correction while walking; introduce wearable reminders (e.g., coloured strap, alarm) if appropriate.  

   **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**  
   - *Assess* home and outdoor pathways for hazards; recommend smoothing of uneven surfaces, use of contrasting paint or edging to delineate walkways.  
   - Recommend removal of clutter from walkways and consistent furniture placement to minimise obstacle collision.  

   **Lighting and Contrast**  
   - *Assess* indoor task lighting for near work; suggest bright, evenly distributed light and glare control for reading tasks.  
   - For outdoor activity, discuss the use of higher‑contrast footwear or gait aid marks to improve edge detection in low light.  

   **Task Adaptations**  
   - Prescribe dedicated near‑vision correction (reading glasses, bifocals or progressive addition) to support reading and medication labels.  
   - Teach patient to remove distance glasses before near tasks and to re‑wear immediately after; use of tactile cues (e.g., placing glasses on a designated hook).  

   **Assistive Technology / Non‑Visual Compensation**  
   - Introduce low‑vision aids for near tasks: handheld magnifiers, electronic reading devices, or smartphone apps with magnification/text‑to‑speech.  
   - Evaluate feasibility of auditory cues for distance judgment (e.g., hearing echo or environmental sounds).  

   **Education and Support**  
   - Discuss importance of consistent spectacle wear for safety; provide strategies to remember re‑wearing (e.g., placing glasses beside keys).  
   - Provide written instructions on adaptation to multifocals if prescribed, highlighting potential temporary gait changes.  

   **Clinical Follow‑up / Communication**  
   - Coordinate with optometrist on adaptation progress and vision stability.  
   - Recommend patient report any falls or persistent blur immediately; schedule early follow‑up if needed.  

5. **Falls Risk Level**  
   **Moderate** – intermittent distance blur due to behavioural spectacle use, combined with environmental hazards (uneven paving) increases fall risk, although no falls have yet occurred.  

6. **Summary of Key OT Considerations**  
   - Promote consistent use of distance correction during outdoor mobility and assess route safety.  
   - Introduce O&M referral to support safe navigation of uneven surfaces and low‑light conditions.  
   - Provide near‑vision correction and task‑specific adaptations for reading and medication management.  
   - Optimize lighting and contrast both indoors and outdoors to enhance distance perception.  
   - Offer education on spectacle‑wear habits and cueing strategies to mitigate behavioural risk.
