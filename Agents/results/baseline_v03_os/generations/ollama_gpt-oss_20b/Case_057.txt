==============================
Case 057
==============================


1. **Patient Context**  
   - 84‑year‑old male, community resident, living with wife.  
   - History of ischemic stroke (≈ 2 years ago) with right homonymous hemianopia; bilateral cataracts with progressive glare/blur.  
   - Hearing impairment, arthritis, hypertension, on antihypertensive and antiplatelet therapy.  
   - Recurrent falls: two documented falls in the past 4 months (one during a doorway turn, one when stepping from bright sun into shade).  
   - Uses bifocals full‑time; does not drive.  
   - Currently under rehabilitation follow‑up for mobility and home safety.

2. **Key Vision Issues**  
   - **Right‑sided field loss** affecting both eyes; missing right‑hand walls/objects, difficulty locating hazards.  
   - **Reduced clarity and contrast** (VA 6/60 OD, 6/36 OS; contrast sensitivity 1.05 log units) → difficulty identifying step edges, surface changes, and peripheral cues.  
   - **Glare and haze** with bright illumination, especially when moving from light to shade, contributing to loss of situational awareness.  
   - **Bifocal use on stairs** creates focal‑plane mismatches that can mask depth perception and step edge visibility.  
   - **Hearing loss** may limit auditory cues that normally aid orientation and fall avoidance.

3. **Functional Impact**

   *Mobility and Falls*  
   - Difficulty detecting right‑hand walls and obstacles → increased risk during turning and entering/exiting rooms.  
   - Reduced contrast and glare sensitivity raise the likelihood of tripping on step edges, surface changes, or uneven terrain.  
   - Bifocals on stairs may conceal step depth, raising tripping risk.  
   - Hearing impairment may reduce ability to use auditory cues for environmental awareness.

   *ADLs/IADLs*  
   - Potential challenges in meal preparation and kitchen navigation (identifying cupboard fronts, appliance handles).  
   - Medication management may be affected if labels are difficult to read in glare or low contrast.  
   - Bathroom safety (identifying thresholds, detecting changes in floor height) could be compromised.

   *Participation / Confidence*  
   - Uncertainty about safe navigation may limit confidence in leaving home, attending social events, or performing errands.  
   - Fear of falling could restrict engagement in leisure activities, especially outdoors or in busy environments.

   *Environmental Interaction*  
   - Variable lighting (bright outdoors to shaded entry) exacerbates glare/blur.  
   - Potential clutter or inconsistent placement of objects on the right side of rooms increases collision risk.

4. **Vision‑Informed OT Considerations**

   **Self‑care**  
   - *Assess* whether right‑hand side of bathroom (shower head, toilet seat) is clear of obstacles and visible in current lighting.  
   - *Consider* training in the use of a white‑washed or highly contrasting step‑edge indicator (e.g., white tape or contrast strip) on steps and thresholds.

   **Household Management**  
   - *Assess* organization of pantry, kitchen cabinets, and storage areas for consistency, especially on the right side of the house.  
   - *Support* spouse to maintain a predictable layout: same items stored in same spots, minimal clutter near pathways.

   **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**  
   - *Assess* overall lighting levels in living areas, kitchen, bathroom, and entryways.  
   - *Consider* installing brighter, glare‑reduced lighting (e.g., LED fixtures with adjustable color temperature) in high‑use areas and at transitions between light and shade.  
   - *Explore* use of high‑contrast paint or tape on step edges, door frames, and thresholds to aid depth perception and edge detection.  
   - *Recommend* removal or repositioning of obstacles that are not in the right‑side line of sight but could be missed during turning.

   **Lighting and Contrast**  
   - *Assess* task lighting adequacy for reading medication labels and cooking tasks; adjust to reduce glare and improve contrast.  
   - *Consider* the use of adjustable lamps with warm, diffused light in the kitchen and bathroom to minimise glare when moving from outdoor light.

   **Mobility Strategies**  
   - *Assess* current scanning technique taught in rehabilitation (e.g., systematic head‑turning to the right).  
   - *Recommend* periodic reinforcement of scanning strategies, possibly with an orientation & mobility specialist, to ensure safe navigation of doorways and transitions.  
   - *Consider* introduction of a mobility cane (single‑hand or tandem) once cataract surgery status is clarified; discuss with low‑vision services for fitting and training.  
   - *Consider* a support guide (family member or companion) for ambulation in complex or unfamiliar environments, with brief training on safe hand‑hold and positioning.

   **Task Adaptations**  
   - *Assess* meal‑preparation workflow: placement of cutting boards, pans, and utensils to ensure they are within clear field and contrast.  
   - *Consider* using tactile or color‑coded labeling on frequently used items (e.g., spice jars) to aid identification.  
   - *Support* spouse in reading medication labels together, using a magnifier or high‑contrast reading glasses if needed.

   **Assistive Technology / Non‑visual Compensation**  
   - *Assess* feasibility of a smartphone or tablet with text‑to‑speech or voice‑assistant for medication reminders, navigation, and communication.  
   - *Consider* a hand‑held flashlight or ambient light sensor to aid in low‑light areas.  
   - *Encourage* use of a hearing aid or amplification device if hearing loss is significant, to improve overall environmental awareness.

   **Education and Support**  
   - *Educate* patient and wife on the importance of consistent lighting, high‑contrast step indicators, and environmental clutter reduction.  
   - *Inform* spouse of safety cues to watch for (e.g., sudden change in lighting, step edges).  
   - *Provide* written or visual reminders of scanning strategies and safety tips for doorways and transitions.

   **Clinical Follow‑up / Communication**  
   - *Coordinate* with the rehabilitation team to monitor progress of scanning strategies and fall frequency.  
   - *Plan* a reassessment of functional mobility and home safety after cataract surgery (expected improvement in clarity but not field).  
   - *Keep* eye‑care provider informed of any changes in glare tolerance or contrast perception that may influence spectacle prescription adjustments.

5. **Falls Risk Level**  
   **High** – The combination of right‑sided field loss, poor contrast sensitivity, glare‑related visual degradation, and recent recurrent falls creates a significant, multifactorial fall risk that warrants proactive, comprehensive OT assessment and intervention.

6. **Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings**  
   - Prioritise a detailed home‑environment assessment focusing on lighting, contrast, and obstacle removal, especially on the right side of rooms.  
   - Reinforce and monitor right‑sided scanning strategies; consider referral to an orientation & mobility specialist for advanced training.  
   - Introduce high‑contrast step‑edge markers and safe stair‑navigation practices, and evaluate the potential use of a mobility cane post‑cataract surgery.  
   - Provide education to the patient and spouse on environmental modifications, scanning techniques, and safety cues, and coordinate with the rehabilitation team for ongoing fall‑risk monitoring.
