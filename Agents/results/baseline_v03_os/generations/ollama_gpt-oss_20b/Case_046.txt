==============================
Case 046
==============================


1. **Patient Context**  
- 74‑year‑old male, community optometry referral after a single low‑kerb stumble and new reading difficulty.  
- Left eye (OS) has central vision loss from branch retinal vein occlusion with macular oedema; right eye remains relatively clear.  
- Uses separate distance and near spectacles; reports reduced driving since visual decline.  
- Medical comorbidities: hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, atrial fibrillation on anticoagulation.  
- No other ocular or neurological deficits reported.

2. **Key Vision Issues**  
- **Central visual acuity loss OS** (6/30 best‑corrected 6/24) → limited ability to resolve fine detail at distance and near.  
- **Reduced contrast sensitivity** (1.20 log units) → difficulty detecting low‑contrast edges such as curb transitions and small print.  
- **Limited stereopsis** (80″) and **near‑vision decrement** (N14 habitual) → challenges with depth perception during feeding, dressing, and navigating cluttered spaces.  
- **Patchy macular oedema** → intermittent blurring and scotoma may alter visual reliability over time.

3. **Functional Impact**  

- **Mobility and Falls**  
  - Stumbled over a low kerb; central vision loss and reduced contrast make edge detection unreliable.  
  - Potential for difficulty negotiating uneven flooring, stair nosings, and steps.  
  - Limited stereopsis may impair depth judgment when walking in cluttered or narrow spaces.  

- **ADLs / IADLs**  
  - Reading small print and price labels is impaired; may affect medication labeling, food labels, grocery lists, and financial documents.  
  - Near‑task safety during meal preparation, dressing, and grooming could be compromised by reduced depth perception and central scotoma.  
  - Potential difficulty in identifying household objects, locating keys or remote controls.  

- **Participation / Confidence**  
  - Reduced driving confidence; may limit community mobility and social participation.  
  - Uncertainty about visual reliability may increase anxiety during everyday tasks.

- **Environmental Interaction**  
  - Low‑contrast or poorly lit areas pose increased hazard; clutter can be harder to detect.  
  - Inconsistent spectacle use may further reduce visual input.

4. **Vision‑Informed OT Considerations**  

- **Self‑care**  
  - *Assessment*: Examine current medication packaging for label size and contrast; evaluate ability to read labels with current spectacles.  
  - *Intervention*: Introduce large‑print labels or digital medication lists; consider a medication management system (e.g., pill organizer with high‑contrast tabs).  
  - *Education*: Emphasise consistent use of spectacles, especially for near tasks, and safe lighting practices at home.

- **Household Management**  
  - *Assessment*: Observe meal‑preparation flow; check for potential hazards such as sharp knives, hot surfaces, and clutter in the kitchen.  
  - *Intervention*: Suggest tactile cues (e.g., colour‑coded pot lids), high‑contrast kitchen labels, and organisational strategies (fixed item locations).  
  - *Assistive Technology*: Recommend smartphone apps or smart devices that provide audible cues for cooking times or water temperature.

- **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**  
  - *Lighting and Contrast*: Assess ambient and task lighting; advise increasing brightness where needed and reducing glare.  
  - *Clutter Management*: Encourage decluttering of walkways, consistent furniture placement, and the use of high‑contrast floor markings or tactile floor strips to delineate edges.  
  - *Kerb & Surface Changes*: Discuss the feasibility of raising or marking low kerbs, or providing a handrail along outdoor walkways.

- **Mobility Strategies**  
  - *Assessment*: Perform a gait and balance screen; observe reaction to uneven surfaces and step negotiation.  
  - *Intervention*: Teach cautious scanning techniques (e.g., “look ahead, look down, look at the edge”) and hand‑hold strategies with a cane or walker if indicated.  
  - *O&M Referral*: Recommend a formal Orientation & Mobility assessment if the patient struggles with familiar routes or if uneven outdoor terrain is common.

- **Task Adaptations**  
  - *Reading*: Introduce magnification devices (handheld magnifier, screen magnification software) and high‑contrast reading material.  
  - *Near Tasks*: Use tactile markers for depth cues during dressing (e.g., feel for button placement) and meal prep (e.g., feel for pot handle).  
  - *Driving*: Discuss alternative transport options; advise on safe driving practices (e.g., avoid night driving, use adaptive equipment if indicated).  

- **Assistive Technology / Non‑visual Compensation**  
  - Evaluate suitability of a smartphone with voice‑over or text‑to‑speech for reading documents.  
  - Consider a wearable device that alerts to obstacles or steps (e.g., vibration cue).  

- **Education and Support**  
  - Train a primary support person in maintaining a predictable environment, clearing walkways, and reinforcing scanning strategies.  
  - Provide written information on high‑contrast labeling and safe lighting.  

- **Clinical Follow‑up / Communication**  
  - Coordinate with ophthalmology and the GP for ongoing medical review.  
  - Schedule an OT re‑assessment post‑retinal treatment to evaluate changes in visual function and adjust interventions accordingly.

5. **Falls Risk Level**  
- **Moderate** – The patient has experienced one kerb‑related fall linked to central vision loss and reduced contrast sensitivity; functional deficits affect mobility and near tasks, but no recurrent falls or severe visual impairment are reported.

6. **Summary of Key OT Considerations**  
- Prioritise fall prevention through environmental modification, mobility strategy training, and possible O&M referral.  
- Support reading and near‑task performance with high‑contrast labeling, magnification, and assistive technology.  
- Address driving concerns by exploring alternative transport, educating on safe driving cues, and coordinating with the ophthalmology team.  
- Provide consistent education to the patient and informal support network about environmental safety and routine visual task strategies.
