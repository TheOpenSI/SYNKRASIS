==============================
Case 083
==============================


1. **Patient Context**  
   - 78‑year‑old female with progressive normal‑tension glaucoma OU, now classified as moderate right eye (RE) and advanced left eye (LE) visual field loss.  
   - Lives independently in a shared‑care setting; reports increasing side‑collision events (doorframes, laundry baskets) and increased caution in cluttered environments.  
   - Drives short daytime distances only; has been advised to refrain from driving until an Esterman binocular field assessment is completed.  
   - Medical comorbidities: hypertension, migraine, Raynaud’s phenomenon (noted to have potential for fluctuating visual function in certain circumstances).  
   - Prescribed single‑vision distance spectacles for walking and separate readers for near work; no central blur, diplopia or flash phenomena reported.

2. **Key Vision Issues**  
   - **Inferior peripheral field loss** with documented arcuate defects (RE) and paracentral inferior loss (LE) → loss of spatial awareness on the right side.  
   - **Reduced contrast sensitivity** (1.35 log units) → difficulty detecting low‑contrast edges, especially in low‑light or cluttered settings.  
   - **Stable near vision** with add (N5) but no central visual impairment → near‑task performance likely preserved.  
   - **Orthophoric alignment** with a 4 Δ exophoria at near → mild binocular alignment issue that may influence near‑task comfort.  

3. **Functional Impact**  
   - **Mobility & Falls**  
     - Increased risk of side‑collision when turning, especially in tight hallways or around obstacles.  
     - Clipping doorframes and laundry baskets indicates impaired peripheral detection of obstacles.  
     - Reduced contrast sensitivity may hinder identification of step edges, thresholds, and low‑light transitions.  
   - **ADLs/IADLs**  
     - Navigation of cluttered areas (e.g., kitchen, living room) may become unsafe, potentially affecting meal preparation, cleaning, and household organization.  
     - Turning in confined spaces could compromise safe completion of dressing, bathing, and transfer tasks.  
     - No documented falls yet, but functional decline suggests that near‑fall risk is rising.  
   - **Participation / Confidence**  
     - Heightened caution may reduce confidence in independently moving around home; could impact leisure or social activities that involve walking or navigating unfamiliar spaces.  

4. **Vision‑Informed OT Considerations**  

   **A. Self‑Care & Household Management**  
   - **Assess** whether turning while dressing or grooming is safely managed; identify any difficulty with reaching or avoiding side‑collisions.  
   - **Assess** meal‑prep tasks (e.g., stirring, pouring, cutting) for potential safety issues related to reduced peripheral awareness.  

   **B. Environmental Modifications**  
   - **Lighting & Contrast** – Evaluate overall illumination, especially in hallways and stairways; ensure even lighting with minimal glare.  
   - **Task‑specific Lighting** – Consider task lighting for cooking, reading labels, and reading near‑work.  
   - **Edge Visibility** – Use high‑contrast strips or tactile markers on step edges, thresholds, and doorframes to improve detection.  
   - **Clutter Management** – Advise consistent storage placement and removal of unnecessary objects from walkways; assess whether a clutter‑free environment is feasible.  
   - **Consistent Furniture Placement** – Encourage predictable layout to aid spatial orientation.  

   **C. Mobility Strategies**  
   - **Referral to Orientation & Mobility (O&M)** for training in scanning, route learning, and safe turning techniques tailored to her field loss.  
   - **Assess** the use of a cane or other mobility aid if the patient’s confidence or safety is compromised during turning or stair negotiation.  

   **D. Assistive Technology / Non‑Visual Compensation**  
   - **Large‑print or audio labeling** for household items to reduce reliance on peripheral vision for locating objects.  
   - **High‑contrast labels** for medicine bottles and frequently used items to aid identification and reduce medication errors.  
   - **Potential use of an electronic assistive device** (e.g., smartphone app with audio alerts for obstacles) may be considered after a formal OT assessment of usability and training needs.  

   **E. Driving & Transport**  
   - **Education**: Reinforce the recommendation to refrain from driving until an Esterman test is completed; discuss alternative transport options (public transport, ride‑share, family assistance).  
   - **Support**: If driving cessation is imminent, collaborate with family to establish a safe vehicle removal plan and identify assistive technology (e.g., GPS navigation, auditory cues) for independent travel if possible.  

   **F. Support‑Person Education**  
   - Train carers or family members to keep walkways clear, maintain consistent furniture placement, and reinforce safe turning practices.  
   - Discuss the importance of avoiding clutter and ensuring adequate lighting during shared home activities.  

   **G. Follow‑Up & Communication**  
   - Coordinate with the optometrist/ophthalmologist on progress of field loss and any changes in prescription that may affect home‑based adaptations.  
   - If the ophthalmology review leads to surgical or medical changes (e.g., MIGS), reassess the impact on functional vision and update OT plan accordingly.  

5. **Falls Risk Level**  
   - **Moderate** – Progressive peripheral field loss and reported side‑collisions increase risk of near‑fall events, but no falls have yet occurred. Continued monitoring and proactive environmental and mobility interventions are warranted.

6. **Summary of Key OT Considerations**  
   - Prioritize assessment and modification of environmental lighting, edge visibility, and clutter to reduce collision risk.  
   - Evaluate and train safe turning, turning strategy, and stair negotiation, with referral to O&M if needed.  
   - Implement high‑contrast and large‑print labeling for medication and frequently used items to support safe ADLs.  
   - Educate support persons on maintaining clear pathways and consistent furniture arrangement; coordinate with eye‑care team for updates on visual status and driving safety.
