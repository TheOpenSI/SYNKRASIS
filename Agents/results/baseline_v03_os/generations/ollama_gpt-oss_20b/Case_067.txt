==============================
Case 067
==============================


1. **Patient Context**  
   - 79‑year‑old male, living with wife in a community dwelling.  
   - Recent fall on a poorly lit outdoor step; now reluctant to go outside at dusk.  
   - Dense bilateral cataract with severe blur, glare and markedly reduced contrast sensitivity.  
   - Uses old bifocal spectacles full‑time; stopped driving one month ago.  
   - Relies on wife for transport and for reading small print.  
   - Medical history: hypertension and COPD (inhalers); no other ocular surgery.  


2. **Key Vision Issues**  
   - **Reduced visual clarity** (6/120 OD, 6/96 OS at distance; 6/60 best‑corrected) – impairs edge detection.  
   - **Severe glare** – worsens in low‑light outdoor environments.  
   - **Markedly low contrast sensitivity** – not measurable; limits visibility of subtle step‑edges and low‑contrast signage.  
   - **Inconsistent bifocal use** – may obscure step‑edges during stair negotiation.  


3. **Functional Impact**  

| Domain | Potential Impact (inferred) | Evidence for Assessment |
|--------|----------------------------|-------------------------|
| **Mobility & Falls** | Poor step‑edge visibility in low light → risk of trips/falls; reluctance to walk outdoors at dusk. | Recent fall on poorly lit step; glare complaint. |
| **ADLs/IADLs** | Difficulty reading small print (medication labels, mail, instructions) → potential medication mis‑management, safety risk. | Wife reports reliance on her for reading small print. |
| **Participation** | Reduced outdoor mobility → limited social participation, possible isolation. | Reported reluctance to go outside at dusk. |
| **Environmental Interaction** | Inadequate indoor/outdoor lighting and contrast at step edges → increased visual strain and risk. | Advice for path/entry lighting, high‑contrast step edging noted in plan. |


4. **Vision‑Informed OT Considerations**

- **Mobility Strategies**  
  - *Assess* outdoor step‑edge detection, stair negotiation, and kerb recognition in varying light.  
  - *Consider* referral for Orientation & Mobility (O&M) training if step‑edge difficulties persist post‑surgery.  
  - *Plan* for high‑contrast step edging and clear, glare‑controlled lighting at outdoor entrances and stairways.  

- **Home Environment**  
  - *Evaluate* current lighting levels (ambient and task) and glare sources in entryways, stairwell, and outdoor patios.  
  - *Recommend* installation or adjustment of brighter, evenly distributed fixtures; use light‑reflective or colored trim to delineate edges.  
  - *Advise* the couple to keep pathways clear of clutter, maintain consistent furniture placement to aid navigation.  

- **Task Adaptations**  
  - *Reading* small print:  
    - Assess need for larger‑print materials, high‑contrast labeling, and voice‑output devices.  
    - Evaluate medication management strategy (e.g., pill organizer with tactile cues).  
  - *Bifocal use*:  
    - *Assess* whether removing bifocals while stepping onto stairs improves edge visibility; train the patient to switch lenses at appropriate moments.  

- **Assistive Technology / Non‑Visual Compensation**  
  - *Consider* magnification tools (handheld or desktop) in consultation with a low‑vision specialist.  
  - *Explore* tactile or auditory cues for identifying step edges (e.g., textured treads).  

- **Education & Support**  
  - Instruct wife on:  
    - Maintaining clear, well‑lit pathways; removing obstacles.  
    - Supporting safe stair navigation (e.g., holding the handrail, guiding steps).  
    - Recognising signs of increased fall risk and when to seek further assessment.  
  - Provide guidance on alternative transport options (public transport, rideshare, community shuttles) given driving cessation.  

- **Safety & Emergency Communication**  
  - *Assess* need for a fall‑alarm or emergency notification system if the patient lives alone or experiences frequent falls.  

- **Interdisciplinary Collaboration**  
  - Liaise with ophthalmology/low‑vision service:  
    - Post‑cataract‑surgery visual rehabilitation plan.  
    - Discuss potential need for updated assistive technology once visual acuity improves.  

- **Clinical Follow‑up**  
  - Re‑evaluate functional mobility and reading tasks after cataract surgery and any visual rehabilitation.  

5. **Falls Risk Level**  
   **High** – Severe visual blur and glare in low light, marked contrast loss, recent fall on poorly lit step, and current reliance on wife for transport all converge to elevate vision‑related fall risk.  


6. **Key OT Priorities (Summary)**  
   - **Assess** outdoor mobility and step‑edge detection; plan for high‑contrast edging and glare‑controlled lighting.  
   - **Adapt** reading tasks with larger print, high contrast, and voice‑output aids to support medication and information access.  
   - **Educate** wife and patient on safe stair use, consistent lighting, and obstacle removal; coordinate with O&M if needed.  
   - **Plan** for post‑surgical visual rehabilitation and re‑assessment of functional safety.  

*All recommendations are based on documented vision findings and inferred functional implications; further OT assessment is recommended to confirm individual needs.*
