==============================
Case 072
==============================

1. Patient Context  
82-year-old female with advanced dry AMD and bilateral geographic atrophy causing central vision loss. Lives independently with occasional family support. Reports difficulty reading medication labels, meal preparation challenges, and a single kitchen stumble likely due to low-contrast object detection. Uses bifocals and a hand magnifier. No current falls recurrence. Non-driver. Comorbidities include hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, and osteoporosis.

---

2. Key Vision Issues  
- **Central vision loss**: Impairs reading, face recognition, and direct-ahead object identification (e.g., dark saucepan on patterned floor).  
- **Reduced distance/near acuity**: Distance VA OD 6/60, OS 6/30; near VA N12 habitual, N6 with high add.  
- **Contrast sensitivity deficits**: Likely contributes to difficulty detecting low-contrast objects and patterned surfaces.  
- **Binocular vision compromise**: 4 XP near (exophoria), affecting near task stability and comfort.  

---

3. Functional Impact  

**Mobility and Falls**  
- Increased risk of tripping on low-contrast or patterned surfaces (demonstrated by kitchen stumble).  
- Potential difficulty navigating unfamiliar environments due to reduced direct-ahead object detection.  

**ADLs/IADLs**  
- Medication management: May struggle with small text on labels; current magnifier use may be insufficient for fine print.  
- Meal preparation: Difficulty reading labels, identifying ingredients, or judging food placement.  
- Face recognition: May affect social participation and safety (e.g., identifying caregivers).  

**Environmental Interaction**  
- Patterned surfaces (e.g., floor mats) and low-contrast objects (e.g., dark saucepans) create visual confusion.  
- Inconsistent lighting and glare may exacerbate central vision loss, particularly in kitchens and during near tasks.  

---

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations  

**Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**  
- **Assess kitchen and common areas** for clutter, patterned surfaces, and low-contrast objects (e.g., dark pots on patterned floors).  
- Recommend **high-contrast markings** on frequently used objects (e.g., handles, appliances) and removal of patterned floor mats where feasible.  
- Address **task lighting**: Evaluate kitchen and reading areas for even, glare-free illumination to support near and distance tasks.  

**Task Adaptations**  
- **Medication management**: Assess need for larger-print labels, color-coding, or talking dispensers. Review magnifier efficacy for medication identification.  
- **Meal preparation**: Suggest using high-contrast containers, labeled jars, and task-specific lighting to improve visibility of ingredients.  

**Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies**  
- **Magnification review**: Evaluate whether handheld magnifier or a stand magnifier with integrated lighting better supports reading tasks.  
- **Contrast enhancement**: Recommend high-contrast color schemes for kitchen surfaces (e.g., light-colored countertops with dark backsplashes).  

**Education and Support**  
- Educate patient/family on **avoiding patterned surfaces** and **improving contrast** in high-risk areas.  
- Discuss strategies for **task timing** during periods of better visual function (e.g., early morning if vision fluctuates).  

**Clinical Follow-up / Communication**  
- Liaise with low-vision service for magnification and lighting optimization.  
- Monitor for new visual field changes or functional decline during OT assessment.  

---

5. Falls Risk Level  
**Moderate**: Central vision loss and contrast sensitivity deficits increase tripping risk on low-contrast/patterned surfaces, compounded by osteoporosis. One documented fall with no recurrence reported.  

---

6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings  
- Assess kitchen environment for patterned surfaces, low-contrast objects, and lighting adequacy to reduce fall risk.  
- Evaluate medication/label identification strategies and meal-preparation adaptations for safety and independence.  
- Review magnifier efficacy for near tasks and consider low-vision service liaison for optimized devices.  
- Educate on environmental contrast modification and task-specific lighting to enhance functional vision.
