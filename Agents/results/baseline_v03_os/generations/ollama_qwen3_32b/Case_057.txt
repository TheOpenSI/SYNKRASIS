==============================  
Case 057  
==============================  

### 1. Patient Context  
- **Age**: 84-year-old male with stroke history (2 years ago), hypertension, arthritis, and hearing impairment.  
- **Care Setting**: Community optometry practice post-rehabilitation team referral.  
- **Vision Impairment**: Right homonymous hemianopia and bilateral cataracts contributing to glare, blur, and recurrent falls.  
- **Functional Context**: Two confirmed falls in 4 months (doorway turn, bright-to-shaded step transition). Uses bifocals full-time. Family reports missing objects on the right side and worsening glare/blur.  

---

### 2. Key Vision Issues  
- **Right homonymous hemianopia**: Visual field loss on the right in both eyes, likely contributing to missed objects/walls during mobility and turning.  
- **Bilateral cataracts**: Glare, blur, and reduced contrast sensitivity (1.05 log units Pelli-Robson), exacerbating difficulty in low-contrast environments and transitions between lighting levels.  
- **Bifocal use**: Potential disruption to depth perception and step negotiation, especially if worn during stair navigation.  

---

### 3. Functional Impact  

#### **Mobility and Falls**  
- **Obstacle detection**: Risk of missing objects/walls on the right side during turning or navigation.  
- **Lighting transitions**: Falls risk during bright-to-shaded step transitions (e.g., entryways, outdoor-to-indoor movements).  
- **Contrast sensitivity**: Reduced ability to discern step edges, surface changes, or low-contrast hazards.  
- **Bifocal use**: Potential instability during stair negotiation, increasing fall risk.  

#### **ADLs/IADLs**  
- **Meal preparation**: Difficulty locating ingredients/objects on the right visual field.  
- **Reading/labels**: Poor acuity (OU 6/24) and contrast sensitivity may hinder reading small print or identifying household objects.  
- **Technology use**: Challenges with digital devices (e.g., phones) due to reduced vision and glare.  

#### **Environmental Interaction**  
- **Lighting**: Need for brighter, glare-free lighting in high-risk areas (e.g., entryways, kitchens).  
- **Contrast**: High-contrast step edges and surfaces to compensate for reduced contrast sensitivity.  
- **Furniture placement**: Consistent, clutter-free layouts to reduce navigation errors.  

---

### 4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations  

#### **Mobility Strategies**  
- **Assess scanning techniques**: Evaluate and train right-side scanning strategies to compensate for hemianopia, particularly during turning or doorway navigation.  
- **O&M referral**: Consider specialist assessment for safe community mobility, including route learning and non-visual strategies for complex environments.  
- **Bifocal use**: Review current practices; advise against bifocal use during stair negotiation due to potential depth-perception disruption.  

#### **Environmental Modifications**  
- **Lighting and contrast**:  
  - Install brighter, glare-controlled lighting in entryways and transitional spaces (e.g., shaded-to-sunlit areas).  
  - Apply high-contrast tape to step edges and surface changes.  
- **Clutter management**: Ensure consistent, uncluttered pathways to reduce obstacle risk.  

#### **Task Adaptations**  
- **ADLs**:  
  - Position frequently used items in the left visual field (non-affected side).  
  - Use high-contrast labels for medications, appliances, and household objects.  
- **Reading**: Trial large-print materials, magnification tools, or text-to-speech technology.  

#### **Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies**  
- **Mobility cane**: Trial for tactile/environmental feedback during community mobility.  
- **Contrast enhancement**: Use of high-contrast color schemes for kitchen tools, medication containers, and clothing.  
- **Digital accessibility**: Explore phone/tablet settings (e.g., screen magnification, voice commands).  

#### **Education and Support**  
- **Caregiver training**: Educate wife on reinforcing scanning strategies, maintaining predictable environments, and avoiding visual clutter.  
- **Safety education**: Reinforce caution during lighting transitions (e.g., wearing a hat to reduce glare).  
- **Support review**: Assess need for formal support/services to address mobility or ADL assistance.  

---

### 5. Falls Risk Level  
**High**: Recurrent falls (2 in 4 months), hemianopia, cataract-related glare/blur, reduced contrast sensitivity, and environmental risk factors (lighting transitions, step negotiation).  

---

### 6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings  
- **Assess and train right-side scanning strategies** to mitigate hemianopia-related falls.  
- **Optimize lighting and contrast** in high-risk areas (e.g., entryways, steps) to improve obstacle detection.  
- **Review bifocal use during stair navigation** and trial mobility cane for community mobility.  
- **Evaluate ADL adaptations** (e.g., item placement, high-contrast labels) to support independence and safety.
