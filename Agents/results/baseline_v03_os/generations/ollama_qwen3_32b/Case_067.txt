==============================  
Case 067  
==============================  

1. **Patient Context**  
79-year-old male with dense bilateral cataracts causing severe visual blur, glare, and reduced contrast sensitivity. Lives with wife, who provides transport and assistance with small print. Stopped driving 1 month ago. Reports a fall on a poorly lit outdoor step and reluctance to go outside at dusk. Has hypertension and COPD.  

2. **Key Vision Issues**  
- **Severe bilateral visual acuity loss** (distance: 6/60–6/96; near: N32–N48) limits visibility of small print, step edges, and environmental hazards.  
- **Marked glare sensitivity** exacerbates difficulty in low-light environments (e.g., dusk, poorly lit patios).  
- **Reduced contrast sensitivity** likely contributes to poor step-edge detection, increasing fall risk.  
- **Inability to use current bifocals effectively** on stairs, as advised by optometry.  

3. **Functional Impact**  

**Mobility and Falls**  
- High risk of recurrent falls due to poor step-edge visibility in low illumination.  
- Reluctance to navigate outdoor environments at dusk may lead to reduced community participation and increased isolation.  
- Potential difficulty with stairs, kerbs, and road crossings due to glare and reduced contrast.  

**ADLs/IADLs**  
- Medication management may be compromised by inability to read small print.  
- Meal preparation could be challenging due to reduced near vision and glare when using appliances or reading labels.  
- Reliance on wife for tasks requiring fine visual detail (e.g., reading, using digital devices).  

**Environmental Interaction**  
- Requires consistent, high-contrast step edging and pathway lighting to reduce fall risk.  
- Glare from outdoor environments or reflective surfaces may further impair visibility.  

4. **Vision-Informed OT Considerations**  

**Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**  
- **Assess lighting in high-risk areas** (e.g., steps, pathways, kitchen/bathroom) for brightness, evenness, and glare. Recommend high-contrast step edging (e.g., non-slip, brightly colored) and task lighting for functional spaces.  
- **Review furniture placement and clutter** to ensure clear travel paths and minimize tripping hazards.  

**Lighting and Contrast**  
- **Evaluate indoor/outdoor lighting conditions** during times of highest visual difficulty (e.g., dusk). Suggest motion-sensor lighting or portable task lights for low-illumination tasks.  
- **Maximize contrast** between steps and flooring (e.g., tape, paint) and between objects and backgrounds (e.g., white plates on dark countertops).  

**Mobility Strategies**  
- **Refer to Orientation & Mobility (O&M) services** for assessment of safe outdoor navigation, including step-edge detection strategies, road-crossing techniques, and use of a long cane if indicated.  
- **Educate on avoiding bifocals on stairs**, as advised by optometry, to reduce tripping risk.  

**Task Adaptations / Assistive Technology**  
- **Assess medication management systems** for visibility of labels (e.g., large-print labeling, color-coding). Consider a talking pill organizer or digital reminders.  
- **Evaluate meal-preparation tasks** for visual demands (e.g., reading labels, using appliances). Recommend large-print recipes, high-contrast tools, or digital text-to-speech devices.  
- **Explore phone/digital accessibility features** (e.g., screen magnification, voice-to-text) to reduce reliance on small print.  

**Education and Support**  
- **Educate patient and wife** on environmental modifications (e.g., lighting, contrast) and fall-prevention strategies (e.g., avoiding stairs in low light).  
- **Discuss driving cessation implications** and explore alternative transport options (e.g., public transit, ride services) to maintain community access.  

**Clinical Follow-up / Communication**  
- **Coordinate with ophthalmology** to plan OT interventions post-cataract surgery, as visual improvements may alter functional needs.  
- **Monitor for post-surgery adaptation**, including re-evaluation of mobility safety and task-specific adaptations.  

5. **Falls Risk Level**  
**High** – Severe visual acuity loss, contrast sensitivity deficits, and documented fall on a poorly lit step indicate significant risk, exacerbated by glare and reluctance to navigate outdoor environments.  

6. **Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings**  
- Assess home lighting, contrast, and step-edge visibility to reduce fall risk.  
- Refer to O&M for safe outdoor mobility strategies, particularly for steps and road crossings.  
- Evaluate medication and meal-preparation tasks for visual accessibility and recommend adaptations (e.g., large-print labels, assistive technology).  
- Coordinate with ophthalmology to address functional needs post-cataract surgery.
