==============================  
Case 032  
==============================  

1. **Patient Context**  
   - 80-year-old male with left sixth nerve palsy causing binocular horizontal diplopia, worse at distance and in left gaze.  
   - Recurrent outdoor falls (2 documented) on uneven footpaths; self-reports difficulty judging kerb height and closing one eye during car park navigation.  
   - Diabetes, hypertension, hypercholesterolaemia; stopped driving due to frequent diplopia.  
   - Lives with wife; reduced independent outdoor mobility.  

2. **Key Vision Issues**  
   - **Binocular diplopia at distance**: Worse in left gaze, likely contributing to instability and falls during outdoor navigation.  
   - **Loss of functional stereopsis**: Impairs depth judgment for kerbs, steps, and spatial navigation.  
   - **Left abduction deficit**: Reduces visual field coverage during leftward gaze, increasing risk of collisions or tripping.  
   - **Bifocal use**: May exacerbate instability during walking tasks due to reduced distance clarity and stereopsis.  

3. **Functional Impact**  

   **Mobility and Falls**  
   - Impaired depth perception and binocular alignment likely increase risk of tripping on kerbs, steps, or uneven surfaces.  
   - Left gaze diplopia may hinder obstacle detection during leftward turns or navigation in crowded outdoor environments.  
   - History of falls and self-reported reliance on closing one eye for stability suggest high fall risk during community mobility.  

   **ADLs/IADLs**  
   - Depth judgment deficits may affect pouring, meal preparation, and reading labels at a distance.  
   - Reduced contrast sensitivity (mildly reduced) may complicate locating objects or reading in low-light conditions.  

   **Environmental Interaction**  
   - Poor distance visual acuity (6/15 with bifocals) and diplopia likely hinder safe navigation of unfamiliar or cluttered environments.  
   - Glare or shadowing from outdoor lighting may further compromise visibility during mobility tasks.  

4. **Vision-Informed OT Considerations**  

   **Mobility Strategies**  
   - **Assess outdoor mobility patterns**: Evaluate how diplopia and left gaze deficit affect kerb negotiation, turning, and obstacle detection.  
   - **Recommend temporary occlusion/prism use**: As per optometry management to reduce diplopia during walking, pending review of alignment.  
   - **Consider O&M referral**: For training in scanning strategies, community navigation, and safe kerb/step negotiation.  

   **Environmental Modifications / Home Environment**  
   - **Review home lighting**: Ensure even illumination for steps, pathways, and outdoor exits to reduce shadowing/glare.  
   - **Enhance contrast at transitions**: Use high-contrast tape or markings on kerbs, stairs, and edges for depth cueing.  
   - **Assess footwear and surface hazards**: Address uneven surfaces or clutter in frequently walked areas.  

   **Task Adaptations**  
   - **Modify walking tasks**: Recommend single-vision distance correction (as prescribed) for outdoor mobility to reduce bifocal-induced instability.  
   - **Adapt pouring and meal prep**: Use measuring tools with tactile markers or verbal cues to compensate for depth judgment deficits.  
   - **Review reading strategies**: Consider large-print materials or digital text-to-speech for low-contrast labels.  

   **Education and Support**  
   - **Educate care partner**: Train wife in reinforcing safe mobility strategies (e.g., monitoring for closed-eye use, ensuring clear walkways).  
   - **Discuss driving cessation**: Address adjustment to loss of independence and explore alternative transport options.  
   - **Liaise with GP/low-vision services**: For systemic management of microvascular risk factors and potential future low-vision support.  

5. **Falls Risk Level**  
   **High**: Recurrent outdoor falls, documented left gaze diplopia, and impaired depth perception create significant risk for tripping on kerbs, steps, or uneven surfaces.  

6. **Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings**  
   - Prioritize assessment of outdoor mobility safety with focus on kerb negotiation, left gaze navigation, and prism/occlusion use.  
   - Implement home environmental modifications (lighting, contrast) to mitigate fall risk during transitions.  
   - Adapt ADLs (pouring, meal prep) to compensate for depth judgment deficits.  
   - Coordinate with O&M and low-vision services for community mobility training and systemic risk factor management.
