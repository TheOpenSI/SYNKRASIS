==============================  
Case 046  
==============================  

1. **Patient Context**  
   - 74-year-old male with central vision loss OS due to branch retinal vein occlusion with macular oedema.  
   - Community-dwelling, referred by GP after a single fall on a low kerb and new reading difficulty.  
   - Uses separate distance and near spectacles; reduced driving due to left eye impairment.  
   - Medical comorbidities: hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, atrial fibrillation.  

2. **Key Vision Issues**  
   - **Central vision loss OS**: Reduced acuity (6/30 uncorrected, 6/24 best corrected) and contrast sensitivity (1.20 log units), affecting reading small print and price labels.  
   - **Binocular dysfunction**: 4 XP near; limited stereoacuity (80”) due to central vision loss, likely impairing depth judgement for steps/kerbs.  
   - **Contrast sensitivity reduction**: May hinder edge detection (e.g., kerbs, steps) and object identification in low-contrast environments.  
   - **Unilateral central defect**: Compromises visual field integration for navigation and task safety.  

3. **Functional Impact**  

   **Mobility and Falls**  
   - Increased risk for misjudging kerbs/steps due to reduced depth perception and contrast sensitivity.  
   - Potential difficulty navigating unfamiliar or cluttered environments, especially under low-light or transitional lighting conditions.  

   **ADLs/IADLs**  
   - Reading challenges with small print (e.g., medication labels, price tags, documents).  
   - Meal preparation risks: difficulty identifying ingredients, measuring quantities, or reading instructions.  
   - Medication management risks: potential errors in identifying pills or reading dosing instructions.  

   **Environmental Interaction**  
   - Lighting and contrast modifications needed to enhance visibility of hazards (e.g., steps, floor transitions).  
   - Visual clutter (e.g., patterned rugs, low-contrast furniture) may exacerbate navigation difficulties.  

4. **Vision-Informed OT Considerations**  

   **Household Management**  
   - **Environmental Assessment**: Evaluate home for step/kerb hazards, lighting adequacy, and clutter. Recommend high-contrast markings on steps and tactile cues for floor transitions.  
   - **Lighting and Contrast**: Assess task lighting for reading and cooking areas. Suggest warm, even lighting to reduce glare and enhance contrast for labels and small text.  

   **Mobility Strategies**  
   - **O&M Referral**: Consider for assessing community mobility safety, particularly for kerb negotiation and unfamiliar routes.  
   - **Compensatory Techniques**: Train in scanning strategies for step/kerb detection and use of a cane if falls risk escalates.  

   **Task Adaptations**  
   - **Reading and Label Identification**: Assess need for magnification (e.g., handheld or stand magnifiers) and high-contrast tools (e.g., label readers, large-print materials).  
   - **Medication Management**: Evaluate current systems; consider pill organizers with color/size differentiation or digital reminders.  

   **Education and Support**  
   - **Patient Education**: Reinforce caution on steps/kerbs, consistent spectacle use, and task timing during periods of better vision (e.g., avoiding visually demanding tasks during fatigue).  
   - **Caregiver Support**: If applicable, educate family on maintaining clear pathways and reinforcing safe mobility practices.  

   **Driving and Transport**  
   - Monitor driving safety; assess alternatives (e.g., public transport, ride services) if vision stabilizes but remains insufficient for safe sign recognition.  

5. **Falls Risk Level**  
   **Moderate**: Central vision loss and reduced contrast sensitivity increase kerb/step misjudgment risk, compounded by a single fall and binocular dysfunction.  

6. **Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings**  
   - Prioritize environmental modifications (high-contrast step markings, improved task lighting) to reduce falls risk.  
   - Assess reading and medication management strategies to mitigate functional limitations.  
   - Consider O&M evaluation for community mobility safety and training in compensatory techniques.  
   - Educate on safe task performance and spectacle use, with caregiver involvement if needed.  

---  
*Note: Recommendations are contingent on further OT assessment of the patient’s environment, task performance, and support system.*
