==============================
Case 067
==============================


1. Patient Context

- 79-year-old male, community (optometry) setting; GP-referred following a recent fall on a poorly lit outdoor step.
- Vision history: progressive dense bilateral cataracts with severe blur and glare; markedly reduced contrast sensitivity; rapid worsening over the past 3 months.
- Functional status: relies on his wife for transport and reading small print; stopped driving about 1 month ago due to vision and glare.
- Spectacles: currently uses old bifocals full-time; patient advised updated spectacles are unlikely to meaningfully restore vision.
- Environment and risk: fall occurred outdoors on a dimly lit step; patient reports reluctance to go outside at dusk.
- Ocular health and systemic context: hypertension and COPD; no diplopia; no recent ocular surgery; best-corrected VA still heavily reduced; plan includes routine cataract referral with priority for functional limitation.

2. Key Vision Issues

- Dense cataracts with glare and markedly reduced contrast sensitivity causing severe blur; functional impact likely includes difficulty discerning step edges and changes in elevation, especially in low illumination.
- Poor visibility of edges/depth in outdoor environments at dusk or in glare conditions; contributes to stepping risk and avoidance of outdoor activity.
- Limited ability to view peripheral surroundings due to media opacity; may reduce hazard detection on walking routes.
- Full-time use of bifocals on stairs/outdoors may worsen edge interpretation or accommodation to changing distances; recommendation already given to avoid bifocals on stairs where possible.
- Driving has ceased due to vision/glare; indicates a reduction in independent mobility and reliance on a caregiver, with potential impact on participation and access.
- Vision findings are unlikely to be fully corrected by spectacles at this stage; cataract surgery is planned.

3. Functional Impact

Mobility and Falls
- High risk of future falls related to poor step-edge visibility in low light and glare; recent fall confirms vision-related mobility risk with outdoor steps.
- Reluctance to walk at dusk may reduce community access and activity participation, potentially contributing to deconditioning and social isolation.

ADLs/IADLs
- Reading small print and identifying medication/labels may be challenging; patient currently relies on wife for reading tasks.
- General task performance that depends on clear distance/contrast vision (e.g., locating objects, navigating cluttered environments) may be affected.

Participation / Confidence / Roles
- Loss of driving reduces independent mobility; increased reliance on spouse for transportation may affect participation in social activities and routine errands.

Environmental Interaction
- Home and outdoor lighting, glare, and step-edge visibility are central to safe navigation; patient’s plan includes lighting improvements and high-contrast edging.

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations

Self-care
- Assess medication management and reading of labels or forms; consider whether non-visual strategies or larger-print/clear labeling could support safe self-management while vision is improving.

Mobility Strategies
- Recommend formal Orientation & Mobility (O&M) assessment/training to address safe community mobility in low-light and glare conditions, including route assessment and strategies for negotiating steps and outdoor thresholds. Do not prescribe a fixed mobility script; pursue individualized training goals.
- Consider non-visual strategies to support navigation (e.g., consistent environmental cues, tactile edge identification) in collaboration with O&M if appropriate.

Environmental Modifications / Home Environment
- Lighting and lighting placement: assess whether evening/entry lighting can be optimized to reduce glare and shadows along common pathways.
- Contrast and edge visibility: plan for high-contrast step edging or markings and reduction of visual clutter along entryways and paths to outdoor areas.
- Navigation set-up: ensure consistent and predictable layout in immediate home environment to reduce tripping risks when vision is most compromised.

Lighting and Contrast
- Emphasize even, shadow-free illumination on travel paths and steps; balance glare control with adequate illumination to enhance edge visibility without causing adaptation difficulties.

Task Adaptations
- Evaluate medication management and other near-distance tasks that require clarity of detail; develop non-visual or larger-print labeling plans and routine checks as interim strategies pending cataract improvement.
- Explore non-visual cues or device-assisted options for daily tasks that are currently visually demanding.

Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies
- Discuss non-visual strategies (e.g., tactile labeling, auditory cues) and potential use of accessible devices (audio reminders, large-print materials) aligned with patient goals and feasibility.
- Do not prescribe devices without alignment to goals and OT scope; coordinate with eye-care and low-vision services as appropriate.

Education and Support
- Educate patient and wife about safety strategies for dusk/dawn walking, the importance of consistent lighting, and avoiding stair navigation with older bifocals.
- Provide guidance on planning for cataract surgery recovery and post-operative safety measures; discuss transport/logistics with spouse.

Clinical Follow-up / Communication
- Facilitate coordination with ophthalmology for cataract management and potential low-vision input if vision remains limited post-surgery.
- Consider referral to low-vision services if functional limitations persist after medical/surgical management and impact on activities of daily living.

Driving / Transportation
- Document driving cessation and translate into OT-relevant implications: plan for alternate transportation, scheduling flexibility to maintain participation, and contingency strategies for shopping and appointments; ensure safety planning in collaboration with spouse and community supports.

Be mindful of feasibility
- Environmental and assistive strategies should consider the patient’s home environment, caregiver availability, and financial feasibility; modifications should be prioritized by safety impact and practicality.

5. Falls Risk Level

Falls risk related to vision impairment: High
- Rationale: documented fall on a poorly lit outdoor step with vision-related edge detection difficulties; recent fall history linked to glare/low-light edge visibility; cataract-related impairment is severe and not fully correctable with spectacles.

6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings

- Prioritize mobility safety planning: arrange O&M assessment to address safe navigation in low-light and glare, outdoor step negotiation, and route planning with reduced vision.
- Environmental and lighting modifications: assess and implement lighting and high-contrast edge enhancements (both at home entry and along outdoor routes) to improve step-edge visibility and reduce shadows/glare.
- Task adaptation and supports: evaluate medication management and other near/far tasks for alternative labeling or non-visual strategies; consider non-visual cueing and accessible devices as interim supports.
- Transportation and participation: support planning for ongoing community participation through alternative transport options and caregiver-supported activities while maintaining safety.
- Coordination with eye-care: acknowledge cataract management plan; liaise with optometry/ophthalmology and, if needed, low-vision services to optimize functional independence during the medical management window.

End of Case 067 OT Summary
