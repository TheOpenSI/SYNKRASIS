==============================
Case 057
==============================

1. Patient Context

- 84-year-old man, community-daced optometry setting; recently referred after recurrent falls following stroke rehab.
- Medical history: prior stroke with right homonymous hemianopia; bilateral cataracts with gradual glare/blur over past year; arthritis; hearing impairment; on antihypertensive and antiplatelet therapy.
- Functional context: lives with wife; non-driver; receives rehabilitation follow-up for mobility and home safety.
- Vision-related functional note from history: family reports missing walls/objects on the right side; patient reports hazy vision and right-sided space awareness difficulties, particularly when moving between bright and shaded environments.
- Current eyewear: full-time bifocals.
- Falls history: two confirmed falls in the past four months (one turning toward a doorway; one when moving from bright sunlight into a shaded entry).

2. Key Vision Issues

- Right homonymous hemianopia with bilateral cataracts causing reduced acuity and worsened contrast, together contributing to right-side field loss and overall haziness in scenes.
- Significantly reduced contrast sensitivity and impaired ability to detect right-sided environmental details (walls/objects) in just-perceptible to bright-to-shaded transitions.
- Glare sensitivity and slower processing of scenes due to cataracts, compounding mobility challenges in complex environments.
- Reading-related tasks (near acuity) and color discrimination are suboptimal, though these are secondary to field loss and acuity issues.
- Visual function is notably worse with bright-to-dark transitions and in visually cluttered or unfamiliar environments; prism trial (Pelli prisms) is planned to expand right-field awareness, pending optometric management.
- Eye-care plan includes cataract referral and consideration of prism therapy; brighter entry lighting and high-contrast edge cues are advised; avoidance of bifocals on stairs if possible; referral to low-vision services for mobility aids (cane) is planned.

3. Functional Impact

Mobility and Falls
- Right-sided field loss and reduced clarity/contrast likely impair detection of obstacles, edges, and doorway thresholds on the right, increasing risk during turning, entering/exiting spaces, and negotiating doorways.
- Glare and rapid lighting transitions (sunlight to shaded entry) can destabilize orientation and mobility, particularly when moving through entryways or stairs.
- Reported falls during turning toward a doorway and during a transition from bright to shaded environments align with risks in outdoor and threshold contexts.
- Bilateral cataracts contribute to overall haziness and slower scene processing, impacting safe, efficient ambulation in unfamiliar or visually complex areas.

ADLs/IADLs
- Reading/identifying medication and instruction labels may be challenging due to reduced acuity and contrast; near tasks are affected but less critical than mobility violations linked to field loss.
- Potential difficulty with shopping, kitchen tasks, and informational reading when scenes involve rightward dispersion of items or clutter, given scanning limitations.
- Lighting changes and glare could affect task safety in self-care and domestic tasks, particularly in entry/egress and transitional spaces.

Participation / Confidence / Roles
- Mobility confidence is likely reduced in visually complex environments and during transitions (e.g., doorways, bright-to-shade changes); participation in community activities and independent mobility could be limited without supports.
- Visual impairment may influence willingness to navigate unfamiliar environments or times of day with variable lighting.

Environmental Interaction
- Visual clutter, glare, shading, and inconsistent lighting may compromise edge/step visibility and overall navigation.
- Right-sided space perception is asymmetrically affected, suggesting environmental arrangement and hazard placement should be tailored to support safer movement.

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations

Self-care
- Assess ability to read medication labels and perform near tasks reliably; consider how lighting, print size, and contrast affect medication management and form completion.
- Consider contingency strategies for reading instructions within home tasks if acuity/contrast remains limiting.

Household Management
- Review home environment for safety around thresholds, doorways, and stairs, prioritizing right-side edge detection and obstacle awareness.
- Recommend environmental setups to support consistent navigation (see Environmental Modifications below).

Environmental Modifications / Lighting
- Assess entryway lighting, glare control, and step-edge visibility; implement functional modifications such as brighter, even lighting at entry points and high-contrast, clearly marked step edges.
- Ensure clear contrasts at edges and pathways to aid right-side detection; reduce visual clutter in critical travel routes.
- Advise against abrupt lighting transitions where possible; consider task lighting for near tasks to support reading and fine-motor tasks.

Mobility Strategies
- Consider involvement of orientation and mobility (O&M) services to tailor scanning and route strategies for right-field loss; movement adaptations should emphasize safe rightward exploration and safe turning.
- Given plan for prism therapy, OT should coordinate with the eye-care team to monitor how field expansion affects mobility in real-world settings and adapt environment and tasks accordingly.
- Promote use of a mobility cane as indicated by low-vision services to support obstacle detection and spatial orientation; ensure cane-use education is aligned with family/caregiver support.

Task Adaptations
- Use high-contrast, large-print labeling for medications and household items; organize frequently used items in predictable, right-ward-aligned locations to reduce search demands.
- When possible, align task setup to reduce required rightward scanning (e.g., place critical items within closer, more central visual fields during daily activities).

Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies
- Continue with non-visual strategies supported by low-vision services: tactile cues, enhanced lighting, and auditory prompts where appropriate; consider integrating talking devices or non-visual cues for essential information when reading is unreliable.
- Explore coordinated use of prism therapy results with practical mobility strategies and environmental modifications.

Education and Support
- Educate patient and spouse on safe guiding approaches, environmental safety (e.g., keeping walkways clear, consistent furniture placement), and the importance of pacing during transitions.
- Provide guidance on when to seek assistance during busy or visually challenging tasks and how to communicate falls risk to caregivers.
- Liaise with the rehabilitation team regarding ongoing mobility training, home-safety revisits, and the introduction of any assistive devices.

Clinical Follow-up / Communication
- Maintain communication with optometry and rehabilitation team about prism trial outcomes, cataract management, and eye-care recommendations.
- Ensure OT is informed of any changes in vision status, lighting needs, or mobility confidence to adjust OT considerations accordingly.

Driving / Transport
- Not driving currently; focus remains on maintaining safe community mobility and accessing transportation as needed, supported by low-vision aids and mobility training.

Eye-wear considerations
- Acknowledge that bifocals may impede mobility on stairs or in high-glare situations; coordinate with optometry to discuss mobility-appropriate eyewear strategies (for example, possible use of single-vision lenses for walking tasks) without prescribing or altering medical management.

Low-vision service coordination
- Facilitate linkage to Vision Australia/Guide Dogs Australia for mobility cane and related low-vision supports; coordinate with the OT plan to integrate non-visual strategies as needed.

Falls risk considerations
- Given documented falls linked to right-field loss and glare/contrast issues, prioritize home-entry and threshold safety, right-side obstacle detection, and lighting/contrast improvements as high-priority areas for assessment and intervention.

5. Falls Risk Level

- Falls risk level: High
- Rationale: Documented recurrent falls with direct attribution to right-sided field loss and reduced contrast/glare sensitivity, in the context of history of stroke and progressive visual impairment; multiple interacting visual impairments materially contributing to mobility risk.

6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings

- Major vision drivers: right homonymous hemianopia plus bilateral cataracts with glare/contrast reduction significantly impact right-side detection, doorway/threshold navigation, and safe ambulation in glare-heavy and transitional environments. Priority areas include environment safety around entryways and stairs, and right-side obstacle detection during mobility.
- Mobility-focused actions: consider OT coordination with O&M services; support prism therapy results and monitor functional impact in real-world settings; encourage cane use where appropriate; emphasize safe scanning strategies and edge/step visibility in environments with glare or bright-to-shadow transitions.
- Task adaptations: implement high-contrast, large-print materials for near tasks such as medication management and reading instructions; reorganize commonly used items to reduce rightward search demands; minimize reliance on bifocals for mobility when stairs or transition tasks are involved.
- Environmental and lighting considerations: ensure brighter, glare-controlled entry lighting with high-contrast step edges; minimize clutter and ensure consistent layouts in travel paths; address shadowing and lighting transitions to improve edge detection.
- Education and care coordination: educate patient and caregiver on safety practices, guiding approaches, and progression with vision rehabilitation; coordinate with optometry for lens and prism decisions; plan for low-vision service involvement to optimize mobility aids and strategies.
- Overall care coordination: maintain multidisciplinary communication with optometry, rehabilitation team, and low-vision services; monitor functional responses to any changes in sensory status or mobility confidence; adjust OT considerations based on prism trial outcomes and cataract management.

End of Case 057 OT summary.
