==============================
Case 064
==============================


1. Patient Context

- 82-year-old man in a rehabilitation outpatient clinic, following a right occipital stroke with left homonymous hemianopia.
- Falls history: one fall at home shortly after discharge when turning into a doorway on the left; another fall risk context noted during rehabilitation after stroke.
- Vision-related functional concerns: misses leftward spatial information (doorways, left side of reading lines, crowded spaces); reports left-side scanning difficulty contributing to collisions and slower navigation.
- Visual function snapshot: central acuity largely preserved with bifocal wear; reduced contrast sensitivity; left hemianopia confirmed; full ocular motility with no diplopia; reading and crowded-environment fatigue noted.
- Ongoing therapy: engaged with occupational therapy and physiotherapy for mobility retraining.
- Medical context: stroke history; atrial fibrillation; hypertension; on anticoagulation and antihypertensive therapy.
- Driving: not driving currently; not driving without comprehensive medical/visual field clearance.
- Eyewear: bifocals used; IOLs OU.

2. Key Vision Issues

- Left homonymous hemianopia with preserved central acuity limits left-field awareness during mobility (turns, doorway navigation, crowded spaces) and is linked to left-side collisions and the recent fall.
- Reduced contrast sensitivity increases difficulty distinguishing edges, steps, and doorway cues in low-contrast environments and in busy settings.
- Reading-related scanning difficulty (difficulty locating the start of lines) suggests left-field scanning impairment affecting near tasks like reading forms or schedules.
- Near/reading comfort may be impacted by bifocal use (possible split between distance and near fields) though motility is intact; no diplopia.
- Ocular health supports a relatively stable functional vision profile aside from the hemianopia; colour vision is normal.

3. Functional Impact

Mobility and Falls
- Left-field loss with impaired leftward scanning increases risk when turning into doorways, negotiating thresholds, and navigating in unfamiliar or crowded environments.
- Documented fall risk associated with left-sided impairment; high-risk situations include doorway entry, turning in tight spaces, and busy indoor areas.

ADLs/IADLs
- Reading and locating information on the left side (e.g., line starts when reading; forms or labels with left-justified information) may require extra effort and compensatory strategies.
- Potential challenges locating or organizing objects positioned toward the left in home or community environments.

Participation / Confidence / Roles
- Busy environments and left-field challenges may reduce participation in some activities (e.g., shopping, social events) due to fatigue and fear of collision.
- Ongoing mobility retraining is appropriate and may influence participation decisions and confidence.

Environmental Interaction
- Lighting, glare, and background clutter in indoor environments can compound left-field scanning difficulties and fatigue.
- Doorway thresholds and left-edge obstacles (frames, furniture) present predictable hazards without targeted environmental cues.

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations

Self-care
- Assess tasks requiring leftward search (reading labels, forms, medication cues, shelf items) and implement accommodations such as high-contrast, large-print materials and strategic arrangement of essential items within the right/upward field when possible.
- Consider task simplification or stepwise task analyses for near-work that commonly extends into the left field.

Environmental Modifications / Lighting
- Assess and optimize lighting quality, glare control, and even illumination to minimize shadows that could obscure left-field perceptions.
- Introduce or reinforce high-contrast environmental cues at key transitions (doorways, thresholds, stairs) and ensure clear, uncluttered travel paths.
- Encourage consistent furniture placement and organization to reduce unpredictable leftward hazards.

Mobility Strategies
- Recommend formal Occupational Therapy–led or Orientation & Mobility (O&M) input to tailor scanning strategies, route planning, and hazard awareness for left-field loss in both known and new environments.
- Consider a mobility cane as a non-visual cue and safety aid; discuss how and when to use it, and role in public/community spaces.
- Discuss use of a visual identification badge or other non-visual signals to help others recognize vision impairment in unfamiliar environments.
- Emphasize pre-movement leftward scanning before turns or doorway entry; avoid scripted routines and tailor strategies to the person’s environment and preferences.

Task Adaptations
- Develop simple, task-specific strategies: pause and scan left before continuing reading or walking; position critical information within the right-field or use large, high-contrast formats.
- For forms or schedules, provide left-side layout considerations or alternative formats (larger print, clear headings) to reduce leftward search demands.

Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies
- Explore high-contrast print, enlarged fonts, and clear, uncluttered displays for reading tasks.
- Leverage compatible device accessibility features (screen magnification, text-to-speech, simplified displays) if aligned with goals and feasible.
- Teach non-visual cues and environmental cues (e.g., tactile markers or audible prompts) where appropriate.

Education and Support
- Educate patient and rehabilitation team (including family) about left-hemispheric field loss and safe navigation strategies, especially around doorway turning and crowded spaces.
- Provide guidance on consistent routines and environmental setup to support safe ambulation and reduce fatigue.

Clinical Follow-up / Communication
- Ensure ongoing communication of vision findings with the rehabilitation team and primary care, with a planned review or re-assessment if functional status changes.

Falls Risk Level
- High: A documented fall linked to left-field loss, plus ongoing mobility risk in real-world environments.

Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings
- Prioritize O&M or vision-rehabilitation input to tailor scanning strategies, route planning, and hazard recognition for left-field loss.
- Implement environmental and task adaptations focused on left-space safety, including lighting, contrast, and clutter management, plus consider a cane and/or visual identification aids.
- Plan task adaptations for reading and form-related activities to minimize leftward search demands and leverage high-contrast/large-print formats.
- Emphasize patient and caregiver education on left-field scanning, doorway navigation, and strategies to maintain independence in daily and community activities, with a safety-focused follow-up plan.
