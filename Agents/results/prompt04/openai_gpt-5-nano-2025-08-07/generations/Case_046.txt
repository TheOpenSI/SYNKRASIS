Case 046
==============================

1. Patient Context
- 74-year-old male, community optometry patient referred by GP for possible retinal cause after new reading difficulty and a recent stumble on a low kerb.
- Diagnosis: left eye (OS) Branch retinal vein occlusion with macular oedema; right eye (OD) relatively preserved.
- Vision history: gradual reduction in central clarity OS over ~2 months; difficulty reading small print and identifying price labels.
- Medical context: hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, atrial fibrillation; on antihypertensives and anticoagulation.
- Functional status: drives locally but has reduced driving since left-eye worsening.
- Prior retinal treatment: none.
- Ocular findings: macula OS with oedema; intraretinal haemorrhages and hard exudates; OCT confirms central macular thickening. OD macula unremarkable. Central fixation OS less clear; binocular alignment normal at distance, mild exophoria at near; reduced stereo due to macular disease.
- Practical safety note: advised caution on steps and kerbs until vision stabilises; discussed avoiding driving if vision not adequate for hazard detection.

2. Key Vision Issues
- Central vision loss OS from BRVO with macular oedema, leading to decreased central clarity and difficulty with reading small print and identifying price labels. Near tasks at 40 cm are disproportionately affected.
- Reduced contrast sensitivity and limited binocular depth perception (stereo) primarily from OS macular involvement; this can impair edge/step detection and safe judging of elevation or distance in everyday tasks.
- OS central fixation impairment with relatively preserved peripheral fields OU; colour vision OS appears inconclusive due to macular disease.
- Practical safety cue from history: a kerb stumble while visually distracted by blurred vision OS, indicating real-world mobility risk in outdoor environments.
- Driving status affected (reduced driving) but not completely ceased; impact on community mobility and access to services.

3. Functional Impact
Falls and Mobility
- Falls risk: Moderate. Documented kerb-related stumble plus central vision loss increasing hazard awareness needs during outdoor ambulation and curb/edge negotiation; however, only one fall is documented and no ongoing fall history.
- Outdoor navigation may be more challenging in unfamiliar or low-contrast environments and at kerbs/steps, especially when lighting is suboptimal or glare is present.

ADLs/IADLs
- Reading-dependent tasks are affected (reading small print, price labels, forms). Near-vision difficulty may extend to medication labeling, shopping lists, and device instructions.
- Visual search tasks (locating items in clutter or crowded shelves) may be slower or less reliable due to reduced central vision and contrast sensitivity.

Participation / Confidence / Roles
- Driving reduced; local driving continues but with caution, potentially limiting independent travel to non-essential activities and affecting community participation.
- Reading and shopping independence may be affected, with possible impact on confidence performing everyday tasks away from home.

Environmental Interaction
- Vision fluctuations are not described; however, lighting, glare, and contrast could further influence performance in home and community settings, particularly for steps, kerbs, and street crossings.

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations
Self-care
- Assess ability to manage near tasks that rely on central vision (reading labels, forms, medication packaging). Consider non-visual strategies and, if appropriate, accessible formats (large print, high-contrast materials) in collaboration with eye-care providers.
- Education for safe medication identification and administration, including tactile labeling or high-contrast containers where possible.

Environmental Modifications / Lighting and Contrast
- Evaluate and address home and community lighting needs to optimise contrast and reduce glare during reading and navigation.
- Consider environmental strategies such as high-contrast markings on stairs/edges, well-lit entry/exit paths, and consistent furniture placement to aid orientation.

Mobility Strategies
- Given OS central vision loss and kerb-related fall risk, consider referral to Orientation & Mobility (O&M) for mobility safety assessment, route planning, and community travel strategies where independence is important.
- Emphasise non-visual strategies where appropriate (e.g., planning routes with familiar landmarks, using consistent cues) while avoiding prescriptive mobility techniques not within OT scope.

Task Adaptations
- Explore task analysis for reading-dependent activities (e.g., grocery shopping, price comparisons, reading instructions) and identify compensatory strategies (non-visual cues, tactile or auditory feedback, smartphone accessibility features).
- Encourage consistent task routines and item placement to reduce reliance on vision for locating objects.

Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies
- Discuss options for non-visual or low-vision support as an OT pathway, including magnification devices, screen-accessibility features (talking menus, larger on-device text), and audio-enabled information where feasible and aligned with patient goals.
- Avoid prescribing devices; focus on feasibility, training needs, and alignment with functional goals.

Education and Support
- Provide education about the safety implications of central vision loss during ambulation and activities that require precise central vision.
- Discuss driving safety preferences, alternative transportation planning, and ways to maintain community access while vision is suboptimal.
- Facilitate communication with ophthalmology and GP to ensure consistent monitoring of macular oedema and cardiovascular risk management.

Clinical Follow-up / Communication
- Highlight the need for ongoing collaboration with the ophthalmology team and the GP for visual status and systemic risk management (BP, lipids, glucose, anticoagulation considerations).
- Ensure documentation includes patient goals and any agreed safety or functional priorities to guide OT input.

Driving / Transport
- Acknowledge reduced driving and support planning for safe transportation options and community access, with OT exploring whether driving cessation or restriction should be revisited based on functional needs and goals.

Low Vision Considerations
- If not already engaged, consider referral to low-vision services for further optical magnification strategies and contrast enhancement to support reading and near tasks, contingent on ophthalmology/low-vision service pathways.

Information Gaps / Next Steps for OT Consideration
- Clarify patient goals (maintain independence with driving vs. transition to alternative transport).
- Confirm living environment details (lighting quality, stair configurations, clutter) to tailor environmental modifications.
- Determine willingness for O&M involvement and any preferences for assistive technologies or reading aids.
- Identify whether formal support, caregiver involvement, or community services are currently available or needed.

5. Falls Risk Level
- Moderate. Based on central vision loss with a history of a kerb-related stumble and potential mobility safety concerns in outdoor settings, balanced against the absence of multiple recent falls.

6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings
- Central vision loss OS with macular edema significantly affects reading, near tasks, and edge/contrast detection, with implications for daily activities and independent mobility.
- Mobility safety requires assessment or planning for outdoor ambulation, especially around kerbs and stairs; consider O&M referral for tailored mobility guidance.
- Reading-dependent ADLs and shopping tasks may benefit from non-visual strategies, larger/high-contrast formats, and device accessibility features.
- Environmental and lighting adaptations (contrast, glare control, edge marking) can support safer navigation and task performance.
- Consider liaison with low-vision services for magnification/contrast supports; ensure ongoing ophthalmology collaboration and patient-goal alignment.
- OT planning should prioritise safety in activities essential to independence and community participation, while aligning with the patient’s preferences regarding driving and mobility.

End of Case 046 summary.
