==============================
Case 067
==============================

1. Patient Context

- Age: 79, male
- Care setting: community optometry; referred by GP after fall
- Falls/referral context: single fall on a poorly lit outdoor step; falls risk elevated by night/dusk outdoor navigation
- Vision impairment: dense bilateral cataracts with blur, severe glare and markedly reduced contrast sensitivity; functional edge detection for steps is poor, especially in low illumination
- Vision history: progressive bilateral blur/glare over 12 months with rapid worsening in last 3 months; currently uses old bifocal spectacles full-time
- Functional status: stopped driving 1 month ago due to vision/glare; relies on wife for transport and reading small print
- Comorbidities: hypertension, COPD (inhalers, antihypertensives); no diplopia or flashes; no prior ocular surgery

2. Key Vision Issues

- Dense nuclear sclerosis with posterior subcapsular cataracts causing severe distance acuity loss and glare (best-corrected OD/OS/OU ~6/60 at 6 m)
  - Functional relevance: widespread difficulty with distance discrimination and edge detection in outdoor environments, especially under glare and low light
- Markedly reduced contrast sensitivity (not testable clinically due to acuity limits)
  - Functional relevance: difficulty distinguishing step edges, uneven terrain, and contrasts in evening/mixed lighting; increases tripping risk
- Poor step-edge visibility in low illumination (driving stopped; safety concern on outdoor stairs/entries)
  - Functional relevance: risk of misjudging steps, tripping or falling during dusk/dawn or poorly lit environments
- Near vision reduced (habitual N48 @40 cm; best with add N32 @40 cm)
  - Functional relevance: may affect reading small print, medication labels, or device screens, particularly if held at typical near distances
- Visual field: full to confrontation OU (despite media opacity)
  - Functional relevance: no gross field loss documented, but acuity/contrast limitations reduce functional field awareness in dynamic tasks
- Eyewear status: ongoing use of bifocals; optometry plan notes updated spectacles unlikely to restore function
  - Functional relevance: bifocal wear during walking/stairs may distort depth/edge cues; may complicate safe ambulation in low light

3. Functional Impact

Mobility and Falls
- Outdoor navigation at dusk/low light is high-risk due to poor edge contrast and glare; recent fall on a poorly lit patio edge highlights this risk
- Stair and step negotiation outdoors may be particularly hazardous; potential for near-miss or subsequent fall when lighting is inadequate
- Stopped driving; increased reliance on spouse for transportation limits independent mobility and may affect participation in activities

ADLs/IADLs
- Reading small print and local signage remains challenging; spouse currently assists with reading print/mail/labels
- Near-vision tasks (e.g., medication labeling, grocery/shopping cues) may require closer proximity or better contrast, which is impaired by cataract-related blur and glare

Participation / Confidence / Roles
- Hesitation to go outside at dusk; reduced community mobility and social participation potential
- Increased caregiver involvement for transportation and accompaniment; possible impact on independence and routine activities

Environmental Interaction
- Lighting and glare are central contributors to functional difficulty; entry path lighting and step-edge visibility are key modifiable factors
- Contrast between steps and surrounding surfaces is likely inadequate in low light

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations

A. ASSESS BEFORE PRESCRIBING
- Assess current home and entry lighting, outdoor path lighting, and step contrast in typical lighting conditions (daytime, twilight, dusk)
- Assess stair and step visibility on common routes (entry, patio, porch) and identify high-risk points
- Assess feasibility of environmental modifications in the home and immediate surroundings (lighting, edging, clutter, consistent pathway)

B. ENVIRONMENTAL MODIFICATION
- Lighting improvements at path/entry to reduce shadowing and improve edge detection (functional purpose: safer approach to steps and doorway)
- High-contrast edge edging or tactile/visual cues for outdoor steps to improve step-edge visibility
- Clear pathways with reduced clutter near entry and along routine routes
- Consider consistent outdoor lighting placement to minimize glare into eyes (functional rationale: stabilize adaptation between lighting zones)

C. LIGHTING AND CONTRAST
- Evaluate and optimize lighting to provide even illumination with minimal glare and shadows on step edges
- Enhance contrast at key environmental landmarks (doors, steps, thresholds) to aid detection under low-light conditions

D. MOBILITY AND ORIENTATION & MOBILITY (O&M) REFERENCE
- Given vision significantly affecting safe mobility, consider referral to Orientation & Mobility (O&M) for a specialized mobility assessment focused on low-vision outdoor navigation, route planning, and safe crossing/edge detection in variable lighting
- DO NOT prescribe step-by-step mobility routines; support functional goals and appropriate professional referral

E. TASK ADAPTATION / COMPENSATION
- Explore non-visual cues and task strategies for high-risk activities (e.g., using consistent route, companion-assisted night outings, marking steps with non-glare tactile cues)
- Consider strategies to minimize reliance on bifocals during outdoor ambulation (in collaboration with eye care), recognizing OT should not prescribe eyewear changes but may support routines that improve safety

F. ASSISTIVE TECHNOLOGY / NON-VISUAL COMPENSATION
- If vision remains unreliable for critical tasks, evaluate non-visual supports (e.g., tactile cues, audible/contrast-enhanced signage, larger-print materials for home use)
- Discuss with eye-care team about feasibility and integration of any low-vision devices if/when appropriate

G. EDUCATION AND SUPPORT
- Educate patient and spouse about safety contingencies and consistent lighting strategies; emphasize wearing appropriate footwear with good tread for outdoor steps
- Provide caregiver education on maintaining safe outdoor routes and reinforcing agreed safety practices

H. ENVIRONMENTAL ASSESSMENT AND MODIFICATION (ONGOING)
- Recommend environmental assessment of home entry, exterior steps, and outdoor path lighting by OT in collaboration with the patient and caregiver
- Consider feasibility of gradual changes (where feasible) to enhance safety without requiring major home renovations

I. DRIVING / TRANSPORT / COMMUNITY ACCESS
- Functional goal: preserve safe mobility and community access through supported transport options while driving is restricted
- Plan for alternative transportation arrangements with spouse or community services; discuss potential engagement with O&M for future mobility planning if vision improves or stabilizes

J. LOW VISION / SERVICE LINKAGE
- Acknowledge low-vision service involvement as appropriate; OT role focuses on interim functional assessment, task setup, safety, and environmental adaptation while awaiting ophthalmology/low-vision input

K. OTHER CONTEXTUAL FACTORS
- Consider COPD and need for energy conservation during outdoor activity in dusty or adverse weather; fatigue could affect mobility safety

5. Falls Risk Level

- Falls Risk Level: High
- Rationale: severe acuity loss from dense cataracts with glare and reduced contrast; single fall on a poorly lit outdoor step; reluctance to ambulate at dusk increases exposure to low-light risk environments

6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings

- Priority 1: Elevate safety for outdoor mobility in low light through environmental lighting and high-contrast step-edge cues; consider O&M referral for low-vision mobility optimization
- Priority 2: Address feeding into independence (driving cessation) with planning for alternative transport and caregiver-supported activities; support participation in essential outdoor tasks with safer routes and lighting
- Priority 3: Minimize fall risk through home/entry environmental assessment and modifications; ensure consistent pathway lighting and reduce glare/shadowing
- Priority 4: Support near-vision limitations in tasks like reading small print or labels by non-visual strategies and lighting, while coordinating with eye care for long-term plans

Note: The OT summary is intended to translate optometry findings into actionable occupational therapy considerations and does not replace optometric management. It highlights assessment needs, safety considerations, and possible environmental or compensatory strategies while respecting role boundaries and ongoing eye-care plans.
