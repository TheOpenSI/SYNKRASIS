==============================
Case 057
==============================

1. Patient Context
- Age: 84-year-old male, community-dwelling
- Falls/referral: referred after recurrent falls following stroke rehabilitation; two falls in the past 4 months (one when turning toward a doorway; another when moving from bright sunlight to a shaded entry)
- Vision diagnosis: right homonymous hemianopia (hemispheric field loss) with visually significant bilateral cataracts; glare and blur reported
- Vision-related needs/context: uses bifocals full-time; hearing impairment; not driving
- Functional concern context: family reports missing walls/objects on the right side; lives with wife; receiving mobility/home-safety rehabilitation follow-up
- Ocular history: stroke ~2 years ago; no diplopia or acute visual change; fundoscopy with otherwise stable findings; no RAPD
- Medical context: hypertension, arthritis; on antihypertensive and antiplatelet therapy

2. Key Vision Issues
- Right homonymous hemianopia + bilateral cataracts with reduced acuity and contrast sensitivity
  - Functional implication: diminished right-field awareness and overall scene clarity, increasing risk when turning toward the right and navigating in cluttered or transitional spaces; glare/contrast reduction worsens detection of hazards and edge/step cues.
- Glare sensitivity and lighting transition challenges
  - Functional implication: daytime bright-to-shaded transitions (e.g., entryways) may cause disorientation and slower safe navigation, contributing to falls.
- Visual processing limitations
  - Findings: reduced distance acuity (OD 6/60 at habitual, best-corrected OD 6/36), contrast sensitivity 1.05 log units; stereo not testable; color naming borderline/inconclusive
  - Functional implication: difficulty distinguishing signs, edges, or contrasts in many environments; depth perception and reliable sign interpretation may be limited, affecting safe mobility and task performance in dynamic environments.
- Visual field loss is largely non-reversible with current plan
  - Functional implication: compensatory strategies (scanning, environmental contrast) remain essential; surgical correction for cataracts may improve clarity but not field loss.

3. Functional Impact
Mobility and Falls
- Right-sided field loss and reduced contrast increase risk when turning and negotiating doorways or thresholds, particularly in transitions from bright to shaded areas.
- Glare and clutter may reduce safe navigation in unfamiliar or visually complex environments (e.g., entryways, stair edges).

ADLs/IADLs
- Reading/recognizing labels, signs, and instructions may be more challenging due to acuity and contrast deficits.
- Locating or organizing objects (especially on the right side) could be inconsistent, increasing time and cognitive load for routine tasks.

Participation / Confidence / Roles
- May experience reduced confidence in community mobility and outdoor activities; increased reliance on a caregiver or rehabilitation team for safety in navigating unfamiliar spaces.

Environmental Interaction
- Lighting quality, glare, and step/threshold visibility are critical; uneven lighting and high glare can impair performance unless addressed with environmental adjustments.

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations
A. ASSESS BEFORE PRESCRIBING
- Conduct an OT-focused functional observation of mobility and navigation in typical home/entry environments, with emphasis on rightward scanning, turning through doorways, and transitions from bright to shaded spaces.
- Assess ability to locate objects and read common signs or labels in a controlled task (e.g., kitchen or hallway tasks) to identify task-specific risks.
- Evaluate daily-use tasks that might rely on right-space awareness (e.g., reaching for items on right, doorway clearance) and determine if a cane or other non-visual cue supports safety.
- Review current lighting at key environments (entry, living areas) and document glare/contrast issues impacting task performance.
- Confirm alignment with rehabilitation team goals and communication channels (OT, GP, rehab physician).

B. ENVIRONMENTAL ASSESSMENT AND MODIFICATION
- Lighting: optimize entryway and interior lighting to minimize glare and ensure even illumination; use high-contrast, non-reflective surfaces where possible.
- Pathways: reduce clutter on travel routes; ensure clear, straight paths with consistent furniture placement; mark edges or steps with high-contrast cues.
- Thresholds/Steps: implement high-contrast step edges and well-lit transitions to aid edge detection.
- Item placement: consider placing frequently used items within the left or more central field to reduce reliance on the right visual field if feasible; ensure signage/objects use high-contrast backgrounds.

C. LIGHTING AND CONTRAST
- Balance ambient and task lighting to reduce shadows and glare; minimize rapid lighting level changes (e.g., from bright outdoors to indoor).
- Ensure step edges and doorway thresholds have clear contrast to support orientation and safe ambulation.

D. MOBILITY AND ORIENTATION & MOBILITY (O&M)
- Given the right hemianopia with a falls history, an O&M assessment/referral is appropriate to tailor scanning strategies, route planning, and safe community mobility. Do not prescribe fixed step-by-step routines; goals should focus on safe, independent mobility within the person’s environment with appropriate professional guidance.

E. VISUAL + NON-VISUAL COMPENSATION / ASSISTIVE TECHNOLOGY
- Maintain engagement with low-vision services and cane training ( Vision Australia/Guide Dogs Australia as indicated in plan ); consider non-visual strategies (tactile cues, consistent object placement, audible cues) to support independent activity.
- Do not prescribe devices without coordinating with the eye-care team; OT focus is on how to use available supports safely and effectively.

F. TASK ADAPTATION
- Assess and, if needed, adapt home routines to accommodate right-field loss (e.g., reducing clutter on travel paths, arranging items to support rightward scanning).
- Assess medication management and labeling needs in light of acuity/contrast reductions; involve caregiver strategies for signage/label clarity if required.

G. SUPPORT, CARERS AND FORMAL SERVICES
- Engage spouse as a safety partner; educate on consistent environment layouts, safe navigation practices, and when to seek assistance.
- Consider formal follow-up with OT and/or community mobility services to monitor progress and adjust supports as vision and functional needs evolve.

H. PARTICIPATION AND COMMUNITY ACCESS
- Explore options to preserve community access and independence (e.g., transport plans if driving is reduced/ceased; supervised outings with cane; use of mobility aids in unfamiliar environments).

I. DRIVING / LICENSING / TRANSPORT
- Not driving at present; standard OT education around transportation options and safety planning should be considered.

J. EYEWEAR FUNCTION
- Acknowledge bifocals may influence scanning and depth perception during transitions and tasks; focus on practical routines to mitigate risks rather than changing eyewear without optometry input.

K. LOW VISION / SERVICE RESPONSE
- Acknowledge planned cataract management and ongoing low-vision supports; coordinate with eye-care providers for interim functional goals, task adaptations, and safety training.

L. BEHAVIORAL ADAPTATION
- Encourage consistent routines and environmental cues to support safe performance, especially during periods of glare or poorer right-field detection.

M. FLUCTUATING VISION
- Not explicitly described; monitor for potential fluctuations (e.g., glare-related fluctuations) and adapt task timing if relevant.

N. FEASIBILITY
- All recommendations should consider the home environment, caregiver capacity, and access to services; avoid assuming ability to implement extensive modifications without assessment.

O. PRIORITISATION
- Safety-critical priorities center on right-field awareness, turning through doorways, glare/contrast management, and consistent environmental layout to reduce falls risk.

5. Falls Risk Level
- High
- Rationale: recurrent falls within 4 months in the context of a permanent right-field deficit due to stroke, compounded by glare/contrast impairment and cataracts; while cataracts may improve with surgery, the hemianopic field loss is non-reversible, elevating ongoing risk.

6. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings
- The combination of right homonymous hemianopia and cataracts elevates risk for safer mobility, especially when turning toward doorways and transitioning between bright outside and shaded entryways; address through environmental modifications, mobility assessment, and targeted scanning/compensation strategies (including O&M referral and cane training).
- Lighting, glare, and contrast are critical environmental targets to optimize safety during navigation and tasks that involve edges/steps.
- A collaborative plan with low-vision services and the rehab team is appropriate to support interim function, safety, and participation while cataract management and field-loss management proceed.
- Task-adaptation and caregiver education should focus on right-field awareness, consistent environmental layout, and safe engagement in daily activities and community mobility.

Final quick notes
- Case-specific OT emphasis remains on translating the documented right-field loss and glare/contrast problems into practical safety and independence strategies, with assessment and collaboration to determine precise OT-initiated interventions.
