==============================
Case 033
==============================

1. Patient Context

- 63-year-old woman living/presenting in a community optometry setting
- Falls pathway: routine eye examination; mobility-related blur identified when not wearing glasses; no confirmed fall, but reports hesitancy on uneven paving when walking without glasses
- Ocular history: gradual distance blur over 18 months with habitual removal of spectacles for near tasks; currently uses single-vision distance correction only; no dedicated reading correction
- Functional notes: drives regularly and avoids driving without glasses; license requires wearing glasses
- Vision function: distance blur is uncorrected when mobile but normalizes with optimal correction; near vision improves markedly with near add; binocular near exophoria and reduced stereo acuity noted
- Management plan: updated distance and near prescriptions provided; consideration of first-time multifocals with adaptation guidance and fall-risk awareness; advised wearing distance correction consistently when walking indoors and outdoors; driving requires distance correction; follow-up in 6 weeks

2. Key Vision Issues

- Uncorrected distance blur when mobile (habitual removal of glasses) leading to reduced distance clarity outdoors; functional relevance: difficulty detecting edge contours, pedestrians, and uneven surfaces, especially in dull weather
- Distance correction markedly improves acuity (to 6/6 OU at 6 m with best correction); functional relevance: wearing the correct distance spectacles is likely to restore safe outdoor mobility and traffic reading, reducing hesitancy
- Near vision without reading correction is blurred (habitual N10 at 40 cm unaided; with add and best correction: N4 at 40 cm); functional relevance: near tasks (reading labels, forms) may be compromised without proper reading correction; habit of removing glasses for near tasks worsens near clarity
- Near binocular alignment and depth perception: 4 prism diopters exophoria at near with stereoacuity of 60 arcseconds; functional relevance: potential difficulty with precise depth judgments and spatial localization during walking and obstacle negotiation
- Early nuclear sclerosis and mild meibomian gland changes; functional relevance: could contribute to mild blur or comfort issues, particularly in certain lighting, but contrast sensitivity is currently normal
- Visual field intact; color vision normal; overall ocular health stable

3. Functional Impact

Mobility and Falls
- Outdoor mobility may be compromised when uncorrected distance vision is relied upon, particularly on uneven paving or in low-light conditions; this may manifest as hesitancy, slower gait, or avoidance of uneven surfaces
- Near exophoria and reduced stereo acuity could contribute to less reliable depth perception during dynamic tasks (e.g., stepping off curbs, judging distances to obstacles)

ADLs/IADLs
- Reading and interpreting near tasks (forms, labels, instructions) may be challenging without appropriate near correction; current habit of removing glasses for near work increases risk of missed details
- Potential reliance on non-visual strategies (memory, routine) to compensate for vision-light and task demands when near correction is not worn

Participation / Confidence / Roles
- Hesitancy outdoors without glasses may limit walking routes, outdoor activities, or social participation during low-light/dull-weather conditions
- Driving safety is supported by wearing distance correction; ongoing adaptation to new multifocals may influence confidence in transit and independent travel

Environmental Interaction
- Low-light and dull-weather scenarios exacerbate depth perception and contrast cues; lighting quality and glare/shadow management will influence functional safety during ambulation

4. Vision-Informed OT Considerations

A. Self-care
- Assess near-task demands that rely on reading (e.g., forms, medication labeling) and determine if near correction is needed or if routines should enforce consistent eyewear use

B. Environmental Modifications / Home Environment
- Recommend strategies to support consistent spectacle use (e.g., place glasses by entry/door, use a dedicated reading pair accessible at typical work/study areas)
- Consider lighting optimization in commonly used mobility routes (indoor and outdoor) to reduce glare and improve edge/step visibility; ensure high-contrast environments where possible

C. Lighting and Contrast
- Evaluate and address lighting in key areas (entryway, hallway, outdoor paths) to minimize shadows and glare that worsen depth perception during walking

D. Mobility Strategies
- Given vision materially affects mobility when uncorrected, consider referral to Orientation & Mobility (O&M) for a professional mobility assessment if hesitancy or near-miss events persist after correction
  - O&M goals would center on safe community mobility, route awareness, and decision-making under varying lighting
  - Do not prescribe specific mobility routines here; refer for individualised assessment

E. Task Adaptation
- Promote consistent use of distance correction for moving around indoors/outdoors
- For near tasks, implement near-vision strategies: use or accept near add when reading or identifying small details; if not currently using a reading correction, assess feasibility and trial a near correction in collaboration with the eye care provider

F. Assistive Technology / Non-visual Strategies
- If near tasks remain challenging, consider non-visual supports (large-print labels, higher-contrast materials) and ensure accessibility of essential information
- Encourage non-visual cues and routines to support spectacle use (habits, placement cues)

G. Education and Support
- Educate about the importance of wearing distance correction during ambulation and driving-related activities
- Discuss adaptation period for multifocals if prescribed, and establish a plan for monitoring comfort and safety during the adaptation phase
- Provide guidance on consistent spectacle storage and retrieval to minimize forgetting to wear glasses

H. Clinical Follow-up / Communication
- Coordinate with the optometrist to review adaptation to updated prescriptions and assess mobility confidence at the 6-week follow-up
- Communicate any persistent mobility concerns or near-vision tasks that continue to rely on unaided vision to ensure appropriate multidisciplinary input (e.g., O&M, low-vision services if indicated)

I. Driving / Transport
- Driving safety relies on wearing distance correction; reinforce importance of corrective wear for driving tasks and ongoing monitoring of adaptation to new prescriptions
- If driving difficulties persist after correction or there are new safety concerns, discuss with the optometry team and, if appropriate, the patient’s driving support network

J. Other Health and Contextual Factors
- Hypertension is controlled; no diabetes reported; no cognitive or other non-vision issues documented that would alter OT reasoning beyond what vision findings indicate

Falls Risk Level
- Moderate: Based on uncorrected distance blur during ambulation, near-vision demands when not wearing reading correction, and depth-perception considerations (exophoria at near and reduced stereo acuity) in contexts of uneven surfaces and low lighting; vision is correctable with glasses, so risk is contingent on consistent spectacle use

5. Summary of Key OT Considerations from Vision Findings

- The primary functional risk is outdoor mobility safety when uncorrected distance vision is relied upon; ensuring consistent wear of distance correction is critical to reduce hesitancy and misjudgment on uneven surfaces
- Near-vision tasks require appropriate reading correction; habitual removal of glasses for near work increases risk of missed details and misreading
- Depth perception concerns (near exophoria and reduced stereo acuity) may affect spacing and obstacle negotiation; consider O&M assessment if mobility confidence does not improve with corrected distance vision
- Environmental and routine strategies to support consistent eyewear use and optimized lighting/contrast can mitigate functional risks without relying solely on eyewear prescription changes
- Driving safety hinges on wearing distance correction; ensure adherence to correction for all driving scenarios and monitor adaptation to any new multifocal use

Final notes
- The OT summary presents a case-specific, function-focused interpretation that should not supersede optometry management. All vision-related decisions about correction, prisms, occlusion, or optical treatment remain with eye care professionals.
