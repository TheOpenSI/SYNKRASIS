- The graph consists of:
  - A fixed forward chain of edges 1 -> 2 -> ... -> 2N.
  - N extra edges, each from a white vertex to a black vertex. Let white vertex be at position w and the matched black vertex be at position b. The edge is w -> b.

- Key observation about strong connectivity:
  - To move from a vertex with a larger index to a vertex with a smaller index, you must take a backward crossing edge (one that goes from a white to a black with the white index larger than the black index).
  - Consider the cut between k and k+1 (for k = 1..2N-1). The only way to travel from the right side to the left side across that cut is via a backward cross edge that crosses this cut, i.e., an edge w -> b with w > k and b <= k.
  - Therefore, the final graph is strongly connected if and only if for every k in [1, 2N-1], there exists at least one backward cross edge crossing the cut k. Equivalently, for every k, there exists a pair (w, b) with w > k and b <= k.

- Translate to the matching problem:
  - The backward cross edges correspond exactly to pairings where the white vertex index i is greater than the black vertex index j, i.e., i > j.
  - Each such backward edge contributes the interval [j, i] and it covers all cuts k with j <= k < i.
  - The coverage condition "every cut k is covered by at least one backward edge" is equivalent to: for every k in [1, 2N-1], there exists a backward pair (i, j) with j <= k < i.
  - Equivalently, define for each k the number A(k) of backward pairings whose left endpoint (black index) is <= k. The condition is A(k) >= 1 for all k.

- Counting with inclusion-exclusion:
  - Let E_k be the event that cut k is not covered by any backward edge (i.e., all backward edges lie completely to the left of k or completely to the right of k). We want the number of perfect matchings that avoid all E_k for k = 1..2N-1.
  - By inclusion-exclusion, the answer is:
    sum over subsets T of {1,2,...,2N-1} of (-1)^{|T|} * M(T),
    where M(T) is the number of perfect matchings that satisfy E_k for all k in T (i.e., no backward edge crosses any cut in T).

- How to compute M(T)?
  - For a single cut k, E_k means all blacks in [1..k] must be matched to whites in [1..k] (no black in the left part can be matched to a white in the right part). This condition yields a closed-form for the count:
    If Lw = number of whites in [1..k] and Lb = number of blacks in [1..k],
    M({k}) = C(Lw, Lb) * Lb! * (N - Lb)! when Lb <= Lw, otherwise M({k}) = 0.
  - For multiple cuts T, the constraints interact in a nontrivial way. A naïve inclusion-exclusion over all 2N-1 cuts would be exponential and infeasible for N up to 2e5.
