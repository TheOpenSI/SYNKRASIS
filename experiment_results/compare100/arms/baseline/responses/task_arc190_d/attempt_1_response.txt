We need the resulting N×N matrix modulo p.
- Observations:

  - Let Z be the set of zero positions in A. Each B corresponds to choosing a nonzero value at each position in Z; denote these choices collectively by a K-dimensional vector U with entries in F_p^*.
  - Then B = A + U in the sense of filling those zero positions with the chosen nonzero values. We want S = Σ_{U ∈ (F_p^*)^K} (A+U)^p (mod p).
  - A direct expansion of (A+U)^p is nontrivial because matrices do not in general commute, so the naive binomial expansion does not apply. The combinatorics of all mixed terms (words in A and U of total length p) make a straightforward approach intractable for large K.
  - A plausible general approach for such problems is to use inclusion-exclusion over the zeros. If we allow U_j to run over all p values (including 0) and then subtract cases where at least one zero was actually 0, we can write the desired sum as an alternating sum over affine subspaces of the form F_T = Σ_{U ∈ F_p^{K-|T|} with U_j free for j∉T and U_j = 0 for j∈T} (A+U)^p, for T ⊆ Z. Then S = Σ_{T⊆Z} (-1)^{|T|} F_T.
