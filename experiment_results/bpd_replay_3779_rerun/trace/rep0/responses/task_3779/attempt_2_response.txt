### Step-by-step reasoning
- Problem restatement: We have to partition n pizzas (n is multiple of 4) into D = n/4 groups of 4. For each group with weights W <= X <= Y <= Z, on odd days we gain Z, and on even days we gain Y. Days are 1..D, so odd/even alternates. The groups can be arranged in any order; we just need the maximum total gain after all pizzas are eaten.

- Key insight: The two numbers in each group that contribute to the total are the largest (Z) and the second largest (Y). The other two numbers don’t contribute directly, but they must be placed in the group so that W and X are not larger than Y.

- Greedy strategy that achieves optimality:
  - Sort weights in descending order.
  - Maintain two pointers on the sorted array:
    - left points to the current largest unused element.
    - right points to the current smallest unused element.
  - For each day i from 0 to D-1 (0-based; day 1 is i=0, an odd day):
    - Take Z as the current largest unused: Z = a[left], then advance left by 1 (consume Z).
    - If day i is even (which corresponds to odd-numbered day 1,3,5,...):
      - We form the group by pairing Z with the three smallest remaining numbers. The group’s Y is the largest among those three smallest, but since we only need to update the pool, we simply remove the three smallest from the pool and add Z to the answer.
      - Implementation: decrease right by 3 (consume three smallest).
    - If day i is odd (even-numbered day 2,4,...):
      - We form the group by pairing Z with the three largest remaining numbers to maximize Y for this day. The Y value will be the largest among those three, which is the next largest after Z, i.e., a[left] after consuming Z. We add that Y to the answer and then remove those three largest from the pool (advance left by 3).
- This approach uses O(n log n) time due to sorting and O(1) extra space, aside from the input array.

- Why previous approach fails: The earlier solution mixed removal from ends in a way that could create invalid groupings where Y could exceed the second largest of its group, or it could waste large numbers on odd days. The corrected two-pointer strategy explicitly ensures:
  - On odd days, Z is paired with three smallest numbers (to avoid wasting potential Y values).
  - On even days, Z is paired with the three largest remaining numbers (to maximize Y for that day).

- Correctness check with examples:
  - Example [8,7,6,5,4,3,2,1]:
    - Day 1: Z=8, pair with three smallest (1,2,3). Day1 gain 8.
    - Day 2: Z=7, pair with three largest remaining (6,5,4). Day2 gain 6.
    - Total 14, matches optimal.
  - Example [2,1,1,1,1,1,1,1]:
    - Day 1: Z=2, pair with three smallest (1,1,1). Day1 gain 2.
    - Day 2: Z=1, pair with three largest remaining (1,1,1). Day2 gain 1.
    - Total 3, matches.

### Code
```
from typing import List

class Solution:
    def maxWeight(self, pizzas: List[int]) -> int:
        n = len(pizzas)
        a = sorted(pizzas, reverse=True)

        left = 0          # index for the next largest unused
        right = n - 1     # index for the smallest unused
        days = n // 4
        ans = 0

        for i in range(days):
            # Take the largest remaining as Z for this group
            Z = a[left]
            left += 1

            if i % 2 == 0:
                # Odd day: pair with three smallest -> remove from right
                ans += Z
                right -= 3
            else:
                # Even day: pair with three largest remaining -> Y is the largest among them
                Y = a[left]
                ans += Y
                left += 3

        return ans
```
