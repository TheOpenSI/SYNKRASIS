### Step-by-step reasoning
- Problem understanding:
  - We have an array pizzas of length n (n is a multiple of 4). We partition pizzas into D = n/4 groups of 4. For each group with weights W <= X <= Y <= Z, on day 1,3,5,... (odd days) we gain Z, and on day 2,4,6,... (even days) we gain Y. We want to maximize the total gain after all pizzas are eaten.
  - The grouping order is flexible; only the daily parity matters for what we add from each group.

- Key observations:
  - In any day, the contribution comes from the two largest numbers of the chosen quadruple: Z (the largest) and Y (the second largest). The two smallest numbers in the quadruple (W and X) do not contribute directly but must be chosen to complete the group.
  - To maximize the total sum, we should allocate the largest numbers strategically across days:
    - On odd days (day 1, 3, 5, ...), we gain Z. It is optimal to use the current largest remaining weight as Z because Z is always the maximum of its group and contributes fully on that day.
    - On even days, we gain Y. We want Y to be as large as possible, but Y must be the second largest in its group. To maximize Y on an even day, we should form the group by taking the three largest remaining numbers after removing Z, so that the largest among those (the next largest after Z) becomes Y.
  - A practical way to implement this is:
    - Sort pizzas in descending order.
    - Maintain two pointers: left points to the largest remaining element, right points to the smallest remaining element.
    - For each day i from 0 to D-1:
      - Take Z = a[left], advance left by 1 (consume Z).
      - If i is even (corresponding to odd-numbered day), add Z to the answer and remove the three smallest remaining elements by moving right leftwards by 3.
      - If i is odd (even-numbered day), take Y = a[left] (the largest among the remaining after removing Z), add Y to the answer, and remove the next three largest elements by moving left forwards by 3 (consuming Z, Y, and the three largest remaining after Z).
  - This approach ensures the largest values are used as Z on odd days and the next largest values are used as Y on even days, maximizing the total.

- Correctness considerations:
  - Each day uses Z as the current largest remaining, guaranteeing the maximum possible Z for that day.
  - On odd days, pairing Z with three smallest ensures that we do not waste potential large values that could serve as Y on later even days.
  - On even days, after consuming Z, the three largest remaining will yield the maximum possible Y for that day.
  - This greedy strategy respects the constraints (groups of 4, correct ordering within a group) and achieves the maximum total sum.

- Complexity:
  - Sorting: O(n log n)
  - Linear pass with two pointers: O(n)
  - Space: O(1) beyond input array (since we operate with indices)

- Edge cases:
  - n is a multiple of 4 by problem guarantee; smallest n=4 yields just the maximum pizza weight as the only Z on day 1.
  - Duplicates and ties are handled naturally by sorting and the pointer scheme.

### Code
```
from typing import List

class Solution:
    def maxWeight(self, pizzas: List[int]) -> int:
        n = len(pizzas)
        a = sorted(pizzas, reverse=True)

        left = 0          # index for the current largest unused element
        right = n - 1     # index for the current smallest unused element
        days = n // 4
        ans = 0

        for i in range(days):
            # Take the largest remaining as Z for this group
            Z = a[left]
            left += 1

            if i % 2 == 0:
                # Odd-numbered day (1,3,...) -> gain Z; pair with three smallest
                ans += Z
                right -= 3
            else:
                # Even-numbered day (2,4,...) -> gain Y; pair with three largest remaining
                Y = a[left]
                ans += Y
                left += 3  # consume three largest after Z (including Y which was at left)
        
        return ans
```
